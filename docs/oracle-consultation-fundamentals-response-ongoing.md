**"The mid-run step collapse" no longer exists as an object.** The min-h column is the start-up step divided by the horizon in every row; the accepted-step distribution is healthy (1st percentile ~10⁻³·T). That is exactly what a C¹-but-violently-curved field produces — the coupling's `u`-dependence switching its 50–291× amplification on and off as reservoirs approach bounds and corner loci sweep. The dead-band controller striding hot and paying ~30% probes is the efficient strategy on this field, and I treated it as pathology for three rounds.

With those retired, the forward verdict is clean: **cost = (accuracy-set evaluation rate) × O(M), and both factors are at their floor.** Every decomposition lost because it re-paid the second factor more often than the first requires; every stiffness-exploiting method lost because there is no stability margin to sell. ## 3. Item A — the gradient seam: one first-order bug, one locator, three side benefits

The known open correctness item is now fully specified by the corner geometry. The operating point is the fold of a nested root: on the wall side the productive branch's inner root ceases to exist. Its defining system is therefore the bordered fold system — `{R(v, p; state) = 0, ∂R/∂v = 0}`, two equations, two unknowns, regular Jacobian at a quadratic fold — solved by safeguarded Newton initialised from the existing bracket, with derivatives available from the same forward-AD machinery that supplies `∂P/∂p`. Present `p*` as a scalar implicit-function node. Effects, in order: (i) the adjoint gains the dropped first-order term `(∂c/∂p, ∂g/∂p)·(∂p*/∂state)` exactly, and the finite-difference reference that re-solves the inner problem will finally agree — the correctness contract becomes satisfiable; (ii) `τ` is deleted as a knob, and the acceptance test is already tabulated: the J(τ) column must flatten at 5.87×10⁻⁸ for every setting; (iii) the midpoint bias and the clamped-argument one-sided-difference hazard vanish because nothing is differenced. Cost is a handful of residual evaluations against the search's — near neutral. It is a documented forward change that removes an error, which is the best kind. The <0.5% non-full-search exits keep their existing branch treatment, recorded and replayed as schedule data.

## 4. Item B — J's geometry is intrinsic; the response is model-side, and it is two coordinated moves

A moment of a measure with an absorbing weight boundary is piecewise-C¹ in θ, full stop: the adjoint computes a branch slope, a finite difference straddling an extinction flip also sees the jump, and no integrator, tape, or tolerance reconciles them. Two moves, together: **(i) mollify the survival entry into J** — a smooth window in the survival margin near removal, its width licensed by the fact you already carry a 23% inter-scheme spread (a few percent of mollification bias is free against it), making dJ/dθ continuous — steep but finite — which is what any outer optimiser actually needs; **(ii) point the member insertion at the moment, not the trajectory** — a refinement indicator on `ρ·|c|` and `φ·ρ` mass near thresholds is computable for free from byproducts already in hand — and then run the M-refinement certification of J that has been flagged since round five and, per this record, still has not been run. The 23% lives on the member axis. It is the largest unquantified error term left in the program, and nothing on the time axis can touch it.

## 5. Item C — the multi-block failure: a stability *exception*, and why it dies instead of rejecting

The new datum — overflow at *growing* ~0.25-unit steps — reads as follows. After a quiet spell the dead-band controller strides (growth up to 5×). In the multi-block configuration the shared-`u` draw aggregates across blocks, multiplying the coupled amplification (50–291× per block), so the local stability ceiling that "0% of steps" touched on single-block runs — a fact measured only on sequences that *complete* — is crossed within one stride. An explicit stage then explodes through the `q ≈ 16` self-loss; and note that if any stage undershoots `u_min`, `pow(negative, 16.14)` is NaN. The error controller is an explicit method's only stability guard, **and it fails exactly when the overshoot is so large the error estimate itself goes non-finite** — NaN-unsafe comparisons (`rmax > 1.1` is false for NaN) fall through to the accept/grow path and the run is poisoned. Checks: inspect the accept/reject logic for non-finite `rmax`; rerun with reject-on-non-finite and watch whether the failure degrades into ordinary rejections and completes. Fixes, cheap to strong: a one-line NaN guard; a growth clip driven by the already-logged reservoir margins (cheap stability proxy near depleted/saturated states); and — the real one — the shelved exact recession map, whose true job emerges here. It was refuted as a *speed* lever; as a *robustness* lever it is exactly right: it removes `u^q` from the RK stages entirely, is positivity-preserving by construction, and makes the overshoot impossible rather than merely caught. Once the multi-block run completes, log `h·|λ|` on it — the "0% stability-limited" fact deserves re-verification at the higher loop gain.

## 6. Item D — cost at scale: attribute the M⁰·⁴, then parallelise

Whole-run cost ~M¹·⁴ decomposes into the irreducible O(M) per evaluation and an unexplained ~M⁰·⁴ in evaluation count. Leading candidate: extreme-value growth of the max-norm — more components means a larger expected maximum of the scaled error, hence smaller steps, with no accuracy benefit to J if the argmax components are marginal-ρ members or depleted reservoirs. The instrument is the norm-argmax log (proposed in round 8, still unrun per this record): one field per attempted step, attribution offline in minutes. If marginal components dominate, a J-relevance-weighted norm (absolute-tolerance floors below each component's J-sensitivity) is a documented change that flattens the exponent — verified in J-units on the patch library. If genuine structure dominates, accept the exponent and take the remaining factor in wall time: the member sweep is embarrassingly parallel (independent solves plus reductions), unexploited anywhere in this record, and at M = 800 it is the largest pure-engineering lever available with zero numerics risk.
