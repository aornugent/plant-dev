## The verdict first

Events are the right object for workflow 1 and an optional one for workflow 2. Workflow 1 needs one thing: the map from `θ` to the answers on a frozen grid must be smooth enough that a second difference is stable, and nothing but cuts at the kinks gives that. Workflow 2 needs cheap, consistent evaluations over a box four length scales wide; its probe already passes the nudge test unsplit, its ε is loose, and its cost is dominated by sweeps that the loop may mostly not need. So build events for the base run, keep the probe's design around the stored recording, and take second derivatives by forward second differences, which need no change to the sweep at all. Details, failure modes and tests follow; the smallest build is at the end.

## 1. What makes `J(θ)` smooth with events

**The structure is combinatorial; only `J` across its changes can be smooth.** The structure is the set of (member, step, number of cuts). It changes at passages, grazes and stops, and your measurements already show each handled: a passage moves `J` by 5e-9 on the quintic, a graze is continuous once cuts are placed at interior extrema of `P`, and a crossing at a stop is a passage whose boundary is the stop. The passage jump is not an error in the location; it is the difference between a member integrated on the stage fields (unsplit) and the same member re-integrated on the step's interpolant (a degenerate split), which is `O(h⁴)` because the internal stage values are not solution values. It cannot be removed without re-integrating every member on the interpolant, and at 5e-9 it need not be.

**Branch-select the sub-steps.** This is the one change I would make to the forward. Today a sub-step evaluates the smooth `P⁺`, so if the cut sits off the true crossing by `δτ`, one sub-step holds the kink and carries `h²Δ·K(δτ/h)`. Write the discrete map as `F(θ, τ)` with the cut at `τ`; the exact derivative of the located-event model is `F_θ + F_τ·t_c′`, and both terms carry `±hΔK′·t_c′`, which cancel. So with smooth-`P⁺` sub-steps the implicit-function term is not a refinement: dropping it leaves an `O(h)` error, the unsplit noise back again. If instead the first sub-step integrates with `P⁺ := P` and the second with `P⁺ := 0` (and `u⁻` accordingly), each sub-step's field is smooth, `f⁺ = f⁻` at the cut, and `F_τ = O(h^p)`. The cut time then enters `J` only at the pair's order, the sweep may freeze `t_c` at `O(h^p)` cost, and the IFT routing becomes a cheap exactness option rather than a correctness requirement. The declared model change is `P⁺` replaced by its branch over the `ε_P/|Ṗ| ≈ 2e-7` transition, of order `ε_P` times that interval, far below `tol`; measure it once and report it. A located root is still needed to `O(h)` or better in `ϑ`; with branches a mislocation by `δϑ` costs `Ṗ(δϑ·h)²`, which at your single location on the quartic is `O(h⁴)` per crossing and is why `J` already converges as `tol`.

**What the tape holds.** Each sub-step is an ordinary RK row for one member: six stage solutions, read fields, end state. The read fields are fixed linear combinations of recorded stage derivatives (the chain's, and every member's `x_j` through the rebuilt `Φ`), so the reverse pass pushes the split member's field adjoints at its sub-stage times back onto those stage derivatives with the quartic's weights. That is the only new fan-out, one split member touching all members' `x_j` adjoints through `Φ`'s 65 knots, about `N × 65` flops per sub-stage against a taped member evaluation of 2.4 forward evaluations; on 11–14% of steps with ~45 splits each it is a few percent of the sweep. The cut time is read from the tape, never re-located in the sweep. If you route it, the IFT at the located root is `∂t_c/∂(·) = −(∂g/∂(·))/(∂g/∂t)` with `g = P` on the interpolant, so the partials run through the same quartic weights; one scalar sink per cut. The corrected end state must also be the state at which that member's next first stage (FSAL) is evaluated, with its own inner solve; a stale first stage carries `O(h²ΔK)` into the next step, and the test is bit-equality of the next step's first-stage rates with `f` at the corrected state.

**No adjoint jump.** A kink leaves the state sensitivity continuous, so the continuous adjoint is continuous with a kink in its slope; in the sweep nothing happens at a cut except the sub-step rows and, if routed, the `t̄_c` sink. With branches the sub-steps' Jacobians use the branch's `dP⁺/dP` (1 or 0), not the smooth `½` at `P = 0`.

**Near a graze.** With branches the cut's influence is `F_τ·t_c′ = O(h^p)/Ṗ`: negligible until `|Ṗ| ≲ h^p`, so cap `1/Ṗ` at the located root and report the cap firing. Without branches it is `O(h)/Ṗ`, a spike over the `~1e-5` window in `θ` where a pair is born. The model itself (`ε_P → 0`) has `J ∝ (θ_g − θ)₊^{3/2}` at a graze, so its second derivative carries an integrable `(θ_g − θ)^{−1/2}` spike of that width; any difference with `u ≫ 1e-5` integrates over it correctly and a pointwise second-order adjoint evaluated inside it does not. Report members with `|P_min|` inside a step below a threshold, per run.

**Class switches.** They are `C⁰` kinks in `p_j`, hence in the draw and in `Ṡ` through `u⁻`, and on a frozen grid they leave the coherent second-derivative bias `−Σ(λ·Δ)(∂t/∂θ)²` that the crossings' cuts removed for `P = 0`. Your residual with crossings split, "within about 1ε", is marginal against a bar of ε and is the natural place for that bias to sit. Decide empirically: compute the forward second difference at `u = 3e-3`, `1e-2`, `3e-2`; noise falls as `1/u²`, a bias stays. If a bias of order ε remains, build class switches as a second switching function in the same machinery (condition: the first-order condition at `p_lo` changes sign along the member's interpolant; branch: `p = p_lo` on one side, the unconstrained root on the other, bracket allowed slightly past `p_lo`), behind a flag. They coincide in time with upward crossings, so the two-cut path already covers most of the structure, and since they appear in 23–34% of the members' own rejections they may pay for themselves in steps. Their kink in the chain's rate through the draw is not removed by a member's cut; it is weighted by the chain's small reach and can be left.

## 2. Second derivatives: route and noise floor

Three routes, with the floors your measurements fix:

- *Forward second differences of `J` on the frozen grid.* Noise: passages, 5e-9 each, about 650 over `±1e-2`, random, so `√650 × 5e-9 / u² ≈ 3e-3` absolute in `(ln J)″`, i.e. 2e-3 of ε for `θ_A`; truncation `(u/0.245)²/12 ≈ 1e-4` relative at `u = 1e-2`. Cost 2 forwards per constant and no sweep. The multirate step's adaptive inner chain adds 2.5e-7 per replay until its inner steps are frozen on the recording; freeze them (below) and that term vanishes.
- *Chords of gradients.* Noise: the gradient's passage jumps, 3e-5 of itself each, `√650 × 3e-5 × |g| / 2u ≈ 0.3ε` at `u = 1e-2`, which is your "within about 1ε". Cost 2 gradient runs per constant, about 7 forwards. It gives a Hessian row, which the diagonal does not need.
- *Forward-over-reverse.* No differencing noise, but it is the pointwise second derivative of the frozen-grid map, which carries every unlocated kink's coherent bias (class switches today) and the graze spikes; cost about 5 forwards per direction and a second-order sweep.

So the diagonal comes from forward second differences at `u ≈ 1e-2`, a hundredfold lower floor than the chord at a seventh of the cost, and it needs only a driver script. The chord or forward-over-reverse is for rows, and only after class switches are located. One identity reduces the mixed derivatives workflow 2 may want: with the base re-equilibrated so that `J′(θ; θ) ≡ 1`, along the diagonal `∂_θ′J′ + ∂_θJ′ = 0` and `∂²_θ′θ′J′ + 2∂²_θθ′J′ + ∂²_θθJ′ = 0`, so the environment's response gradient is the negative of the selection gradient for free, and one of the three second derivatives follows from the other two.

## 3. Events on multirate steps

On a multirate step the chain's path inside the step is its own inner integration under the corrected linear draw. Three consequences. First, the inner steps are controller decisions and must be recorded and frozen; replays and probes walk them. That removes the 2.5e-7 noise and makes the sweep's row structure fixed; the tape cost is five components times a few dozen inner steps per multirate step, negligible. Second, a split member reads `φ` from the inner integration's own quartic, at `1e-9`, which is better than the outer step's interpolant, and `Φ` from the other members' outer quartic as before; the location uses the member's own quartic. Nothing about the order changes, and the location is if anything better, which matters because these are the pulse-onset upward crossings on steps that are now longer. Third, the order of operations: outer stages on the predictor chain, splits read the predictor chain, corrected end states, end draw, corrector chain, then the FSAL end evaluation on the corrected chain. The split members' sub-steps see the predictor chain, an inconsistency of the coupling error's own order, which the norm already carries. The sweep differentiates the inner chain rows like any RK rows, with the draw's linear interpolation as a recorded map.

## 4. The gate

The pre-registered test compared every component of the interpolated state with a reference against its own error weight. It fails for all interpolants because dense-output error constants exceed a step's local error by small factors, and because it tests components the split member never reads, with weights that are tiny for empty pools. The quantity that matters is the split member's increment through what it reads, against the kink error it removes. Two gates, one offline and one online:

- *Offline, per interpolant:* halve only the crossing steps; the split members' end-state residual must fall at the pair's order (`h⁵`–`h⁶`), and `J`'s error must fall as `tol`. The cubic failed this (`h⁴`), the quartic and quintic pass it. That is the gate for choosing an interpolant.
- *Online, per split:* the split member's own sub-step embedded estimates within its `σ`; and an interpolant indicator, the quartic-minus-cubic difference in the read fields (free), propagated through `h_sub·∂f/∂(φ,Φ)`, required below a tenth of the split-minus-unsplit end-state change. It overestimates the quartic's error, so it is conservative, and it never looks at members the split does not read.

## 5. Workflow 2: the probe around the stored recording

**Walk the base's steps; refine inside them.** A probe step that coincides with a base step reads the recorded stage fields, bit for bit, so `J′ = J` on the diagonal; only refinements read the interpolant, and at `θ′ = θ` nothing triggers. Refinements are the same mechanism as events: a member re-integrated on sub-steps inside the global step, here when its own embedded estimate (free on a walk; compute it, act on it only in a probe) or `h/τ_eff,j` demands. With the `15δ` cap no probe needs it today; keep the mechanism as the answer to the next record that breaks a walk. A fully independent program would need the fields everywhere from the interpolant, lose the diagonal, and save at most the factor from finding 4 on the walk, which is not where the probe's cost is. The base must store nothing new: the recording already holds stage derivatives for the chain and every member's `x_j`, and `Φ` at an interior time is rebuilt from interpolated `x_j`, never interpolated from its stage values, which are built from internal stages.

**Thinning and the diagonal.** Thinned creation times cannot keep `J′ = J` at `θ′ = θ`; the diagonal keeps the base's program, and thinning turns on at a fixed distance from it (say `|ln θ′/θ| > 0.1`) rather than by a share threshold, so that the first gradient steps from a resident run on an unthinned program and the jump at the switch sits where `J′` is already far from `J`.

**Each evaluation exact for its own program.** The probe's tape is the base recording's identifier, `θ′`, its own stage solutions, and its program: thinning decisions, refinement sub-step times, cut times as located. The sweep replays it and reads the base's fields identically. Across evaluations `J′(θ′)` is piecewise smooth with jumps bounded by the program's error estimate, and each point's `(J′, ∇J′)` is consistent; this is the setting of trust-region methods with inexact evaluations whose required accuracy scales with the trust radius (Carter 1991; Heinkenschloss & Vicente 2002; Kouri et al. 2014), so report the estimate with every evaluation and let the optimiser tighten the program only as it converges. The probe's own error estimate costs nothing new: its embedded estimates on the walk paired with its own sweep's `λ′`, and for the creation axis its own two-rung difference on the base's existing ladder (a walk on a coarser rung is cheap). Convergence orders: fifth in `h` (∝ `tol`) with events, `tol^{0.15–0.2}` noise without; second in the creation spacing with the spread rule, inheriting the base's field error as a bias of the same order.

**Do probes need events?** Not for the gradient: 0.08–0.32 of `ε/3` unsplit at `1e-4`. For a probe's own curvature at `θ′ = θ` (ε = 3.8 for `θ_A`), forward second differences of walks at `u = 1e-2` average the staircase and are likely inside ε unsplit; test that before paying for probe events. If the base has events, the probe's splits are local and cheaper than the base's (the fields are data, no fan-out).

**Where the loop's cost actually goes.** Tens of probes at a walk plus a sweep is `tens × 3.4` forwards against a base of a few forwards to equilibrium plus one gradient; the probes dominate unless each is well under a forward. Three things change that. The selection gradient at `θ′ = θ` is free from the base's own sweep, accumulated as a second adjoint set with the field response removed. Along that gradient, forward-only walks at a geometric ladder of step lengths find `J′ > 1` without any probe sweep when the ray succeeds, and adoption needs no gradient at all; sweeps are for the rays that fail. And successive probes at nearby `θ′` warm-start their inner solves from the previous probe's recorded solutions at the same member and stage. With thinning at 0.53–0.66 and the sweep's own 47–51%, a probe that needs a gradient falls toward one forward, and most will not need one. The equilibrium iteration is a scalar secant in the input rate started from the previous loop's value; a tangent column buys nothing there.

## 7. Order and gates

The smallest build for workflow 1 is four stacked changes, the first three in the solver and the fourth a script:

1. Forward events in the step: quartic, two cuts at interior extrema, branch-selected sub-steps, FSAL re-evaluated at the corrected state. Gate: `J` ∝ `tol`, passage jumps ≤ 1e-8, graze continuous, forward cost ≤ 6%.
2. Multirate inner steps recorded and frozen. Gate: replay noise below 1e-10; cost unchanged.
3. Sweep: sub-step rows with field-read adjoints routed to stage derivatives, `t_c` frozen. Gate: sweep gradient against central differences of the frozen-grid forward to the replay floor; sweep cost ≤ +6% (sub-rows are under 2% of rows; the rest is the `Φ` fan-out).
4. Second derivatives by forward second differences at `u = 1e-2`, stable across `u ∈ [3e-3, 3e-2]` within `ε/10`. Gate: agreement with the chord to the chord's own floor, and no drift with `u`.

Then, as options gated by measurement: IFT routing of `t_c` (gate: immeasurable change, confirming branches); class switches behind a flag (gate: the `u`-scan shows a bias of order ε, or a step-count saving); and the three sweep savings you found, which are worth more than everything above combined. Workflow 2 waits on none of this: the diagonal gradient from the base sweep, the forward-only ray search, thinning beyond `0.1`, and on-demand refinement can be built on today's probe.

## 8. What you may have missed

Two things, both reductions. First, the Hessian diagonal is a forward-only product once `J(θ)` is smooth; the sweep is for gradients, and the pressure to make the sweep carry second derivatives disappears. Second, the probe loop as described pays for gradients it rarely needs: the invasion question is a feasibility question along a ray whose direction the base sweep already gives. Organised that way, the recording is a landscape engine, walks are its evaluations, and sweeps are the exception. If there is a version of either workflow in which the crossings stop mattering, it is this one for workflow 2: a probe steered by a loose-ε gradient over a box four length scales wide is never limited by kink noise, only by cost, and its cost is the sweep.
