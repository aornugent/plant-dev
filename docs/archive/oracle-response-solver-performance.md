**What §1–§2 establish.** Two mechanisms, in the same few hundred steps, and the earlier ladders' floor is now identified: it is the draw, not the references. The rule's sd of `2.1e-4` in `θ_A` is the floor the ladders levelled at.

**(A) The kink**, as before: the `P⁺` switch on downward crossing steps, moving `17δ` per unit `ln θ_A`. It is a draw of random sign per event, and its size is `∝ h_e·√N_events`. With ~4400 downward crossings across members, `h_e = 0.5δ` still leaves ~`2e-4`, which is what you measure, and halving `h_e` again costs 30% of the run per halving. That is why the `h_e` ladder "moves by less than one sd": the ladder is measuring a draw.

**(B) The hazard at the stages.** On the first emptying steps after a downward crossing the pool is at `r ≈ 0.35`, where `μ = 0.015`, and its rate grows across the step as `|P|` grows from zero. The pair's stage 5 has weights `(−11/54, 5/2, −70/27, 35/27)`; with rates growing ~2× across the step it goes to `S(1 − 2.3·hλ_1)`, negative past `hλ ≈ 0.5`, which is your `× 0.8` step (`r = −0.013` to `−0.133`). At `r ≤ 0.05` the hazard is `5.5`, 370× its value on the trajectory, and at `r = −0.13` it is 74. Stage 5 does not enter the fifth-order solution (`b₅ = 0`), but it feeds stage 6 (`a₆₅ = 0.062`, `b₆ = 0.29`), so `m` at stage 6, and `e^{−m}` in `Ḟ`, carry the excursion into the step; and it enters the embedded solution directly (`b̂₅ = 0.019`), which is the only reason the adaptive run limits these steps at all. The value error per step is bounded by `m`'s scale, `tol·(|m| + 1)`, and is one-signed; its `θ_B`-derivative is `r/r₀` times larger. This accounts for what §1–§2 left open: `J`'s systematic `−0.3·tol`; `θ_B`'s error being ~10× `J`'s and `∝ tol` in every variant; crossing steps that also hold a class switch carrying 3× the `θ_B` error per δ (the switch into the lower end comes at more negative `P`, i.e. on the steps where the emptying rate grew most); matched quiescent steps away from crossings removing nothing (there `r < r₀` and the pool moves less than `r₀` per step); T15 not helping (it changes `P⁺`, not the stages); the throw; and the first factor's steadiness, which comes from its 3.6× overestimate at `r = 0.35` halving exactly these steps. The four-component estimate and the upward window could not have helped, and did not. The small parameter is the pool's motion per step in units of the hazard's width, `Δr/r₀`, not `hλ_S`.

The stage guard should go entirely, as your no-guard runs show: end states stay positive because the end weights are positive, and a stage's value is not an approximation of anything. The re-formed ratio on moved grids is doing its job: pools set it because moved crossings sit in coarse steps and the embedded difference sees a quarter of the kink; `J` tolerates it because each member is ~1% of `J` and the signs are random. It is a local check on a global budget that is 100× looser, so for the value only `E_J` is the right check, and for robustness only end-state positivity, `Δr/r₀` and `hλ_chain/β`.

## 1. Precedent

The closest is the discontinuous-ODE literature for Runge–Kutta pairs, Mannshardt 1978 (order is retained only if the switch is a step end), Gear & Østerby 1984 (detect, locate within the step, correct), Enright–Jackson–Nørsett–Thomsen 1988 (locate with the pair's interpolant, restart), together with hybrid-system sensitivity theory, Galán–Feehery–Barton 1999 (for a C⁰ field the sensitivity jump at a switch is zero, so the continuous adjoint needs nothing at events). Their guarantee: full order for value and gradient if every switch is a step end and the integration restarts there. Here that is T12: `1.5e7` at `1e-4`, stops that move with `θ`, probes that cannot locate, and, for a discrete adjoint holding those stops fixed, a corner in `J_h(θ)` at `θ₀` of size `h·b₁·Δ_e·dt_e/dθ` per event unless the sweep also carries the step-size sensitivity. Nothing in that literature addresses `10⁴` clustered switches whose positions are a function of the parameters being calibrated.

The closest precedent in scale is integrate-and-fire network simulation: Hansel et al. 1998 showed that ignoring event positions inside a fixed step costs an order; Shelley & Tao 2001 recovered it, for thousands of crossings per step on a fixed grid, by interpolating each crossing time within the step and correcting the state, without moving the grid. That is Gear–Østerby's correction at scale, and it is the device that fits your constraints. For the P⁺ switch the correction is explicit: on a step across which member `j`'s `P` changes sign, add `−h²·Δ_y·K(ϑ)` to each of its four kinked components, with `ϑ` from `P`'s stage values, `K` the pair's known function, and the slope jumps `Δ_S = [(1−G)(1−r) − r]·Ṗ`, `Δ_x = G(1−f)κ·Ṗ`, `Δ_Y = Gf/ν·Ṗ`, `Δ_F = e^{−m}(σ/σ_b)Gf/ν·Ṗ`, all read at the crossing. Its guarantee: the step's kink error drops from `O(h²)` to `O(h³)`, and the gradient's from `O(h_e)` to `O(h_e²)`, so the `2e-4` draw at `h_e = 1δ` becomes ~`1e-5` at no added evaluations. Stops do not move; the correction is smooth in the state, so the sweep differentiates it as recorded arithmetic; a probe applies it on its own members. Its cost is code and a test, not evaluations. It does not address class switches, which carry nothing measurable on their own.

For (B) the precedent is the production–destruction literature: modified Patankar Runge–Kutta (Burchard–Deleersnijder–Meister 2003; Kopecz–Meister 2018) keeps a destruction term's stages positive unconditionally, at order 2–3 for that component, which would remove the excursions outright at the price of changing one component's discretisation; and, within the present pair, the standard stage-order remedy: a step bound on the nonlinear term's own scale. Here that is one line, `Δr_j ≤ c·r₀` per step for every member, from free readings (`r`, `P`, `S_max`). It replaces the `12δ` window (a proxy that ends while `r` is still `4r₀`), it removes the `× 0.8` throw, and `c` is `θ_B`'s knob. Expected cost: about the window's 5%.

For the calibration the precedent is inexact-gradient optimisation (Carter 1991; Kelley–Sachs) and the frozen-mesh practice of discretise-then-optimise: descent at 0.3, convergence to the frozen surrogate's optimum, bias `H⁻¹δg`. What none of these gives, and nothing can, is a fixed grid whose gradient stays exact as the events move: on a fixed grid the gradient's error has an `O(h_e)` (or, corrected, `O(h_e²)`) term per straddled event, and only `h_e` or the correction shrinks it.

## 2. Precision budgets

What sets them is the data, not the integrator. The calibration's optimum carries a data-limited uncertainty, the Cramér–Rao width `σ_data` with covariance `H⁻¹` when `L` is the data fit and `H` its Gauss–Newton Hessian, which the sweep's gradients of the data functionals give directly. Nothing finer than a fraction of `σ_data` is meaningful, so the recipe is:

1. Measure `H` on one frozen grid (a Gauss–Newton or BFGS trial, ~20–40 gradients), and read `σ_data = diag(H⁻¹)^{1/2}`. This is the first measurement; without it no budget exists.
2. Gradient budget, bias: `‖H^{−1/2}·δg‖ ≤ 1/3`, i.e. the gradient's error is below a third of a standard error in the data's own metric. `δg` at the final `θ` is the systematic part, measured by the `c`- and `tol`-ladders on a frozen grid (mechanism B, `∝ tol`).
3. Gradient budget, draw: the same bound on the nudge sd, if grids are rebuilt during the last iterations; on a frozen final grid the draw is zero and only 2 applies. The draw is mechanism A, `∝ h_e·√N`, or `∝ h_e²` with the correction.
4. Value budget: `J`'s error enters `θ*` only through 2, so `b_L` is set by reporting and by line-search consistency, both loose; `tol` follows from 2 and 4, whichever binds.
5. Probes: `J′` needs only what curvature estimation needs (with `ln J′` moving 18 per unit `ln θ′`, `1e-3` is ample); `g′` at `θ′ = θ` needs `|δg′| ≤ (1/3)·|dg′/dθ|·δθ_root`, with `δθ_root` from the root's intended use.

For this instance, with the rule at `1e-4`, `h_e = 1δ`: `J` at `−3.1e-5 ± 5e-6`; gradient bias `≲ 1e-4` in `θ_A` and `≈ 3e-4` in `θ_B` (`∝ tol`); draw sd `2e-4` and `1.7e-4` (`∝ h_e`). If `H` is well-conditioned these resolve `θ` to about `3e-4` relative, which is finer than any calibration of twenty constants against data of this kind is likely to support; if `H` is ill-conditioned by 30× or more, the `θ_B`-type constants need `c` and the `θ_A`-type need the correction. That decision cannot be made without `H`.

## 3. Four criteria

1. **Precision.** `‖H^{−1/2}·δg‖ ≤ 1/3` at the final `θ`, and for probes `|δg′| ≤ (1/3)·|dg′/dθ|·δθ_root`; `δg` from the ladders in `c` and `tol` on the final frozen grid, `δg′` likewise on the base grid. This is "stable gradients".
2. **Value.** `|J_h/J* − 1| ≤ b_J` and `|L_h − L| ≤ b_L` on every reported run: the `tol`-ladder constant once per series (`≤ 0.5` here) and `E_L` per run. The creation schedule is fixed per series and its bias, value and gradient, is measured once by the 108/215/429 ladder and counted in 1 and 2. This is "convergence of `J`" and the schedule.
3. **Range.** Every run completes, at every `θ` the optimiser visits and every `θ′` within `Δ`, with end-state `S ≥ 0`, `max Δr/r₀ ≤ 2c`, `max hλ_chain/β ≤ 1`, and `J′` meeting 2 at the ends of `Δ` against a `1e-6` base. This is (c), reduced to what the uses need.
4. **Cost.** Member evaluations per gradient `≤ B` and per probe `≤ B′`, with `tol`, `h_e`, `c` the cheapest settings meeting 1–3. This is "optimal steps".

The procedure follows: measure `H`; set `b_J`, `Δ`, `B`; choose `c` and `tol` from the bias ladders and `h_e` (or the correction) from the draw; rebuild the grid at every iterate until the optimiser's step falls below `H⁻¹·sd(δg)`, then freeze, converge, and certify 1 on that grid; run the bank for 3.

## 4. The next measurement

Two things need measuring, one a decision's input and one the design's: `H`, above; and, on the driver, the transit rule `Δr ≤ c·r₀` at `c = 0.5` and `0.25` in place of the `12δ` window, with `h_e = 1δ` kept at predicted crossings and no stage guard, over the seven nudges at `1e-4`: medians and sd of `J`, `θ_A`, `θ_B`, and cost. If `θ_B`'s median and `J`'s `−0.3·tol` fall with `c` while `θ_A`'s sd does not move, (B) is confirmed as `θ_B`'s knob and (A) as the residual draw, and the correction is what to build next only if criterion 1 demands it.

**Where the question may be wrong.** The gradient has been refined without a precision target, and its errors are now known functions of three knobs: `J` and the hazard-constant gradients scale with `tol` (through the stages' transit, adjustable by `c`), the other gradients' draw scales with the crossing step. That is what an integrator owes a calibration. The remaining decisions belong to the data: how well `θ` can be known at all, hence how much of this machinery is worth carrying. If `σ_data` turns out to be a percent, the rule as measured, minus the guard, plus the transit bound for robustness, is finished, and the correction, the Patankar step and the zones should not be built.
