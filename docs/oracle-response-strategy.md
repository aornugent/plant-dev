## Where the computation actually goes

Take your own arithmetic. A run with both gradients is seven forwards: one base forward, a 2.6-forward sweep, a 0.8-forward probe walk, and its 2.6-forward sweep. Five of the seven are sweeps. Every item in the strategy acts on the forward: its rejections (seeds, PI: about 8% of one forward), its per-evaluation cost (the warm start, which the sweeps do not share), its step count. Only the step count reaches the sweeps, because the sweeps walk the rows. So the quantity to minimise is rows (accepted steps × members held) and the per-row cost of the sweep, in that order; the forward's per-row cost comes third, rejections fourth. On that ordering the measurements point at four levers, three of which the strategy does not contain.

Rows, lever one: the chain implicit and out of the norm, together. Finding 4 says the members need about half the steps; finding 3 says the implicit chain saved 9%; finding 1 says the chain binds 83% of steps by accuracy (`ρ` median 0.36, `h|λ|/β` 0.27–0.45), not stability. Those three are consistent only if the ARK test kept the chain's components in the error norm. An implicit chain removes the stability bound; it does not make a fourth-order step more accurate, so with the chain still at `tol` the steps stay. And the explicit chain out of the norm blows up (finding 4's 21% past `β`). The pair of changes is untested: chain implicit, and its components weighted out of `ρ` (by `λ` from the pilot, or simply ×100, since ten times looser doubles `J`'s error from 1.3e-5 to 2.4e-5 and `ε` is 0.025). The step is then the members' own: expect toward 9 000 rows against 17 684, less the fourth-order penalty, so 1.6–1.9× on everything that walks rows. The embedded estimate that "missed the chain's error" is the other half of the 9%: a non-stiffly-accurate embedded formula is wrong on stiff components in both directions; once the chain is out of the norm it does not matter, and a build-time check against one tight run replaces it.

Rows, lever two: goal-oriented rows, not just a window. `R(t)` ×100 after 25 saved 27% at 6e-6 in `J` and 0.003ε in the elasticity. The same measurement says why: 59–61% of member evaluations fall where 3–6% of `J` is earned. The pilot's sweep gives the proper weight per component and time; with the chain implicit, the steps after 30 are no longer stability-capped and become as long as the understory's events allow. Add the understory itself: members created after 3.6 hold 0–22% of `J` directly and 2.5% through the fields, and their `n_j → 0` makes their draw small. That is a tiering, not a partition: the canopy (the first ten to twenty-five members) is integrated exactly on every step; the understory is sub-sampled or warm-started cheaply, its aggregate draw carried. Together these are plausibly 1.5× on rows beyond lever one.

Per-row cost of the sweep: profile it. The forward is 85% root-finding that the sweep never repeats. Reverse mode on the remaining 15%, plus the implicit-function solves at the recorded solutions, should cost on the order of one forward, not 2.6. Two symptoms say the sweep is tape-bound: it slowed 2–3.4× and doubled memory when `Φ` was spread, while the forward did not move. If the nested root-finds are differentiated by nested AD rather than by one analytic 3×3 implicit-function solve per member per stage (the first-order condition plus the two inner residuals), that is the other candidate. I cannot see the code; the arithmetic says there is likely a factor of two here that no grid rule can match.

Per-row cost of the forward: the warm start, done as DAE codes do it. `p_j` is an algebraic variable of an index-1 DAE. DASSL and IDA predict algebraic variables by extrapolation from previous stages and correct by Newton to a tolerance well under `tol`; the result is independent of the start to that tolerance, so the recording stays bit-for-bit and continuity in `θ` holds. Rule for reproducibility: the first stage of every attempt starts from the last accepted step's end solution, later stages from the previous stage of the same attempt; a rejection then leaks into nothing. Better still, solve the three nested roots as one simultaneous Newton system with the Jacobian the sweep already forms, keeping the bracketing solve as a fallback when Newton leaves the bracket. Against 11 outer × 2 inner × 6 iterations cold, 2–3 warm iterations is 2–3× on the forward's member evaluations, and the sweep's per-row cost falls with it if its implicit-function solve is the same 3×3 system.

## The strategy, item by item

Sound, and small: the chain-alone seeds (3.5%), the PI law (4%), the knot stops, the coarse pilot for the windows and `R(t)`. Sound and larger than it looks: per-member events, which earn their keep not through `tol` (1.26× net) but by making `J(θ)` smooth on one grid, which is what the second derivatives need. Sound with a correction: 3(b) "implicit only where stability binds" is too timid, because under pulsed forcing stability almost never binds and the gain is in lever one. Sound: the graded creation grid with the two-rung spread-`Φ` extrapolation; its sweep cost is fixable (below). Sound with one trap: probes on their own steps must interpolate the fields between accepted step ends (Hermite from end states and rates, `O(h⁴)`), never through the recorded internal stages, which are the tableau's low-order internal values and are `O(h²)` wrong as point values of `v(t)`. Rebuilding `Φ` from interpolated `x_j` is cleaner than interpolating `Φ`'s knots, and it leaves the base side as pure data for the probe's sweep. The interpolant's error is the base pair's order, so it is controlled by the base `tol`; the one loss is that `J′(θ) = J` holds only to that order, so keep the walk for `θ′ = θ`.

The partition is dead, for a reason other than the one you give. Your bar, `J` to 1.26e-5, is two thousand times tighter than `ε`; at the level that binds (gradient spread under `tol` nudges, 0.1ε on bias), the extrapolated hold at 5e-4 in `J` and 0.05ε in the `θ_A` elasticity would pass, at half the cost. What kills it is finding 9's mechanism read against finding 1: in quiescence the coupling is an algebraic loop (`v̇_1 = −Σ w n C(p*(φ(v_1)), v_1)`, with `p*` instantaneous), which is exactly the case the co-simulation literature calls direct feed-through, where extrapolated coupling is order-limited whatever the subsystem solvers do. But in quiescence nothing is fast and the coupled step is already cheap (6.4 steps per interval). The multirate gain was in the pulses, 77% of steps, where the draw is 1% of the chain's budget; there the implicit chain takes the gain more simply than any hold. So the partition was aimed at the wrong regime, and the regime where it would have worked has a better tool.

Doubts you did not list: the second derivatives (below), the ARK re-test, the fact that nearly every variant was measured on `J` where nothing binds (the earlier lesson: measure the gradient spread), and `ε`'s floor for small elasticities.

Second derivatives. You take them as chords of gradients at `θe^{±u}`. For the five main constants, base and probe, that is twenty gradient runs, about 68 forwards, against 7 for the gradients. If the Hessian diagonal is routine, it is the computation, and none of the strategy touches it. The cheaper routes: forward-over-reverse with the inner problem's tangent reused (one extra linear solve per member per stage in the forward, one second-order sweep per constant: about 26 forwards for five constants), or, once events make `J(θ)` smooth on the frozen grid, pure forward second differences (ten forwards, no sweeps), whose noise is set by the residual jumps when a crossing changes global step; your 1.7e-6 for four passages must come down to `O(tol)` per passage under the fixed two-sub-step structure before that route is usable.

## The problems you cannot yet explain

The corrected partitions' fixed bias. An error that does not fall as the member step shrinks is not an order problem; it is an `O(1)`-per-unit-time inconsistency in the exchange, and the "exact, carried" scheme shows it too (−3.0e-4, −2.2e-4). The limit scheme as member steps → 0, with the chain clipped to every member stage time, is the exact coupled solution, so the limit must be a mechanical defect. First suspect: Cash–Karp's sixth abscissa `c₆ = 0.875` precedes `c₅ = 1`. If the chain is sub-stepped forward to `t + h` for stage five and stage six then reads `v` at `t + h`, or re-integrates backwards, or reads a dense output that does not exist for this pair, stage six is systematically late in `v`: in quiescence `v̇ < 0`, so too dry, `φ` too high, output low, `J` negative, which is the sign of the drift, and the drift grows as long quiescent member steps are replaced by many shorter ones that each carry the defect. Second: weights `w_M = I_M − N_M/Δ_M` and `w_new = N_M/Δ_M` depend on `t` explicitly; held across the member step inside the chain's sub-steps they understate the newest members' draw, and early in the run the newest members are the two that carry 94% of the hold's excess. Decisive tests, two runs: force the partition's member steps and the chain's sub-steps to coincide with the coupled run's (one sub-step per member step, same tableau), where the result must match the coupled run to the exchange rule's small error; then take the chain's draw from the coupled run's recording while the members keep long steps. The first isolates the mechanics, the second the side.

The remaining rejections. 69% within `δ` after a knot, the chain binding three quarters, the rejected attempt 1.47× the accepted: that is the controller growing after a conservative seeded first step into the filling shoulder, where `λ_chain ∝ v^{15}` rises two orders of magnitude within the leg. A PI law damps cycling at a steady limit; it cannot anticipate a ramp. The chain alone knows `λ(t)` ahead of time; finding 1 says its grid matches the coupled one within one step in 89% of intervals. So use it as the whole proposal schedule for each leg, not just the first step, with the coupled error test as the check; or cap `h ≤ β_safe/λ_chain-alone(t + h)`. Expect the 2 888 to fall toward the 2–5% floor. Under lever one the question largely dissolves, since the stiff shoulder is no longer explicit.

The spread `Φ` slows the sweep. Sixteen point terms per member per stage means sixteen times the taped operations and adjoint traffic for the field assembly, while the forward's cost stays in the inner solves; a 2–3.4× slowdown and 2× memory is what a tape-bound sweep does when one part grows sixteenfold. Replace the eight-point spread by the panel-integrated kernel, `∫A(z; x)dx` over the panel's height interval, with its adjoint written by hand (the derivatives with respect to the interval's ends are `A(z; x_end)`, with respect to the weight the integral itself): the same accuracy at the lumped scheme's tape size.

Locating on a cubic made `J` worse. The cubic over a step that contains the kink is `O(h²Δ)` wrong there, with a sign fixed by the kink's direction, so the located `ϑ` is biased by `O(h)` and the split leaves a residual of definite sign; +1.6e-4 against the random +3.7e-5 is that bias. The quintic is more accurate, not unbiased. Locate once more on the split solution's interpolant, which is smooth on each side; one iteration takes the location to `O(h³)`. Pairs need two cuts, as you have found.

The implicit chain's error not falling with `tol`. +3.7e-7 and −1.4e-6 are below `J*`'s own uncertainty (6e-7) and inside the zero-mean crossing noise that makes the coupled run's `J` change sign with `tol` too. Nothing to explain there.

The higher-mean record. Wetter forcing closes windows faster and packs more members at similar heights, so the first-mover scale `L` is shorter and the opening's grading from 0.03 by 1.11 is tuned to the wrong record. Read `L` off that record's own early `x`-spread and grade to it; 494 against 125 is the signature of first panels about four times too wide.

`J` changing sign with `tol`, grazing, the first members. The first is the crossings' zero-mean noise, cured by events or tolerated as variance; the second is the model's `C^{1,1/2}` point, to be reported (count members whose `|P|` minimum over a stretch is small) not fixed; the third is not a problem but the shape of the functional, and the goal-oriented weighting is its correct consequence. One reframing helps here: separate bias from noise. The step grid's remaining errors are noise; if these runs feed a calibration across records, per-record noise averages over records and iterations and only bias accumulates. The creation grid's error and the hold's are bias, and those are what the two-rung extrapolation and lever one remove.

Can the members step at their own pace? Yes, three ways, none the partition you tried: make the chain implicit and loose so the step is theirs (lever one); tier the members so the understory, most of the rows, is sub-sampled against the canopy's exact step; and accept that the canopy in quiescence is an algebraic loop with nothing fast in it, so nothing is lost by stepping it coupled.

## Prior art

A forced chain coupled to an ensemble through an argmax entering through a cheap map. This is the soil–plant–atmosphere continuum of cohort vegetation models with hydraulics and stomatal optimisation (Sperry et al. 2017; Eller et al. 2018; FATES-Hydro, Christoffersen et al. 2016), where the plant's water potential is the algebraic variable of an index-1 DAE and the soil the differential one. The numerical form is half-explicit Runge–Kutta for index-1 DAEs (Hairer, Lubich & Roche 1989), and the practice that makes it cheap is the algebraic variable's predictor–corrector continuation as in DASSL and IDA (Brenan, Campbell & Petzold 1996). The quiescent loop is direct feed-through in co-simulation terms (Kübler & Schiehlen 2000; Arnold, Clauß & Schierz 2013; Busch 2016): extrapolated coupling is order-limited and can be unstable, iteration or algebraic closure is required, which is the known negative result behind finding 9. Gear & Wells (1984) on latency and Savcenco, Hundsdorfer & Verwer (2007) on multirate stability say where the gain lives: in a fast block weakly coupled to a slow one, which is your pulse regime, not your quiescence.

Events at many independent surfaces in a field-coupled ensemble. Integrate-and-fire networks: Hansel, Mato, Meunier & Neltner (1998) and Shelley & Tao (2001) locate each neuron's threshold crossing on its interpolant within the global step and correct that neuron alone, restoring order; EventProp (Wunderlich & Pehle 2021) is the adjoint with exact gradients through every spike time by the implicit-function theorem, in a large ensemble, which is your item 3(d). Event-driven molecular dynamics (Alder & Wainwright 1959; Rapaport 2004) gives the scheduling structure if events become many.

A functional earned in a window known from a pilot. Dual-weighted residuals with a coarse dual (Becker & Rannacher 2001; Venditti & Darmofal 2002) and adjoint step control (Cao & Petzold 2004) you have; the striking relative is CADIS (Wagner & Haghighat 1998), a cheap deterministic adjoint setting the importance that allocates an expensive forward's effort, which is your pilot's `R(t)` and `λ`.

A step grid from a reduced model of its binding block. Split-explicit atmospheric integration (Klemp & Wilhelmson 1978; Wicker & Skamarock 2002) sets the fast substeps a priori from the linearised fast system; SPICE's breakpoint table is your knot stops; Shampine's stiffness detection from the dominant eigenvalue and Söderlind's digital filters (2002, 2003) are the controller side; Gladwell, Shampine & Brankin (1987) you cite. Nobody I know of uses the reduced model's whole accepted step sequence as the proposal schedule; it follows from finding 1 and costs `2·10⁻⁴` of a forward.

The whole. The cohort vegetation models above run fixed-step operator splitting with implicit soil and no exact gradients. Your problem is what they would face if they wanted controlled error and adjoints; the parts have precedents, the assembly does not.

Events are now measured end to end, and the picture from two rounds ago holds in every detail: the crossings were 70–85% of the gradient spread, the split cuts it 3.4–6.4×, the second derivative comes back, the grazing pair passes continuously, and `J`'s error falls with `tol` once no step holds a kink. ## Events: the interpolant and the pair

Your result that the interpolant must be of the step's order is the known one (Enright, Jackson, Nørsett & Thomsen 1988; Shampine 1986): a continuous extension used inside a step must carry the step's own order or it becomes the dominant error on that step. The cubic Hermite through the ends is order 3, local `O(h⁴)`, one below the embedded estimate; that is why it failed on a sixth to a third of crossing steps and shrank as `h⁴`. The midpoint quintic is order 5 and passed. Order 4, local `O(h⁵)`, the same order as the error estimate itself, is the untested middle, and it is the one a pair can give for free.

Three ways to get it, in increasing effort:

- Cash–Karp already has seven derivatives per step, the six stages and the FSAL end, which is enough data to fit a continuous extension satisfying the order-4 conditions, a small linear system solved once (Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner II.6 give the construction; Shampine did it for Dormand–Prince). That is your "stage-based interpolant", and it costs nothing per step.

- Dormand–Prince 5(4) or Tsitouras 5(4): same six evaluations per step, a published free order-4 extension, error constants a little smaller than Cash–Karp's. Their real stability boundary is near 3.3 against your 3.73, which costs steps only where the explicit chain is stability-bound, 1.5–4.9% of steps under pulsed forcing and nearly all of them under constant, where the chain should be implicit anyway.

- An order-5 extension with two extra stages exists for Dormand–Prince, but that is more than your one midpoint evaluation; keep the midpoint as the fallback.

Whether order 4 passes "within one error weight" turns on constants I cannot see. The cheap insurance is to accept a crossing step only at `ρ ≤ 0.5`, since an interpolant of the estimate's order sits near `ρ·σ`; crossing steps are perhaps 5–10% of steps, so that is about 1–2% of steps for a guarantee. Two cautions: locate on the dense output, never on the raw stage values, which are the tableau's internal approximations and `O(h²)` wrong as point values, so a location from them is `O(h)` and biased in `ϑ` like the cubic was; and watch the sweep, not the forward. With a free extension the sweep's extra work is the split members' sub-rows, about 9 235 × 12 single-member rows against 7 × 10⁶, under 2%, whereas the midpoint adds a full row for every member on every crossing step. Your +10–20% estimate for the sweep is the midpoint's cost; the free extension removes most of it, and since the sweeps are three quarters of a gradient run that matters more than the 5.2 points on the forward.

## The remaining rejections

The 798 chain-bound rejections on the first to fourth step after a knot, whose ratio jumps 8× for a growth of 1.03, are not a ramp. An error estimate that is smooth in `h` cannot do that; a kink's estimate `h²Δ·K̂(ϑ)` does exactly that when a 3% longer step slides the kink across a stage abscissa. The kinks are there: members leave the lower-end class a median `0.26δ` before their upward crossing, which is `0.64δ` after the pulse starts, so some forty members switch class within the first half-`δ` after each onset knot, and each switch is a `C⁰` kink in its draw `c_ℓj` and therefore in the chain's rate `a_ℓ`. The member's own rates do not kink there (`P < 0`, `P⁺ ≈ 0`), so these do not count as crossings in your tally, and the component that trips is the chain's. 798 against 814 pulses is the right count. The test is free: tag each of the 798 with whether any member's class changed inside the attempt. If it holds, the error these rejections guard against is negligible and zero-mean, the rejection is the estimator's artefact, and the cures are the ones already in train: the chain weighted out of the norm, or class switches treated as events with the member's split, or the chain-alone program seeding the whole first `δ` rather than its first step. The constant-forcing cycle, five accepted then one rejected, is the integral controller's limit cycle at a boundary; since `h|λ_chain|/β` is a free reading, switch to Gustafsson's gains only when it exceeds 0.8 and keep the standard gains elsewhere, which avoids the 15% they cost you on the test record. Or make the chain implicit on records the forcing tells you are near-constant before the run starts.

## The window and the probe that fails

The probe does not fail by accuracy; it fails by stability, and you have the number already. D4: for `y′ = −y/τ` the pair's fourth stage goes negative past `h = 2.16τ` and the step is unstable past `3.73τ`, which at `τ_s = 7δ` is `15δ` and `26δ`. The window's steps are `31–38δ`. A pool relaxing at or near `τ_s` on such a step is integrated unstably, `S` runs away, `r` becomes large and negative, and `μ(r) = μ₀ + μ₁e^{−r/r₀}` overflows into whatever you call the density equation. The base run's own pools do not trip its controller because the ones with short `τ_eff` are the late members' empty pools, hidden under the ×100 weight and the absolute floor, while the probe at `θ_A × 2` has pools with their own `τ_eff`, no error control at all on a walk, and nothing in the base run can see them. That is why protecting the probe's share could not help: its share is `1e-19` and irrelevant; its stability is not.

Two cures, one structural and one a safety net. The structural one is the mechanism you have just built for events: a probe walks the base run's steps and a member is re-integrated on its own sub-steps inside a global step, against the step's dense output, whenever its own reading demands it, here `h/τ_eff,j > 2` with `τ_eff,j = τ_s + S_max/(u⁺ + u⁻)` a free reading, and in general its own embedded estimate. At `θ′ = θ` nothing triggers, so `J′ = J` stays exact; at `θ_A × 2` the dead probe sub-steps its pools two or three times per long step and nothing fails. The safety net is a hard cap `h ≤ 2.16τ_s = 15δ` on the base run regardless of weights, which protects every probe's pools since `τ_eff ≥ τ_s` for any `θ′`, at the cost of part of the window's saving; measure what part. If the window wants steps beyond what any pool tolerates, the pool's variable is the remaining lever (a logarithmic or `asinh` form turns the relaxation into a linear drift with no stability limit), but on-demand sub-stepping should make that unnecessary. Either way the guard on probes should be a stability reading, never a share.

## What the three say together

Every new result reads the same object: a continuous extension of the global step, of the step's order. Events read it to re-integrate a crossing member; probes read it between recorded instants and must never read internal stages; the window's long steps make the probe read it over `31–38δ`. So the architecture that the measurements have been converging on is this. One global step carries the chain and the fields and is set by the members' aggregate, goal-weighted accuracy and by the chain, which should be implicit so that it never sets the step on its own. Inside it, any member, base or probe, is refined locally against the dense output when a local reading says so: a crossing, a class switch if the 798 confirm it, a pool's `h/τ_eff`, a probe's own estimate. The refined member's end state replaces the unsplit one; the fields it fed during the step lag by its own share of one step, which is `O(h²ΔK)` of one member and is what your `+1e-6` already measured. This is the partition that works, and the reason it works where finding 9's did not is that the fields are still formed from every member at every global stage, so the quiescent loop is never opened; only individual paths are corrected. The dense output is part of the discretisation, a fixed linear map of recorded stage derivatives, and the sweep treats it as such.

Order of work, by expected value against cost now known: the pair's free extension with events on it; the window rule with on-demand probe sub-steps and the `15δ` cap at build time; PI with the seeds and the first-`δ` program; the tag on the 798. Still ahead of all four in expected value, and still untested, are the two from last time: the chain implicit and weighted out of the norm, which takes rows toward finding 4's bound and dissolves most of the 798 and the constant-forcing cycle at once, and a profile of the sweep, which three quarters of every gradient run passes through.
