Let me own the kill first, then extract what it bought.

**1. Waveform relaxation is dead as a solver — I proposed it, you ran the exact kill test I specified, κ ≈ 10 killed it. But κ is now the most explanatory number in the record.** Per-window κ uniform at 5–12 means the loop gain is set by the fast block's response and is already saturated at H = T/6; shrinking windows until κ < 1 drives H down toward the fast timescale, which is held-coupling territory — the measured 0.1 plateau. Anderson/Newton–Krylov on the residual would need ~10–20 full-M sweeps per window against the global method's single pass: dead on economics regardless of convergence. What survives is everything the number explains at once: the held-`a` plateau, the 3–4× linearization failures, the 6–25× decomposition losses, the ~10× J-amplification, the 23% inter-scheme spread — one measured loop gain behind the entire §7 graveyard. And it explains 9c's strangest result: with gain-10 feedback, the difference between two meshes is dominated by the *global field shift* each mesh induces, not by local interpolation defect — which is precisely why a local surplus indicator anti-correlates (−0.56…−0.86) with actual inter-mesh differences. Local indicators cannot see feedback-dominated error. That's a diagnosis, not a dead end: the correct indicator is probe sensitivity weighted through the feedback resolvent (I − T′)⁻¹ — which brings me to the instrument.

**2. Run Arnoldi on T′ — the sweep machinery from 9b is a matrix-vector product, and the spectrum answers the question underneath everything.** κ ≈ 10 is a directional norm along `a*`; the quantity that decides whether this problem is *solvable at all* is the distance of spec(T′) from +1, because the conditioning of the mean-field fixed point is ‖(I − T′)⁻¹‖. The iteration space is tiny — five functions plus `s(t)` — so 15–25 sweeps on saved fields (all offline, all parallel) maps the dominant spectrum. Outcome A: eigenvalues clustered away from 1 → the fixed point is well-conditioned, the continuum answer exists and is stable, and 9c's non-convergence is *protocol* — fixable, see below. Outcome B: eigenvalues near 1 → J as currently defined is not a stable observable of the model, no discretization choice converges it, and the honest deliverable becomes a banded J or a redefined observable — which you've explicitly licensed by allowing model changes. Either outcome ends the ambiguity that has haunted every measurement since the 23% spread first appeared. As a byproduct, the dominant modes are exactly the resolvent weights the placement indicator needs.

**3. Decompose ΔJ on a common field — the experiment 9c didn't run, now feasible because of the replay-memory fix.** Solve mesh 2N entirely as probes on mesh N's frozen field, then compare to the self-consistent 2N solve, with lineage matching across the pair. That splits the 9–45% family disagreement into three owned terms: quadrature-at-fixed-field (placement genuinely matters), field shift (κ-amplified feedback — placement per se innocent), and survivor flips (branch structure). One caveat from your own 9a table: probe error is O(weight fraction), so the heavy atoms' terms carry a known band — interpret with it, or correct their entries with participant re-solves.

**4. You may be refining along an axis that cannot converge — and 9a quietly proved it.** τ_ins-refinement adds members but never splits the heavy atoms, because skewness is *emergent*: max ρ_j stays ~0.3–0.4 at every mesh density. Your 9a table is direct evidence that a single atom's self-consistency matters at O(its weight fraction) — 6300% at 0.388, 42% at 0.06, vanishing only below ~1%. If the continuum transport equation of §2(i) is the target, a discrete measure whose heaviest atom carries 40% of the mass sits O(tens of %) from its own limit *no matter how many light members you add* — which quantitatively retrodicts the 23% spread and the 9–45% family disagreement, and explains why successive refinement changes grow instead of shrink. The remedy is standard particle-method practice never tried here: **split heavy atoms** into k children with ρ/k and a small ξ-jitter (identical children stay identical forever — the jitter is load-bearing), which preserves the represented measure exactly at the event, injects zero transient, and directly reduces the granularity error. Test: take the finest mesh, split the top handful of atoms 2- and 4-ways across a jitter-scale sweep, watch J. Movement → you've found the true convergence axis; invariance → granularity is innocent and the blame shifts to flips/insertions. Companion fix: replace boundary insertions with *splits of existing members* — measure-preserving birth — after first auditing what current insertions inject (log the u-transient per insertion; if total injected mass grows with mesh density, that alone explains "refinement makes it worse").

**5. The largest untested arbitrage: the O(M) block is being integrated at the fast block's resolution.** Members are 10²–10³× slower than u, yet the shared global step — median 2–7×10⁻⁴, sub-knot — makes every member pay 10⁴–10⁵ solves per run where its own dynamics might need 10²–10³. The members' error components dominating rmax (70%) doesn't contradict this: slow trajectories driven by fast u carry fast *texture*, and the controller is resolving that texture's phase. The question is whether J needs it — and "J converged at modest tolerance, 47× more cost buys nothing" hints it doesn't. Decisive offline test with the 9a machinery: advance probes against low-pass-filtered (u,s)(t) at a sweep of cutoffs, watch J-contributions versus cutoff (interpret with the ±κ band; confirm closed-loop as a model variant where only `g` sees filtered u). If ripple phase is J-irrelevant above some frequency, an averaged member advance is licensed — members at macro steps, u sub-cycled — and the count of full-M solves drops 10–100×. That requires the one thing §7 couldn't supply: a cheap fast refresh of a(u).

**6. That enables a Taylor refresh of a(u) with a *computable* second-order trust monitor, re-expanded on demand rather than per-macro (the failure mode you measured was frozen expansion across full macro excursions near bounds). Offline falsifier before building anything: walk the saved reference trajectory and count how often the trust region would have demanded re-expansion; if it's every fast step near bounds and bounds dominate, it dies as its ancestors did. Standalone, Newton-on-g is still the top per-eval lever (~32 → ~15–20 units) and removes the τ-floor — retiring the 2.4× J(τ) flip as a solver artifact, leaving only genuine branch structure for the inventory.
