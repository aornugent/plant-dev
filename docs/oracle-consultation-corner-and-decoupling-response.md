## 1. What the corner is, and what it retro-explains

`P(p)` at fixed state is a flat shelf, a jump of fixed magnitude ~1.5 at `p*`, then a smooth monotone decline at slope −8.8. That is not the graph of an objective with an interior maximum; it is the graph of a branch switch: on the wet side of `p*` some internal condition fails — an inner root ceases to exist or a feasibility/penalty branch engages (a fixed-size drop is the signature of a fallback branch value, and a root that folds is the classic way a smooth family produces a jump in a value function) — and the "argmax" is simply the last point on the surviving branch. In the language of the very first consultation in this series: this operating point is C5 (an active constraint), not C4 (an interior optimum) — it was misclassified, and every envelope-based idea since inherited the misclassification.

Everything anomalous now falls out:

* E1's objective slope ≈ 1: no stationarity, so `P(p*_τ) − P(p*) ≈ −8.8·O(τ)`. The envelope asymmetry was never there to find.
* R6's tracked-`p` failure, fully: gradient ascent on this landscape has no fixed point — the drive is a constant −8.8 on one side and ~0 on the shelf — so `ṗ = k·∂P/∂p` marches `p` off the branch edge at every gain; non-monotonicity in `k` is exactly what a flow with no attractor does. Not a tuning failure; a structural one.
* The §6.6 adjoint concern is upgraded from suspicion to certainty, and broadened. With `∂P/∂p = −8.8 ≠ 0` at the operating point, no output is stationary — not `c`, not `g`, and not `P` itself. Whatever the current adjoint does with `p*` — freeze it (the envelope-FD-at-fixed-`p*` path) or differentiate through the golden-section comparisons (which yields an affine combination of the bracket-endpoint derivatives, a quantity that coincides with the corner's true motion only if the whole interval translates rigidly — your measured slope −1.0004 could be exactly that accident) — it is wrong at first order through every member solve, by terms of size `(∂c/∂p, −8.8)·∂p*/∂state`, into a `J` that amplifies 10×. This is now the highest-priority correctness item in the program, independent of everything else in this round.
* It is benign for `f`'s smoothness in `y` — the corner lives in `p` at fixed state, `p*(state)` is measured smooth, so `f(y)` is smooth up to the τ-staircase E2 just exonerated. The corner is not the speed mechanism, and "the corner passing through the RK stages" is answered: it doesn't; it moves with the state, smoothly.

The fix is a locator on the switch condition, and it is cheap. Instrument the evaluator to log which internal branch produces the 1.5 drop (one afternoon); call its indicator `S(p; state)`. If the wall is a root-fold of the inner balance, the defining system is the bordered fold system (`balance = 0`, `∂balance/∂v = 0` — two equations, two unknowns, regular Jacobian at a quadratic fold; standard limit-point tracking). Solve it by safeguarded Newton to machine precision — a few evaluations versus the golden search's dozens, so likely cost-negative — and present `p*` as a scalar IFT node on `S`, giving exact `∂p*/∂state` for the adjoint and exact one-sided derivatives for every downstream output. This deletes the τ-staircase at the source, replaces the wrong adjoint channel, and — per your J(τ) table — should flatten that table at 5.87·10⁻⁸ for every setting, which is its acceptance test.

## 2. The speed side: what's actually left after two refutations

That is an unaudited accuracy hole sitting upstream of the J-bifurcation and plausibly a contributor to the 23% inter-scheme spread; count them and their `err/tol` overshoot before trusting any J from any run. (ii) It censored E2's `h_min` row: both runs bottomed on the same clamp, so "demanded minimum h vs τ" was never measured — the rejection invariance carries the refutation (it stands), but one unclamped probe run is needed to learn what step the controller actually wants and what demands it.

With events dead and the floor dead, the surviving candidates are three, and one instrumented field adjudicates all of them. The candidate I put first — flagged in round 5 and never tested, and the only one consistent with every surviving observation — is that the step size is being set by the error norm's treatment of vanishing components: depleted reservoirs `u_ℓ` and collapsing member weights `ρ_j → 0` under relative-error scaling demand absurd absolute accuracy on quantities `J` never feels. Check it against the ledger: broadband and scattered (whichever tiny component currently dominates is pseudo-random); clustered in quiet intervals near depletion (that's when components vanish); the −0.91 correlation with distance-from-balance and the weak collinear correlations with depletion margins and bracket width (all monotone in the same depletion); invariant to inner τ; co-located with no event; uniform ~30% rejections across sequences; J converged three decades earlier (J doesn't care about relative accuracy of things going to zero); and even round 6's implicit-`u` fiasco (the norm demands the same accuracy regardless of stiffness treatment). After two lost bets I present this as the leading candidate of a short slate, not a conviction — but it is the cheapest discriminator left, and the test is decisive:

## 3. The accuracy side: the bifurcation is the 10×/23% problem made mechanical — and τ was never the real input

Your J(τ) table alternates between two values — one marginal member flipping in and out as the O(τ) corner bias wobbles its survival. Read that generally: J is a step function of its inputs near survival thresholds, and τ is merely one input among many. θ is another. Consequences, in order of leverage:

(1) The exact locator (§1) removes τ from the map entirely — with `p*` at machine precision the flip is pinned by the true trajectory, J(τ) flatlines, and the 2.4× production error disappears. Do this first; it is cheap, it is also the gradient fix, and its acceptance test is already tabulated.

(2) But exactness does not make J differentiable — the flip just moves from τ-space to θ-space. Near a survival threshold, `dJ/dθ` is a branch slope plus a jump: the adjoint computes the branch slope; a finite difference straddling the flip measures the jump; the contract "gradient must match FD of the solver as run" is unsatisfiable there by any method, and an outer optimizer fed either object will behave badly. The only genuine fix is functional-side, and the data have now forced what three rounds of ranking kept flagging: mollify the survival entry into J — a smooth window over the survival margin (width declared, budgeted in J-units against the 10× amplification), or a reformulated reduction that never thresholds a member's existence. This is a model decision behind the model-side seam; the numerics can only tell you, as they now have, that without it the functional is not an observable with a derivative.

(3) Audit the forced-accept episodes (§2) before re-baselining any J — uncontrolled error at depletion episodes is upstream of the very survival decisions the bifurcation rides on, and may account for part of the 23% spread that has haunted the program since round 5.

Reducing τ alone is a stopgap (≤10⁻⁶ if you must ship before the locator), not a lever: the approach is non-monotone and the θ-space discontinuity remains untouched.
