## Pass 1 — what the record establishes, and where it doesn't close the door

Since the document poses no question, I'll answer the one §8 seems built for: what the record establishes, and the few places where it doesn't yet close the door — each framed as a measurement on the full coupled system, since the hardest-won lesson here is that anything argued off-system has coin-flip sign reliability.

**1. The gradient seam can be closed without making the argmax an AD citizen.** The open item — the envelope FD at fixed `p*` drops `(∂c/∂p)(∂p*/∂state)` — is framed as needing either a differentiable inner solve or an interior optimum. It needs neither. `p*` is defined not by `∂P/∂p = 0` (no root, as the retired Newton fix showed) but by the branch condition itself: the locus where the nested sub-root ceases to exist. The solver already evaluates that test on every member solve — it's how the fallback shelf fires — so a computable scalar `g(p, state)` with `g(p*, state) = 0` exists in the code today. IFT on `g`, not on `∂P/∂p`, gives `∂p*/∂state = −g_state/g_p`, well-posed precisely because the corner *locus* is smooth even though the objective isn't — and the measurement backs this: `p*(state)` traced linear to the `τ`-floor, slope `−1.0004`. If the branch death is a fold of the nested root-find (`F(r,p,state)=0` with `F_r → 0`), then `g = F_r` at the root and both its derivatives come from one more layer of IFT on `F`; if it's a domain-boundary exit, `g` is that boundary condition. Either way: keep the fixed-iteration primal for the tape, feed the seam the analytically restored `dc = ∂c/∂state + (∂c/∂p)∂p*/∂state` (and the same on the `ẋ` channel if it's frozen there too), and validate against §3's own gold standard — the FD that re-solves the inner problem.

**2. The `J` pathology looks like finitely many survival bits — and that's one experiment away from settled.** The `τ`-flip (one member, `2.4×`), the `~10×` amplification, and the `~23%` inter-scheme spread plausibly share a mechanism: perturbations moving heavy-or-future-heavy atoms across the `ρ`-removal manifold, so `ΔJ` between schemes lives in the *unmatched* members (born/died differently), not diffusely across matched ones. The experiment: pair the two converged schemes at `M ≈ 350`, match members by insertion lineage, decompose `ΔJ` into matched-pair drift vs. unmatched survival differences. If diffuse — `J`'s conditioning is genuinely that bad and 23% is the honest floor. If it's a handful of flips (my bet, given the `τ` datum), then sharp `J` is a step function no integrator converges cheaply, and the remaining choice is definitional: either taper `tw_j` to zero `C¹`-smoothly over a declared width at the removal threshold (functional only, dynamics untouched) and report width-sensitivity, or report sharp `J` with a flip inventory (members within `ε` of threshold, their `φ`-contributions) as a bracket. A cheap corollary check: condition the member-reduction `J`-errors on whether the survivor set changed — if the `10×` amplification is bimodal, scheme comparison becomes fair again as (`J` | matched survivors) + flips. And I'd log near-removal membership at the isolated `h → 10⁻⁹` points behind the tight-tolerance wall: those may *be* these crossings, in which case the wall, the flip, and the spread are one phenomenon with one owner — the observable's definition, not the integrator.

**3. The multi-block overflow reads as a false-negative error estimate walking into the fast block's stability ceiling.** Accepted steps sit 3–4 decades below `h·|λ| ≈ 3.3` only because accuracy binds first; the controller may grow `5×` per accepted step, so roughly five consecutive under-reports — both orders of the pair overshooting together, the classic embedded-pair blind spot when striding over a quiescent-then-active reservoir episode — covers the gap, and an explicit method past its ceiling on `u^16` produces exactly "non-finite at growing ~0.25-unit steps." Checkable from existing logs: `h·|λ_res|` over the last dozen accepted steps before the blow-up. Two guards, no method change: reject any attempt with a non-finite stage or a pre-clamp domain violation in `u`; and cap `h` at a safety fraction of the closed-form `L×L` reservoir bound divided by the measured worst-case coupling amplification (`291×`) — engaged only near the ceiling, free elsewhere.

**4. The uniform ~30% rejection fraction looks like extreme-value statistics of a max-norm over a growing block.** If the component achieving `rmax` reshuffles step to step, you get exactly what's measured: a reject fraction insensitive to forcing shape and `τ`, nothing serial for a PI controller to predict (it lost 13–29%), no event co-location, and rejected trials `2.4–4×` larger — the controller strides until the tail bites. One log query settles it: persistence of the argmax-component identity across attempts. If confirmed, the dead-band striding is vindicated as-is — but it also exposes the one direction §6 never tried: **goal-oriented error control**. The controller currently buys trajectory-norm accuracy while the target is `J`; weighting member-block error components by a `J`-relevance proxy (floored `ρ_j`, or adjoint weights from a prior pass) stops near-zero-weight members from setting `rmax`. The caveat comes from point 2 — at-risk members can be future-heavy, so anything near the survival threshold keeps full weight. Judged in `J`-units, per the house rule. This is the only remaining idea that could reduce *accepted* steps rather than overhead.

**5. On cost, the record proves the skeleton is a floor; what's left is a bounded constant factor.** `ẋ` needs all `M` inner solves per stage however `u` is advanced; step count is accuracy-set; and §5's own split says the search adds only ~3–4 objective evaluations over the mandatory one — so warm *bracketing* (unlike the retired warm Newton) is nearly neutral. The honest levers: cache the `u`-independent setup (~⅓ of member cost) across the six stages of a step and across rejection retries — an `O(h)` perturbation on the slow channel; and salvage rejections via a continuous extension, accepting `θh` from the already-computed stages when its error passes, converting the ~23% of stage-work currently discarded into progress (which argues for a pair with a free interpolant over Cash–Karp). Stacked and validated in `J`-units — including on the `τ`-flip sequence, where any `O(τ)`-scale perturbation can change the branch — that's plausibly `1.5–2×`, and I would not expect more from any direction the record hasn't already killed.

Happy to go deeper on any of these — the `g`-differentiation worked out against the actual form of the nested root-find, the `ΔJ` lineage-decomposition design, or the salvage/interpolant mechanics — whichever thread is the live one.

## Pass 2 — "the record doesn't prove what it feels like it proves"

Good — because the record doesn't actually prove what it feels like it proves. Read §6 closely: every entry is a search over *integrators* against a frozen problem statement. The skeleton (global explicit, all-M per eval) was proven near-optimal. But the problem statement itself has five soft joints that were never varied — the forcing representation, the inner-solve *equation*, the measure representation, the error *norm*, and the observable's definition — and each can be changed at identical model fidelity. That's where the stones are. Ranked by information-per-cost, with the falsifier attached to each, per house rules.

**1. The forcing node lattice is the prime suspect for the entire broadband accuracy limit — and no instrument ever looked at it.** §1 describes `b(t)` as a C² spline; §4 says every measured sequence drove it through a *fine piecewise-linear node grid, a few hundred nodes per time unit*. Piecewise-linear means a C¹ discontinuity of `f` at every node — ~300 per unit, spacing ~3×10⁻³, sitting roughly 10× above the median accepted step. The §6f event inventory logged clamps, switch-off, argmax margins, and the sparse *feature* times — never the grid nodes. Now check what this one hypothesis retrodicts: order-5 crossing a C¹ kink drops to local O(h²|Δb′|), so the controller runs a stride-grow-stumble limit cycle on the lattice — which gives you the uniform 27–35% rejects across wildly different shapes (same lattice density everywhere), the rejected/accepted ratio of 2.4–4× (grown step spans a kink, fails, shrinks under it), the τ-invariance, the PI controller's loss (kink phases are unpredictable relative to steps — nothing serial to predict, exactly as measured), and the wall: h ~ tol^{1/2} under kink-limited error predicts 10^{3/2} ≈ 32× for three decades against the measured 47×, where pure order-5 predicts 4×. It even explains the multi-block overflow: at h ≈ 0.25 a step spans ~75 kinks, *both* orders of the embedded pair alias the unresolved forcing the same way, the estimator under-reports, the controller grows into the u^16 ceiling — a false-negative cascade, not a stability mystery. Tests, cheapest first: (a) one log query — distance from each rejected interval to the nearest *node*, not feature; enrichment ratio against accepted steps; (b) rerun one short sequence with the C² spline option already in the code; (c) extend the existing §6f clipping machinery from feature times to all nodes, then let the controller stride freely *within* intervals where `f` is smooth in t. If confirmed, this is 5–20× on accepted steps and completes multi-block. If refuted, it cost hours.

**2. Solve the right inner equation: Newton on the branch-death condition, not on ∂P/∂p.** The retired warm Newton solved ∂P/∂p = 0 — an equation with *no root* at a corner; of course it marched off the branch. But the corner is *defined* by an algebraic condition the code already evaluates every solve in order to fire its fallback: the nested sub-root ceases to exist, i.e. g(p, state) = 0 (a fold condition F_r = 0 along F = 0, or a domain-boundary exit). Safeguarded Newton/secant on g converges superlinearly: ~3–4 objective-equivalents to 10⁻¹², versus ~14 golden-section evals to 10⁻³. This wins all three legs at once. Performance: per-member solve drops ~32 → ~20 units. Accuracy: the ε_p ~ τ floor vanishes to machine precision, which retires the J(τ) non-monotonicity and the 2.4× flips *as solver artifacts* — the survival bifurcation remains real but stops being excited by inner-solve noise. Gradient: ∂p*/∂state = −g_state/g_p is analytic, closing the dropped-term seam exactly, and making ∂a/∂u and ∂a/∂x analytic byproducts of the member loop. The fixed-iteration-for-smoothness constraint is satisfied *better*, not worse: converged-to-machine p* is the exact smooth map you measured (slope −1.0004), and the tape gets a custom IFT rule instead of differentiating an iteration.

**3. Interpolate the control *field*, not the members.** §6d's collocation subsampled the measure — fatal, because the skewed heavy atoms must be in any sample. The untried move keeps all M atoms and thins the *expensive solves*: at each RHS eval, run the exact argmax at m synthetic nodes spanning member-feature space at the current (u, s), interpolate p* across members — your own measurement says the field is smooth to the floor — then evaluate each member's g and c at its interpolated control (cost 4.6–15.6 units, not 32). The non-separable u-dependence that killed every a(u) surrogate is untouched: u is a *constant within one eval*, and the nodes are re-solved fresh at every eval; non-separability only bites surrogates reused *across* evals. The <0.5% of members near switch-off or branch boundaries get exact solves — the §6f margin instrument is already the classifier. Stacked with per-macro-interval setup caching (§5 declares it cacheable; §6 never did it), per-eval cost falls from ~32M toward ~4.6M + 32m: roughly 5× at M ≈ 350, m ≈ 25, growing with M. Acid test in J-units on the τ-flip sequence, since interpolation error is precisely the O(ε) control perturbation that flips survivors — stone 2's machine-precision nodes are what make this safe.

**4. Remesh the measure — the 23% spread may not be time-integration error at all.** Two *time*-converged schemes with different member-insertion histories carry different discrete measures; the spread between them is plausibly measure-discretization error wearing an integrator costume. Particle-method practice is unambiguous here: Lagrangian particle sets disorder and must be periodically remeshed onto structured nodes with conservative high-order kernels, or accuracy degrades without bound. And the record contains the evidence *for* this: on a prescribed quadrature-friendly set, m ≈ 15–20 gave <0.5% — remeshing manufactures exactly that set every K steps, un-killing §6d, canonicalizing stone 3's nodes, and systematizing birth/death. The hazard is real: remeshing perturbs near-threshold atoms, so exempt a guard band around the ρ-removal and switch-off margins. Decisive test: run a remeshed pair of schemes and measure whether the inter-scheme spread collapses below 23%. If it does, you've found where the irreproducibility lives.

**5. The error norm is a max over a growing block — change what the controller optimizes.** One log query: does the identity of the component achieving rmax churn step-to-step? If yes, your reject statistics are extreme-value statistics of O(M) independent errors — consistent with eval count ~ M^0.4, with broadband no-colocation, and with the PI failure — and near-zero-weight members are setting the global step. Two levers: an RMS norm (what production codes use) and, sharper, an adjoint-weighted norm — weight member components by |λ_j| with a ρ-floor, keeping full weight for anything inside the survival guard band since at-risk members can be future-heavy. This is the only lever besides stone 1 that reduces *accepted* steps. On top: dual-weighted-residual using the tape you already own — forward at loose tolerance, one adjoint pass, correct J by Σ λ·(local residual), get error bars, and confine validity to a survival branch with flips reported from the inventory. That converts the 10× amplification from a threat into the instrument that tells you which steps mattered.

**6. The Jacobian is an arrow matrix — implicit methods were retired on a false cost premise.** §6b died on dense O(M³) LU; but members are independent given (u, s), so ∂f/∂y is M small diagonal blocks bordered by L+1 dense rows/columns (u and s). Block elimination / Woodbury factors it in O(M). §6h died on Jacobians finite-differenced through the bracketing; stone 2 makes the border blocks analytic. So the entire implicit family is un-retired at linear cost with clean Jacobians — and the honest deployment is *not* global (steps are accuracy-set; implicit won't cut them) but as a near-ceiling mode: switch to Rosenbrock-W with the arrow factorization when h·|λ_est| approaches O(1), alongside stone 1's h ≤ few-node-intervals guard. That is the stability leg, purchased only where stability is actually the binding constraint, i.e. multi-block.

**7. Make birth and death first-class numerical events.** Insertions are C⁰ events in N that §5 itself blames for part of the wall: insert only at step boundaries, initialize the newborn on its quasi-equilibrium given current (u, s) — one inner solve — and ramp ρ in C¹ over a window short relative to member lifetime. Removal becomes J-certified: delete only when tw_j·sup|φ|·ρ_j < ε_J, converting silent survival flips into audited ε's. With stone 2 removing the τ-floor and this removing threshold noise, whatever spread survives stone 4 is *genuine conditioning* — at which point last turn's branch-resolved J (interval plus flip inventory) is the correct deliverable, not a defeat.

**8. The constants, since every stone above multiplies through them.** The member solves are embarrassingly parallel, fixed-iteration by deliberate design — lockstep-perfect for SIMD/GPU batching, 10–100× wall-clock with zero numerics change. Rejection salvage via a continuous extension converts the ~25% discarded stage work into progress. Warm *bracketing* (unlike the retired warm Newton) risks at worst one extra bracket expansion.

**And the pile I'll name so no stone is unturned:** parareal and every coarse-propagator scheme need a cheap faithful surrogate, which the document's central lemma forbids assuming; learned a-surrogates are admissible only with a per-step certificate and exact fallback; moment closures just re-derive §6d through the argmax unless built on remeshed nodes, at which point they *are* stones 3+4; stage-rich stabilized families stay dead because every stage pays O(M); the rejected re-charts stay rejected as measured.

The compounding is multiplicative and the legs separate cleanly: steps (stones 1, 5: plausibly 5–20×), per-eval cost (2, 3, 8: ~4–8×), stability (1's aliasing guard + 6, completing multi-block), accuracy-as-reproducibility (2, 4, 7, then 8's reporting), gradient exactness (2). Run them in falsification order — node-distance log, C² rerun, rmax-identity churn, Newton-on-g prototype, one-snapshot field-interpolation fit against all-M exact solves, remeshed-pair spread — each cheap, each capable of killing its stone in a day. Everything graduates only on the full coupled system with an evolved measure, in J-units, with the τ-flip sequence as the acid test. But the claim "no structure remains" is not what §6 measured. §6 measured that no *integrator swap* helps a problem whose forcing basis, inner equation, measure representation, norm, and observable were all held fixed — and at least the first of those looks, from your own tables, like it's been quietly setting the step size the whole time.
