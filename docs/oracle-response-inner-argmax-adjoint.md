The center of this is one fact about comparison-based bracketing which, once written down, resolves your contract question, explains all three symptoms quantitatively with the numbers you already have, and points at a fix that is likely cheaper than what you run today. Your reverse gradient is mostly computing the right object; your correctness reference is measuring a different one — and the reference, not the tape, is where the framing was wrong. But the tape has one genuine, currently-undefined failure mode (the corner regime) that your 14% measurement is masking.

## What the golden-section search actually computes

Golden section's probe locations are affine functions of the current bracket. The objective `P` influences the output only through the `n ≈ ln((B−A)/τ)/ln φ⁻¹ ≈ 16` binary comparisons. Conditioned on the comparison string ω, the returned midpoint is exactly
`p̂(σ) = A(σ) + γ_ω · (B(σ) − A(σ))`, γ_ω a constant,
where σ is state. The string ω(σ) is piecewise constant; a late comparison's P-difference is `O(|P_pp| τ²) ≈ 8·10⁻⁶` and moves with state at `O(|P_pσ| τ) ≈ 10⁻³`, so comparison cells have width a few×τ in σ. Thus `p̂` is a staircase: uniformly within τ/2 of `p*(σ)`, exactly affine inside each cell with slope `A′ + γ_ω(B′ − A′)` — which contains no information about P whatsoever — and jumping by O(τ) at cell boundaries, where all of the optimum-tracking lives. This is the classic split between uniform convergence and derivative convergence: as τ→0 the value converges to `p*(σ)`, but the almost-everywhere derivative converges to `A′ + γ*(B′−A′)`, permanently missing `(B−A)·dγ*/dσ`, which is carried entirely by the jumps (i.e., by the distributional derivative). Your numbers instantiate this exactly: γ = (1.789−0.290)/2.380 = 0.6298; within-cell slope −0.933 + 0.63(0.571) = −0.573; distributional slope 0.652. The "fixed fraction γ" in your §4 is the whole story in miniature: locally frozen, globally tracking.

Corollaries, each checkable:

First, "FD of the code as run" is not one object — it is a δ/τ-indexed family. δ ≪ cell width measures the within-cell endpoint slope; δ ≫ cell width measures a multi-cell secant, which is the ideal slope plus O(τ/δ) staircase noise. Your τ=10⁻⁹ FD agreeing with the node to four digits is not the surrogate slope converging (γ_ω is essentially τ-invariant, so the within-cell slope stays ≈0.573 at every τ); it is your fixed δ becoming thousands of cells wide. Your δ-plateau methodology at τ=10⁻³ lands inside one cell — and it produces the cleanest-looking plateau you will ever see, because within a cell the code is exactly affine. That is the failure mode of "sweep δ for the plateau" on staircase functions: the artifact branch looks the most authoritative.

Second, the reductio: any θ-component that enters through `P` but not through `A, B` has literal-contract sensitivity exactly zero at sub-cell δ — the comparisons quantize it away. If you have such a component, probe it.

Third, taping through the search (piggyback) reproduces 0.573 identically, since comparisons differentiate as constants. It is the only implementation that satisfies your contract as literally written, and it is the one everyone would agree is meaningless. The founding design belief — that a fixed-tolerance search makes `p̂` smooth and tape-differentiable — is inverted: comparison bracketing is the least differentiable-through inner solver; Newton is the most.

(One flag: your printed endpoint formula yields −0.573 against a quoted FD of +0.573. Presumably a state-sign convention differs between the two measurements, but confirm it — if the surrogate slope is genuinely opposite-signed at this point, everything below only sharpens.)

## Q1 — the correctness contract

Two coherent doctrines exist. (A) Discrete-exact: the gradient equals the derivative of the program as executed — valid and valuable exactly when the program is piecewise-smooth in its inputs at the probing scale. This is why your frozen-schedule discrete adjoint of the RK loop is right. (B) Consistent: the gradient converges to the derivative of the underlying solution map at the same rate as the value, computed by differentiating the converged condition rather than the solver's path — the standard for inner iterative solves (Gilbert 1992; Christianson's reverse accumulation of fixed points; Griewank–Walther's treatment of iterative processes). Doctrine (A) is unavailable here in any useful sense, because the program's derivative depends on the probing scale, and the sub-cell derivative has a validity radius of O(τ): it predicts `J(θ+Δθ)` only for `|Δθ| ≲ a few·τ`, which no consumer of a gradient — optimizer, calibrator, UQ — respects. The surrogate slope has no user.

Note that you have already chosen doctrine (B) once without naming it: freezing the step schedule deliberately excludes the adaptive controller's genuine, nonsmooth θ-dependence from both the tape and the reference. The inner search is the same decision one level down; you took the opposite branch there.

The restated contract: the AD gradient must (i) match FD on a frozen schedule at tight inner tolerance, fixed δ, to integrator tolerance — this is the anchor; and (ii) at production τ, sit at the envelope minimum of the FD family, `|AD − FD_δ| ≤ C(δ² + τ/δ)`, whose floor is O(τ^{2/3}) at δ* ~ τ^{1/3} — with the explicit rule that a clean sub-cell plateau whose value is τ-invariant while the envelope moves is the signature of an inner-solve artifact, not of the gradient. Under this contract, 0.573 stops being a reference and becomes a diagnostic.

## Q3 — the two geometries and the tape node

The right frame is parametric optimization (Fiacco; Bonnans–Shapiro): `p*(σ)` is a selection among critical manifolds — interior stationarity `P_p = 0`; the branch-fold `F = 0` (the sub-solve's root-loss condition); the box faces `p = A, B` (never active). Off transition surfaces it is smooth on each manifold with the corresponding IFT derivative; at transitions it is at best piecewise-C¹. That nonsmoothness is intrinsic to the model, so asking for a differentiable regime detector is asking for the impossible and the unnecessary. What is required is correct selection off the boundary, bounded one-sided derivatives at it, and an event term only if the selection jumps.

Reject representation (1) in both regimes, not just the corner. The envelope theorem zeroes `(∂P/∂p)·p*′` inside `dP/dσ`; it says nothing about `(∂c/∂p)·p*′` or `(∂g/∂p)·p*′`, and your own O(τ)-vs-O(τ²) asymmetry measurement is precisely the statement that `c, g` are non-stationary in `p`. Freezing `p*` inside `c` and `g` drops an O(1) term even at a perfect interior optimum. Your §2.2 claim that (1) is "correct if the operating point is a true stationary interior optimum" is false as stated.

The shipped node (2) has a live hazard in the corner regime beyond being the wrong condition: the returned midpoint sits within τ/2 of the wall on either side. On the shelf side, `|P_p| ≈ 0` — a flat shelf mimics stationarity, so a midpoint `|P_p|` threshold will misclassify — and `−P_pσ/P_pp` there divides by shelf curvature ≈ 0. In corner states your current gradient is not 14% off; it is undefined-in-principle and numerically arbitrary, with severity set by how much trajectory time lives there. Measure that first. The robust selector is discrete and free: read the sub-solve's branch indicator at the two endpoints of the final bracket. Same branch on both ⇒ interior manifold, use `−P_pσ/P_pp`. Different branches ⇒ the fold is inside the bracket ⇒ corner manifold, use `−F_σ/F_p` on the root-loss residual (exposable from the same sub-solve that gives you `P_p` in closed form). Anything else ⇒ the exits you have never observed, kept as guarded fallbacks.

Transitions: instrument continuity of `p̂` across regime flips along trajectories. If the interior stationary point slides into the wall (continuous, kink only), piecewise-IFT selection is complete — crossings are measure-zero in time, `dJ/dθ` exists, no adjoint jump condition is needed. If instead the global argmax swaps between separated candidates at a value tie, `p*` jumps: that is an event needing the standard switching-time sensitivity (transversal crossing; adjoint jump proportional to the RHS jump times `dt_event/dθ`) — and note the loose search dithers at value ties, since comparisons decide by O(τ²) P-differences, so ties are also a forward-noise hotspot. The same event logic applies to your switch-off boundary if `c` jumps rather than vanishing there: its <0.5% incidence is concentrated exactly on the bifurcating trajectories where you measured the 2.4×, so gradients near those either need the term or the flagged fix below.

## Q2 — the single move

Terminal polish. Keep bracketing for what it is good at — global localization and branch detection: contract to width ~10⁻¹(B−A) (≈5 comparisons), read the branch flags at the bracket ends, then take 3–4 safeguarded Newton (or exact-`P_p` secant) steps on the active condition — `P_p = 0` interior, `F = 0` at the fold — confined to the bracket, down to `|p̂ − p*| ~ 10⁻¹²`·scale. Cost ≈ 9–13 objective-equivalents versus ≈16 today; the closed-form `P_p` you already possess and do not use is the load-bearing free asset.

This discharges all three symptoms by the same mechanism, because they are one mechanism. Stability: the h-independent estimator floor is the stage-incoherent O(τ)·|∂(c,g)/∂p| staircase ripple, sampled as fast `u` sweeps state across few-τ cells many times per step; polish collapses its amplitude ~9 decades, the controller re-acquires genuine h⁵ truncation, the 27–35% rejection storm and the `h_min` wall go — with the falsifiable side-prediction that corner-regime trajectories improve most, since there even `P` feels the ripple at O(τ) rather than O(τ²). Accuracy: the O(τ) operating-point bias whose τ-dependent sign is what flips your marginal unit — the non-monotone `J(τ)` — vanishes; you land on the τ≤10⁻⁶ converged branch with six digits of margin. Correctness: `p̂(σ)` is now smooth, and the IFT node, tape-through-the-polish (a differentiated Newton step at a converged root returns the IFT derivative plus O(residual)), and FD-as-run at any sane δ all coincide. The contract dispute dissolves rather than being adjudicated: value and derivative finally describe one object.

Alternatives are dominated. Tightening golden section to 10⁻⁶ needs ~30 comparisons (≈2× RHS cost) and still leaves a staircase at the new amplitude. Smoothed argmax relaxations move the value (flag territory anyway) and add their own bias for nothing, given exact `P_p`. Your refuted `ṗ = kP_p` flow fails for a reason the polish does not share: it was the unprojected dynamic version; the bracket is the projection.

Separability: yes, but asymmetric. The gradient can be fixed alone (node + branch-flag selection). The forward floor, the `J(τ)` bifurcation, and tie-dithering live in the values, and no derivative-side construction touches them. Nor can any derivative-side correction restore literal value–derivative consistency: holding the actual residual `r = P_p(p̂)` fixed and differentiating `P_p = r` reproduces the same IFT formula — the true `p̂(σ)` is a staircase, not a nearby smooth manifold, so the O(τ) mismatch is the irreducible price of the mixed mode.

## Q4 — intrinsic vs representational, and shipping shape

Representational, with a small intrinsic core. The pathology is created by finishing the solve with comparisons and is removable by finishing it with a rootfind; afterwards even naive taping is correct. What remains intrinsic: (i) piecewise-C¹ behavior of `p*(σ)` at regime boundaries — a model property, handled by selection plus event terms if the selection jumps; (ii) genuine nonsmoothness of `J_ideal(θ)` across survival bifurcations, where a one-sided gradient is the true object; (iii) if the default value stays loose, an O(τ) value–derivative offset amplified by at most `‖(I−T′)⁻¹‖ ≈ 5–22` — call it O(10τ) relative gradient bias from linearizing the adjoint around the contaminated trajectory, plus unbounded error on the rare bifurcating runs, which need the flagged forward for meaningful gradients at all.

Loose value + ideal derivative is legitimate, documented — it is the standard posture of adjoint codes wrapping inexact inner solves ("differentiate the converged condition, validate at matched tolerances"). The pair with no defensible user is loose value + surrogate derivative. So: default = legacy forward bit-identical, gradient node upgraded to branch-flag selection with fold-IFT (this fixes the undefined corner behavior without moving one bit of the forward, and documents the O(10τ) offset). Flag = polished solve, value and gradient coherent — and since it is likely cheaper per RHS and far cheaper per run once the controller recovers, treat the flag as the candidate next default, not a concession. Forbidden = tape-through-search, which uniquely satisfies the literal contract and is uniquely meaningless. Do not chase the 0.82× against the loose FD; it is not a closable gap and should not close.

## Decisive experiments (using your three instruments)

1. Scale test. Fix δ at the production plateau; sweep τ. FD should sit on the 0.573 branch until a few·τ falls below δ, then jump to 0.652. One plot retires the contract question empirically.
2. Anchor. AD vs FD on a frozen tight-τ schedule, agreement to integrator tolerance = the new correctness bar. Then AD-on-loose-forward vs that anchor isolates the O(10τ) contamination and corner incidence — an attribution ladder, not one number.
3. Corner census. Per-call log of branch flags and `|P_p(p̂)|`; expect a bimodal histogram (≲`|P_pp|τ/2 ≈ 4·10⁻³` vs ≈8.8). Inspect shipped-node outputs inside the corner population for the divide-by-shelf-curvature blowups.
4. Transition continuity. `p̂` across regime flips: O(τ) (kink; selection suffices) vs O(1) (event terms required).
5. Polish A/B behind the flag. Rejection rate, h floor, `J` against the 5.87·10⁻⁸ branch, gradient vs anchor, wall-clock — all four symptoms in one run.
6. Reductio probe. If any θ-component enters `P` but not `(A,B)`: its sub-cell FD is ~0 — the cleanest demonstration that the literal contract points at an artifact.

## Load-bearing vs incidental

Load-bearing: the comparison-based terminal phase (the staircase), the locally-frozen/globally-tracking γ, the unused exact `P_p`, the regime-dependent optimality condition, the O(τ)/O(τ²) asymmetry, the moving endpoints. Incidental to these three symptoms: the q≈16 self-loss and its never-engaging clamp, the one-way transfer, the spline knots (expect at most knot-localized order reduction to become visible once the broadband floor lifts), the skewed ρ, the insertion schedule (frozen consistently on both sides of your check — which, note, is already doctrine (B)). The resolvent bound is the amplifier and the guarantee: a per-unit fix propagates ~10× and stops. Post-polish, the next binding accuracy limit is one already on your list: the unit schedule resolves `x(t)`, not the quadrature `∫c·ρ` — if the ~23% inter-scheme spread survives the polish, that is where it lives.
