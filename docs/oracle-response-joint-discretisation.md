# Oracle response — three grids on one axis

The measurements say the discretisation is unbalanced by four to five orders under sustained forcing (D1), and the reason is not a trade-off: the time grid is not chosen by accuracy at all. 77% of its steps are set by the stability of a component that costs 14% of `f` (D8, D9), so `e_t` is a by-product that cannot be traded against anything. Under impulsive forcing the apparent coupling between the grids (D4) is an artifact of adaptive placement plus restarts, not a property of the errors. Once `𝒢_t` is held fixed while `𝒢_b` changes, the errors separate; once the stability constraint leaves the small block, `N_t` becomes a design variable; and then a two-line Lagrangian, not a DWR machine, sets the split — at error shares proportional to convergence order, roughly `e_b ≈ 2 e_t`, not equidistribution. The adjoint you already compute supplies the *where* and the *certificate*; the *how much* is a calibrated power law.

## 4.1 The joint design

**The frame.** Cost is bilinear (D9): `C ≈ N_t (αM + β)` with `β/(αM) ≈ 0.16` at `M = 88`. Errors are power laws: `e_b = A M^{−p}` (`p = 2` now), `e_t = B N_t^{−q}` (`q = 5` once steps are accuracy-set and the problem is smooth on each step). Minimise `C` subject to `e_b + e_t = ε`. Stationarity gives

`p · e_b · (1 + β/(αM)) = q · e_t`,

so `e_b/e_t = q/(p(1 + β/αM)) ≈ 5/2.3 ≈ 2.2`. The principle is equal marginal error reduction per unit cost; for power laws that puts error shares in proportion to order — the lower-order discretisation, which is more expensive per digit, carries more of the error. Equidistribution is right *within* a grid (across nodes, across steps) and wrong *between* grids of different order. With a fourth-order label quadrature (4.6) the ratio drops to `e_b ≈ 1.1 e_t`.

**Where you are.** `e_b/e_t ≈ 3.6·10⁴` under sustained forcing — not because `M` is hard to refine (D3: nearly free) but because `e_t` is four orders too small for free. So the first move in the joint design is not to add nodes; it is to make `N_t` a variable (4.4b). Worked at a target `ε = 1e−3`: `e_b ≈ 7e−4` (≈100 nodes by D3), `e_t ≈ 3e−4`, which under sustained forcing is a time grid at the slow scale — tens to low hundreds of steps against 530. Under impulsive forcing `N_t` has a floor of a few steps per impulse that no tolerance removes; there `M` is the only free variable and the balance degenerates to "set `M` from the target".

**DWR.** Right as estimator, wrong as allocator, and cheaper than it looks. One adjoint gives three separated contributions:

- time: `E_t = Σ_n λ_nᵀ e_n`, embedded estimate as residual, cotangent of the state as weight (Cao–Petzold);
- node grid: `E_b = Σ_n h_n λ_{u,n}ᵀ δa_n + δJ_direct`, where `δa_n` is the quadrature error of `a_ℓ` at step `n`. Estimate it without re-running: keep the per-member summands `ρ_j c_ℓ(ξ_j,u,p_j)` at each accepted step (~2 MB), and difference the rule you use against a higher one on the same samples — a cubic interpolant across the label coordinate to midpoints, or on the dyadic grid simply delete-every-other-node. The weight is the 5-component cotangent of `u`, which you have. The same surplus on `J`'s own summand gives the direct term;
- forcing: `E_f = ∫ λ_{u_1}(t) δs(t) dt` — not an error but the bias of any declared change to the reconstruction, computable before you make it.

What the characteristic method does not naturally supply: an interpolation operator across members — a topology on the label axis, available precisely because `b` is ordered and never folds, which is the second reason the label coordinate is the right one — plus storage of summands, plus a continuous extension if you prefer a defect residual to the embedded difference. Honest cost: arithmetic and a few megabytes; the reverse pass is already paid. Honest caveats: it is first-order and needs the asymptotic regime, so it is meaningless at `tol = 1e−4` under impulsive forcing until the events and kinks are fixed; signed cancellation means marking uses `|η_j|`; and its effectivity must be checked against a level difference at fixed `𝒢_t`. The standard alternative for a coupled pair with one target — and what I would use for the *design* — is the calibrated power-law balance above, verified by two-level differences on nested grids with the other grid frozen. DWR marks, Richardson certifies, the Lagrangian allocates.

## 4.2 Nesting

`𝒢_b ⊂ 𝒢_t`: keep. Creation is a jump in `f`; a step boundary is required and 88 are cheap.

`𝒢_f ⊂ 𝒢_t`: not as a rule — conditionally. A knot matters only where the reconstruction has a nonzero jump, i.e. where `s ≠ 0` on at least one side. Under mostly-zero forcing that is the knots inside and bounding impulses, two or three per event; aligning to them is what removes the impulsive regime's order loss, at negligible count. Between events `s ≡ 0`, the `C¹` interpolant is identically zero, and there is no kink. Under smooth sustained forcing the 1825 knots carry jumps of `O(Δ_f² s⁽⁴⁾)` and the measured total time error is `6.6e−8` (D1): aligning buys nothing and costs 3.4×.

The kink term is `O(h³ [s''])` per straddling step against a smooth `O(h⁶)`; it dominates for *small* `h`, i.e. when time accuracy is pushed. The balance says not to push it — `e_t ≈ 3e−4` puts `h` at weeks under sustained forcing, where the smooth term wins. So a `C⁴` reconstruction is a contingency, triggered only if the certificate shows kink-dominated time error at the balanced tolerance. Coarsening the reconstruction is never right: it trades data fidelity for a cost you do not have, and its bias `∫λ_{u_1}δs` is computable, so you would know exactly what you paid for nothing. If you do reconstruct: cell integrals exact, positivity, `C⁴`; kernel-smoothing of the piecewise-constant rate is the two-line version.

What nesting buys beyond alignment: (i) separability — with `𝒢_t` frozen across `𝒢_b` levels and `𝒢_b` frozen across `h/2` halvings, each level difference isolates one error, and D4's inseparability disappears; (ii) a free hierarchical estimator on the dyadic `𝒢_b` — deleting every other node from the quadrature (not from the run) is a coarse-level rule on the same integrand, which is the node residual DWR needs; (iii) a global time-error estimate by step doubling on the nested `h/2` grid at ≈2.1 adaptive-run-equivalents (D10's 30% saving), separable from the node error because the creation restarts are identical; (iv) reuse — refinement runs become replays without rejections, so D6's seven to ten runs become cheaper and, more importantly, clean. Richardson *extrapolation* for accuracy — no; kinks and events break the expansion. For *estimation* — yes.

## 4.3 The right indicator

The delete-a-node difference is a local estimate of quadrature error in the member sum. It is not weighted by what `J` does with that error (the feedback through `u`, the direct moment), it ignores the time coupling, and at `tol = 1e−4` under impulsive forcing it is measured through re-rolled time error (D4) — which is where the non-monotonicity comes from. Under sustained forcing it over-predicts 4× (0.99% against 0.242%), a normal ratio for an unweighted local estimate.

Two instruments, two jobs. *Marking*: the adjoint-weighted node surplus `η_j = |Σ_n h_n λ_{u,n}ᵀ δa_{n,j}| + |δJ_j|`. It finds curvature times sensitivity, and it finds kinks in `b` where adjacent members sit on different branches — plausibly D5's two non-monotone refinements, since bisecting near a kink in the integrand does raise a local indicator. *Stopping*: the change in `J` across levels at fixed `𝒢_t`, order-verified. For `O(Δb²)` under uniform bisection `e_k ≈ (4/3)(J_k − J_{k+1})` and `e_{k+1} ≈ e_k/4`; under adaptive bisection the factor is not 4, so require the observed ratio of successive differences to sit in a band (2–8) before believing the estimate, and require the DWR sum's effectivity against it to sit in 0.5–2. Stop when the estimated error of the *current* level is below its share of the budget, both checks pass, and the time error is independently certified at ≤ ⅓ of the node error. No indicator bounds `J` in the strict sense; asymptotic estimate plus order verification is what the field means by certified, and the current rule has neither.

At fixed `𝒢_t` the refinement is a sequence of replays and the indicator should become monotone. If it does not, the integrand has kinks in `b` at branch boundaries; there the label quadrature is capped at `O(Δb²)` and local bisection is all that is available.

## 4.4 Does a designed discretisation put other methods on the frontier?

(a) Multirate's premise is that the fast subsystem is cheap to advance given interpolated slow variables. Here the fast RHS is `s − κu^q + transfer − a_ℓ(x,u)`, and `a_ℓ` is both the expensive term (D9) and a function of the fast variable: every fast substep with fresh `u` needs a member loop, so the MRI-GARK cost model fails unless `a_ℓ(x_n, ·)` is replaced over the macro-step by something cheap in `u` — its linearisation `a(x_n,u_n) + ∂a/∂u (u − u_n)`, `L×L`, once per macro step, from the IFT derivatives you compute for the adjoint anyway. But a fast substepper on a 5-component linearised system with the chain implicit is, up to bookkeeping, a Rosenbrock stage with Jacobian `∂b/∂u − ∂a/∂u`. Multirate with linearised coupling collapses into linearly-implicit treatment of the small block, so the minimal form is not multirate. If you built MRI-GARK anyway: order 3–4 is available; holding `x` over a macro step costs `O(H ‖∂a/∂x‖ ‖ẋ‖)` unless slow stages are interpolated (which MRI-GARK does); and you would still pay the member loop on every fast step for `a_ℓ`, which is the whole cost.

(b) IMEX/partitioned at one step size. What decides: the stiffness lives in a cheap, separable term — `−κu^q` and the transfer, five components, analytic bidiagonal Jacobian, and D8 says the terminus's own loss is the binding rate — not in the expensive coupling; the coupling is slow in `u`; and an L-stable stage on a non-stiff regime is harmless, so IMEX needs no regime detection where multirate would need a regime-dependent step ratio. Minimal forms: an ARK IMEX pair (Kennedy–Carpenter ARK3(2) or ARK4(3), embedded estimate free) with the chain implicit and everything else explicit, or a Rosenbrock-W stage with the 5×5 Jacobian and zero elsewhere (W-methods keep order under an inexact Jacobian; add `∂a/∂u` if the shut-off near `u_min` is steep). The implicit stage is forward substitution down the chain with a scalar Newton per layer; the discrete adjoint is a 5×5 transposed solve per stage (Sandu's Rosenbrock adjoints, or the IFT on the ARK stage equation). Expected effect: the sustained regime moves from `h ≈ 3.5/|λ|` to the slow accuracy step — an order of magnitude in `N_t` — and the frozen grid's failure mode goes away, since D10's failure is stability. A bonus: an implicit stage on a monotone decay preserves `u > 0` and holds `u` above `u_min` far more robustly than explicit stages, removing a class of validity rejections. In the impulsive regime the gain is smaller because `N_t` is event-bound.

(c) With a fixed, known grid: Richardson for global error *estimation* (robust enough; not for extrapolating accuracy); step-doubling certificates; designed refinement patterns after known impulses, replacing discovery by rejection — D7 alone is 17–27% of evaluations, D10 another 30%; and warm-starting inner solves from the previous `θ`'s replay at the same times, a small real saving that is smooth on the tape because the seed is a constant. SDC offers order flexibility you cannot use — kinks cap the order of a straddling step at two — at a cost proportional to sweeps times the member loop: off the frontier. Parareal/PFASST buy wall-clock, not RHS count, and stiff-plus-switching is where they are brittle: off by the metric. Exponential/Lawson integrators need a linear stiff part; `u^q` makes them exponential-Rosenbrock, re-linearised per step — well understood, 5×5 `φ`-functions trivial, adjoint fine — but no better than Rosenbrock for a self-limiting nonlinearity whose Jacobian changes by orders within one transient. The exact scalar drainage flow `u = (u₀^{1−q} + (q−1)κt)^{1/(1−q)}` gives a positivity-preserving, stability-free Strang splitting, but it is second order and the transfer term makes the chain non-closed-form, so the implicit chain is simpler. Verdict: IMEX-ARK, Richardson estimation, designed patterns. Nothing else is both well understood here and on the frontier.

## 4.5 Reuse across parameters

Yes — one designed discretisation per forcing realisation, reused across `θ`, is the correct answer, and it is the standard discretise-then-optimise setting: the optimiser sees a smooth `J_h(θ)` (D11), and the discretisation error is a fixed bias controlled by refinement at the optimum. Certify in two tiers. Per `θ`, during the replay: `Σ_n λ_nᵀ e_n` (the embedded difference is free in a replay; the cotangents come from the reverse pass you run anyway), the stability check `h_n ρ(J_u) ≤ c` until IMEX makes it moot, and the branch census — a qualitative change from population-wide to member-wise crossings is the one thing a local estimate can miss. Periodically — box corners, each recapture, the final optimum — step doubling on the nested `h/2` grid for a Richardson global estimate, and a fixed-`𝒢_t` node level difference. Recapture on failure. Nothing stronger is available, and nothing stronger is needed.

Designed against captured: the difference is structural for forcing- and stability-driven features and incidental for state-driven ones. A designed grid encodes the impulse schedule, which is `θ`-independent, and a stability margin computed for the box (`min` over the box of `c/ρ(J_u)`), so it covers the box by construction; a captured grid encodes `θ₀`'s timing and needs `s` — D10's `s = 2` is paying for the stability margin. For pool arrivals and switching crossings both fail alike when the event moves; the remedy is upstream reformulation, after which the grid's only job is accuracy on smooth dynamics and its reuse region is essentially the box — the non-stiff variant of the previous round replaying at `s = 1` over ±100% is the evidence.

Smallest set of changes, in order:

1. Freeze `𝒢_t` whenever `𝒢_b` changes and vice versa. Zero machinery; restores separability and monotone indicators (D4, D5).
2. Replace the node-grid stopping rule with fixed-`𝒢_t` level differences plus an order check; mark by the `λ_u`-weighted surplus; set the budget from the calibration target, not `2e−2`.
3. Raise the label quadrature's order where the integrand is smooth — a spline or Gregory rule on the fixed `b` grid, weights constant on the tape, care at the creation boundary. Free, and it changes the balance.
4. IMEX or Rosenbrock-W on the chain, with its 5×5 adjoint. The one piece of work; it makes `N_t` a variable and removes D10's failure mode.
5. Design `𝒢_t` per forcing realisation: aligned to impulse boundaries, fixed counts per leg, geometric refinement after impulses, no rejections; balance `M` and `tol` by the Lagrangian; certify as in 4.5.
6. Reformulate the constrained pool and `u_min`, removing the remaining quarter of rejections (D7) and the `θ`-dependent placement.
7. `C⁴` reconstruction only if the certificate says the time error is kink-dominated.

Not asked:

1. **The target.** Nothing in D1–D12 says what accuracy the calibration needs in `J` or in `dJ/dθ`; without it the balance is undefined. At any target ≥ `1e−3`, the entire time-integration effort under sustained forcing is wasted by four orders.
2. **The quadrature rule's order.** `O(Δb²)` on the label coordinate is a choice, not a property; where the population is on one branch a fourth-order rule cuts `e_b` by an order at `M = 88` for nothing. It is the largest free lever on the dominant error.
3. **`J`'s own time accumulation.** If the space–time moment is accumulated by a low-order rule over steps rather than as a state inside the pair, that is the dominant time error and the `O(h⁵)` in 4.1 is fiction.
4. **The gradient's discretisation error.** D11 establishes consistency, not accuracy; measure the gradient's change across node levels at fixed `𝒢_t`, and re-check the optimum on a refined pair of grids.
5. **The forcing adjoint** `λ_{u_1}(t)` as a design tool: it says which impulses `J` cares about (refine there, not everywhere) and prices any reconstruction change before you make it.
6. **The transported coordinate** is 4× less accurate and has no adjoint; retire it, or keep it only as an independent check on the quadrature.
7. **Warm starts across replays** at nearby `θ` — the dominant cost is inner solves, and the previous replay's iterates at the same times are good seeds.
