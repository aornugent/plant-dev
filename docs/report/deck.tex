\begin{frame}{An evolutionary analysis needs fitness and its derivatives, many times over}
  \small
  regnans finds and classifies singular strategies from plant runs of three kinds.
  \medskip

  \begin{tikzpicture}[box/.style={draw=accent, rounded corners, align=center,
      minimum height=1.25cm, text width=3.6cm, font=\footnotesize}, >=Stealth]
    \node[box] (r) {\textbf{Resident run}\\ the stand at traits $\theta$\\ and its fitness $J$};
    \node[box, right=0.8cm of r] (i) {\textbf{Invader runs}\\ a rare strategy $\theta'$ in the\\ environment the resident made,\\ and its fitness $J'$};
    \node[box, right=0.8cm of i] (d) {\textbf{Derivatives}\\ $d\ln J/d\ln\theta$ for each trait\\ (the selection gradient)\\ and its curvature};
    \draw[->] (r) -- (i); \draw[->] (i) -- (d);
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \medskip

  \begin{itemize}
    \item One singular strategy in one trait takes about \textbf{84 such runs}. A resident run on a
      40-year record takes about 90 s.
    \item Until now plant returned $J$ alone. Each derivative was a central difference, two runs
      per parameter, so a TF24 gradient over its 49 parameters cost about \textbf{98 runs}.
    \item An invader ran with its own time steps, so $J'$ moved in small jumps as $\theta'$
      moved, and differences of $J'$ inherited the jumps.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{Run counts: docs/design-grid-controller.md, ``Where the arc starts''.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}[fragile]{The whole analysis step, end to end, in plant's R interface}

p$node_schedule_times <- list(seq(0, 39.6, length.out = 108))  # 108 introductions over 40 years

base <- control_tf24(3e-5, Control(node_density_in_birth_date = TRUE,
                                   ode_split_sign_changes = TRUE))   # slides 8, 9, 10
pilot <- run_scm(p54, env, control_tf24(1e-3), record_trajectory = TRUE)  # p at 54 introductions
ctrl <- control_window(pilot, list(lma_half, lma_double), base = base)  # slide 11

scm  <- run_scm(p, env, ctrl, record_trajectory = TRUE)

  The same answers by brute force take 886 s. Every function above is on the branches; each
  later slide explains one line.
  \src{Timed alone, one run each: docs/measurements/headline/.}

\begin{frame}{Where a cohort's net production changes sign inside a step, its step is cut there}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm, >=Stealth]
        \draw[->, quiet] (0,0) -- (5,0) node[right, black, font=\footnotesize] {$t$};
        \draw[->, quiet] (0,-0.9) -- (0,1.4) node[above, black, font=\footnotesize] {a cohort's net production};
        \draw[thick, accent] (0,1.1) .. controls (1.5,0.9) and (2.2,0.3) .. (2.7,0)
                             .. controls (3.1,-0.3) and (4,-0.6) .. (4.8,-0.7);
        \draw[dashed, quiet] (0.6,-0.9) -- (0.6,1.3); \draw[dashed, quiet] (4.3,-0.9) -- (4.3,1.3);
        \node[font=\scriptsize, below] at (0.6,-0.9) {$t_n$};
        \node[font=\scriptsize, below] at (4.3,-0.9) {$t_{n+1}$};
        \draw[warm, thick] (2.7,-0.9) -- (2.7,1.3);
        \node[font=\scriptsize, warm, below] at (2.7,-0.9) {cut at $t^*$};
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      \small
      TF24 grows a cohort by the positive part of its net production, so the cohort's rate has a
      corner where net production crosses zero. A Runge--Kutta step integrates a smooth rate well
      and a corner badly. As $\theta$ moves, the corner slides past the step's internal stages, and
      $J$ moves in small jumps: the gradient becomes a staircase.
      \medskip

      With \texttt{ode\_split\_sign\_changes = TRUE}, plant finds the crossing on the step's own
      interpolant and integrates that cohort in two pieces, cut there. The other cohorts and the
      soil keep the whole step.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \medskip
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\ln J$ lies 3--13$\times$ nearer the converged answer wherever cohorts cross zero.
    \item A 5\% tolerance change moves no gradient entry by more than $\varepsilon/6$; without the
      cut it moved entries by up to 0.52$\varepsilon$. The cut costs 5--9\% of a gradient run.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{\texttt{PLANT-100}--\texttt{103}, odelia\#53--54, phylloptim\#17; docs/design-sign-changes.md.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{\texttt{control\_tf24()} names TF24's step settings once, each for a measured reason}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.27\textwidth}L{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    setting & why \\
    \midrule
    relative tolerance \texttt{tol}, absolute at $10^{-4}\,$\texttt{tol}
      & Tied this way, $J$'s error stays within about half of \texttt{tol}. With plant's default

    soil stepped alone below a 10\% share
      & The next slide. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}

      so the light field stepped at every node. Its error fell with more nodes, but at no fixed
      rate.
      \medskip

    \item Measured ratios are 3.5--4.0. The half-introduction run reports 0.87--0.99 of the true

  \end{itemize}

    \item At 108 introductions the remaining error comes from the nodes. The half-introduction run
      reports it, so a run can tell that it needs more introductions.
    \item On the episodic record 108 introductions suffice: every quantity lies within
      0.40$\varepsilon$ of brute force's.
    \item No run, invader or gradient fails on any of the five records.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{docs/measurements/headline/prereg.txt, amendment.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What changes for code that uses plant today}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Every new behaviour sits behind a \texttt{Control} field or a new function, and is off
      by default. With it off, results are bit-identical, apart from the two moves below.
    \item The height coordinate, plant's default, does not move.
    \item \textbf{FF16 on the birth-date coordinate moves}, because the spread changes how leaf
      area enters the light field. The one-species anchor goes from 17.14 to 18.01, and the
      two-species test from 12.05 and 16.59 to 12.07 and 16.76. At the default schedule FF16's
      error is now 9--25$\times$ larger. It falls at the square law, so a half-introduction run
      estimates it. Each move was registered before the references were blessed again.
    \item TF24's model version goes to v13. Its numbers change with the gradient pass, a storage
      pool offset, and a newborn's height found to roundoff.
    \item Under plant's default \texttt{Control()}, TF24's $J$ on long drought is 0.45 on
      \texttt{develop}, against 12.75 converged. On this work's code the same defaults give 0.18,
      so the default settings, more than the model, carry that gap.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Known gaps}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Episodic rain needs finer introductions} for the error estimate to hold. Its
      error falls at the square law only once the spacing is below its dry spells (median 31
      days), past 429 introductions.
    \item \textbf{Under constant rain the gradients move by up to 3$\varepsilon$} with where the
      later introductions sit; only the first year is graded finer.
    \item \textbf{A gradient blow-up with the soil stepped with the plants at a loose tolerance.}
      At tolerance 1e-3, with the soil's tolerance ten times looser and no inner steps, the
      resident's elasticities come out near $10^{50}$ while $J$ is right, and nothing refuses.
      Stepping the soil alone, now the preset's default, removes it at every tolerance tried. Not
      yet root-caused.
    \item \textbf{How far one resident's recorded steps can serve} a moved resident or a distant
      invader is unmeasured; \texttt{diagnose\_scm()} reports the distance without a bound.
    \item The branches sit on the fork's \texttt{develop} with odelia 0.5.0, and need a rebase
      onto upstream \texttt{develop} (odelia 0.6.2) before PRs open there.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
