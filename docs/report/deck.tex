          \addplot[only marks, mark=*, warm, mark size=2pt] coordinates

        \end{axis}

      \illus
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \small
      On the birth-date coordinate each cohort stands for the seedlings born between it and the
      next cohort. Its density was the birth rate times the establishment probability at its own
      birth date (the orange points), and integrals over birth date were trapezia through those

      TF24's establishment rises after rain and falls as the soil dries, within days. Cohorts

      were.
    \end{column}

\begin{frame}{How it works: each cohort carries the establishment of its interval as a state}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.5\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}
        \begin{axis}[width=\linewidth, height=4.4cm, xmin=0, xmax=2, ymin=0, ymax=1.1,
            xlabel={birth date (years)}, ylabel={establishment probability},
            xtick={0,0.5,...,2}, yticklabels={}, every axis label/.style={font=\footnotesize},
            ticklabel style={font=\footnotesize}, axis y line*=left, axis x line*=bottom]
          \addplot[fill=wash, draw=none, domain=0.74:1.11, samples=200]
            {0.05 + 0.9*exp(-((x-0.18)/0.03)^2) + 0.7*exp(-((x-0.71)/0.025)^2) + 0.85*exp(-((x-1.34)/0.035)^2) + 0.5*exp(-((x-1.83)/0.02)^2)} \closedcycle;
          \addplot[accent, thick, domain=0:2, samples=400]
            {0.05 + 0.9*exp(-((x-0.18)/0.03)^2) + 0.7*exp(-((x-0.71)/0.025)^2) + 0.85*exp(-((x-1.34)/0.035)^2) + 0.5*exp(-((x-1.83)/0.02)^2)};
          \draw[warm, thick] (axis cs:0.74,0) -- (axis cs:0.74,1.05);
          \draw[warm, thick] (axis cs:1.11,0) -- (axis cs:1.11,1.05);
          \node[font=\scriptsize, warm, anchor=south] at (axis cs:0.925,0.9) {one interval};
        \end{axis}
      \end{tikzpicture}
      \illus
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.48\textwidth}
      \small
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item While a cohort is the newest, it integrates the establishment probability over its
          interval as an ODE state, together with that integral's first moment. The solver's own
          steps resolve each pulse.
        \item When the next cohort is introduced, the interval closes and its integrals stop
          changing.
        \item An integral over birth date weights each cohort by establishment integrated
          against its hat function. Only the rest of the integrand, which is smooth, is
          interpolated between cohorts.
      \end{enumerate}
      A cohort's density is then no longer a state of its own.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The result: establishment is exact, whatever the introductions}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The establishment term is integrated to the solver's tolerance, however the cohorts
      fall relative to the rain. What remains of the node error comes from the smooth part
      of the integrand.
    \item Before, a run's error estimate from its nodes' values saw only 4--5\% of the true
      error: seedlings born into a closed establishment window made steps between nodes
      that no nodal value shows. Parts 8 and 9 rely on that cause being gone.
    \item The height coordinate is unchanged, and K93, which establishes every seed, gives the
      same answers to ten digits.
    \item On this coordinate \texttt{Species\$log\_densities\_state} is removed and
      \texttt{refine\_schedule()} raises an error.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{plant\#94; docs/measurements/field-adjoint-map.md.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Part 2 of 9: every derivative from one reverse pass}
  \roadmap{2}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The problem: a gradient by differences costs two runs per parameter}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A central difference runs the stand at $\theta_j(1 \pm h)$ for each parameter $j$. TF24
      has 49 differentiable parameters, so one gradient costs about \textbf{98 runs}.
    \item An analysis needs a gradient for the resident and one for each invader, at every
      step of its search.
    \item Where moving a trait moves an event, such as a cohort reaching maturity between two
      steps, the difference does not converge as $h$ shrinks.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How it works: the run records its steps, and one pass runs back over them}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth, x=1cm, y=1cm]
    \foreach \i in {0,...,9} \fill[accent] (\i*1.2, 0) circle (2pt);
    \draw[->, thick, accent] (0,0.35) -- (10.8,0.35)
      node[midway, above, font=\footnotesize, black] {\texttt{run\_scm(..., record\_trajectory = TRUE)} stores the state at every accepted step};
    \draw[<-, thick, warm] (0,-0.35) -- (10.8,-0.35)
      node[midway, below, font=\footnotesize, black] {\texttt{stand\_gradient()} runs back over the stored steps, last to first};
    \node[font=\footnotesize, left] at (-0.1,0) {$t=0$};
    \node[font=\footnotesize, right] at (10.9,0) {$t=40$ y};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}
  \small
  At each stored step, from the last to the first, the pass:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item re-evaluates the step's six Runge--Kutta stages, with XAD recording the arithmetic;
    \item takes $\lambda$, the derivative of $\ln J$ in the state at the step's end, and turns
      it into the derivative in the state at the step's start;
    \item adds that step's contribution to $\partial\ln J/\partial\theta$ for all 49 parameters
      at once.
  \end{enumerate}
  Where a cohort was introduced, the pass carries $\lambda$ into the newborn's initial size, so
  seed traits get their share. The leaf's inner solve is never recorded: phylloptim supplies its
  derivatives directly. Only doubles cross into R.
  \src{Branch \texttt{offspring-adjoint} (plant\#91), TF24 v11.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The result: all 49 derivatives for the cost of 2.5 runs}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item One pass costs \textbf{2.5--2.7 runs}, whatever the number of introductions, against
      about 98 runs by differences.
    \item It agrees with central differences to 7e-4 of the resident's \texttt{lma} elasticity,
      and closer for the other entries checked.
    \item A derivative that does not exist is refused with a stated reason; a NaN without one
      would be a bug.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{docs/measurements/perf-adjoint.md; eps-spread.md.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How it works: the invader reads the field the resident recorded}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[>=Stealth, x=1cm, y=1cm,
      st/.style={draw=accent, fill=wash, minimum width=0.9cm, minimum height=0.55cm, font=\scriptsize}]
    \node[font=\footnotesize, anchor=east] at (-0.3,1.2) {resident run, step $k$};
    \node[font=\footnotesize, anchor=east] at (-0.3,-0.4) {invader run, step $k$};
    \foreach \s in {1,...,6} {
      \node[st] (r\s) at (\s*1.5-1, 1.2) {stage \s};
      \node[st] (i\s) at (\s*1.5-1, -0.4) {stage \s};
      \draw[->, warm] (r\s) -- (i\s);
    }
    \node[font=\scriptsize, warm, align=center] at (4.0,0.4) {the field at each stage: light at the canopy's heights, the soil's water};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The resident's run records every accepted step: its time and size, and at each of
      its six stages, the field every cohort read.
    \item \texttt{scm\$run\_mutant(invader)} integrates the invader's cohorts on exactly those
      steps. At each stage it loads the recorded field instead of building one.
    \item The invader's own reverse pass holds that field fixed, which is what a selection
      gradient asks for.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{\texttt{PLANT-95}, \texttt{PLANT-99} (plant\#95, \#99).}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The result: $J'$ is smooth in $\theta'$, and equals $J$ at the resident's traits}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item With the steps fixed, $J'$ is a smooth function of $\theta'$, so its differences and
      its reverse pass are meaningful.
    \item An invader at the resident's own traits returns $J' = J$ to the last bit. The tests
      check this on every record.
    \item The first invader reuses the resident's recorded run instead of repeating it:
      139 s against 300 s.
    \item An invader run costs about 0.77 of a resident run.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The problem: growth has a corner where net production crosses zero}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}
        \begin{axis}[width=\linewidth, height=4.4cm, xmin=-1, xmax=1, ymin=-0.1, ymax=1.05,
            xlabel={net production $P$}, ylabel={growth},
            xtick={0}, ytick={0}, every axis label/.style={font=\footnotesize},
            ticklabel style={font=\footnotesize}, axis y line*=left, axis x line*=bottom]
          \addplot[accent, thick, domain=-1:1, samples=200] {(x + sqrt(x^2 + 0.0004))/2};
        \end{axis}
      \end{tikzpicture}
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small
      TF24 grows a cohort by the positive part of its net production $P$. Below zero a cohort
      draws on storage and does not grow.
      \medskip

      The positive part is smoothed over a band of $10^{-4}$ kg/yr. A cohort crosses that band
      in a small fraction of one step: in the median crossing the step is $5\times10^4$ times
      longer than the bend.
      \medskip

      So at the scale of a step, the cohort's rate has a corner.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
  \src{docs/design-sign-changes.md \S1.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The problem: a corner inside a step turns the gradient into a staircase}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}
        \begin{axis}[width=\linewidth, height=4.4cm, xmin=0, xmax=1, ymin=0, ymax=1.1,
            xlabel={trait $\theta$}, ylabel={$d\ln J/d\theta$},
            xticklabels={}, yticklabels={}, every axis label/.style={font=\footnotesize},
            axis y line*=left, axis x line*=bottom, legend style={font=\scriptsize, draw=none, at={(0.02,0.98)}, anchor=north west}]
          \addplot[warm, thick, const plot, samples=9, domain=0:1] {0.25 + 0.6*x};
          \addplot[accent, thick, domain=0:1] {0.3 + 0.6*x};
          \legend{without the cut, with it}
        \end{axis}
      \end{tikzpicture}
      \illus
    \end{column}
    \begin{column}{0.52\textwidth}
      \small
      A Runge--Kutta step evaluates the rate at six stages inside the step. Its error from a
      corner depends on where the corner sits among those stages.
      \medskip

      As $\theta$ moves, each crossing slides through the step. Each time it passes a stage
      the error jumps, so the gradient moves in steps. A 5\% change of tolerance moves every
      step boundary, and so moves the gradient by up to 0.52$\varepsilon$.
      \medskip

      A curvature taken across one of those jumps was off by 13$\varepsilon$.
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How it works: find the crossing inside the step, and integrate that cohort in two pieces}
  \begin{columns}[T]
    \begin{column}{0.46\textwidth}
      \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm, >=Stealth]
        \draw[->, quiet] (0,0) -- (5,0) node[right, black, font=\footnotesize] {$t$};
        \draw[->, quiet] (0,-1) -- (0,1.5) node[above, black, font=\footnotesize] {a cohort's $P$};
        \draw[thick, accent] (0.5,1.1) .. controls (1.5,0.9) and (2.2,0.3) .. (2.7,0)

      \small
      After odelia accepts a step, it hands plant the step just taken, with an interpolant of the
      whole state across it. With \texttt{ode\_split\_sign\_changes = TRUE}, plant then:
      \begin{enumerate}
        \item reads each cohort's $P$ at five fractions of the step, 0, ¼, ½, ¾ and 1, and finds
          where the curve through them crosses zero;
        \item for each cohort that crosses, integrates that cohort alone from the step's start to
          $t^*$ and from $t^*$ to the end, in the light and water read off the interpolant;
        \item leaves every other cohort and the soil on the whole step.
      \end{enumerate}
    \end{column}
  \end{columns}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How it works in reverse: the cut moves with the traits}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item In the reverse pass, each piece is a step of its own: its stages are re-evaluated and
      recorded like any other step's.
    \item The cut time $t^*$ is where $P(t^*; \theta) = 0$, so it moves as $\theta$ moves. The
      pass differentiates it through that equation (the implicit function theorem), so
      $dt^*/d\theta$ enters the gradient.
    \item plant owns which cohort is cut, the field it reads and its pieces. One body of code
      serves the forward run and the reverse pass, so the two cannot disagree about a cut.
      odelia owns the arithmetic: the interpolant, the pieces and the root-finding.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{\texttt{PLANT-100}--\texttt{103}, odelia\#53--54, phylloptim\#17; docs/design-sign-changes.md \S8.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The result: smooth, reproducible gradients for 5--9\% more time}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\ln J$ lies 3--13$\times$ nearer the converged answer wherever cohorts cross zero.
    \item A 5\% change of tolerance moves no gradient entry by more than $\varepsilon/6$, against
      up to 0.52$\varepsilon$ before.
    \item The reverse pass through the cuts agrees with central differences to 2e-3 on every
      record.
    \item The leaf's solve now stops at roundoff (phylloptim\#17), so re-running a step at a
      trait $10^{-12}$ away moves $\ln J$ smoothly. Its noise fell from 2.7e-8 to about 1e-14.
    \item The cuts cost 5--9\% of a gradient run.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Part 5 of 9: TF24's step settings in one preset}
  \roadmap{5}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How plant decides a step is accurate enough, and what a weight does}
  \small
  plant's integrator accepts a step when, for every state $y_i$, its estimated error is below
  its allowed error:
  \[
    |\text{error}_i| \;\le\; w_i \,\big(\texttt{tol\_rel}\,|y_i| + \texttt{tol\_abs}\big)
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $w_i$ is a weight per state, new in this work and 1 by default. A weight of 10 lets that
      state carry ten times the error, so it takes fewer, longer steps.
    \item Each weight can also change over the run: part 7 raises them late in the patch's life.
    \item \texttt{control\_tf24(tol)} sets TF24's values for these knobs once, so every run
      of an analysis uses the same ones. plant's \texttt{Control()} defaults do not change.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{odelia \texttt{OdeControl::adjust\_step\_size}; \texttt{state-weights}.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{What \texttt{control\_tf24()} sets, and why each value}
  \footnotesize
  \begin{tabular}{@{}L{0.27\textwidth}L{0.68\textwidth}@{}}
    \toprule
    setting & why \\
    \midrule
    \texttt{tol\_abs} = $10^{-4}\times$\texttt{tol\_rel}
      & Tied this way, $J$'s error stays within about half of \texttt{tol}. With plant's default
        absolute tolerance it was about 20$\times$ larger at \texttt{tol} = 1e-4. \\
    soil layers' weight 10
      & The soil set most steps while carrying little of $J$'s error. With ten times its error
        allowed, a gradient run takes 21\% less time, and both roles still pass the accuracy and
        reproducibility tests at 3e-5. \\
    every weight bounded at 100
      & Part 7 multiplies weights by up to 100. Unbounded, the soil's reached 1000, an accepted
        step could carry a soil layer to its potential ceiling, and the gradient was refused there. \\
    steps capped at 15 days
      & An invader's storage pools go unstable on steps longer than 26 days. On the episodic and
        dry records, the invader at twice the resident's \texttt{lma} failed on 27-day steps. \\
    soil alone below a 10\% share & Part 6. \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \medskip

  \small
  With part 7, these settings save 31--34\% of a gradient run against the tied tolerance alone,
  and with the cap no invader fails on any record.
  \src{\texttt{PLANT-104}, \texttt{PLANT-105} (plant\#104, \#105); docs/grid-dynamics.md \S10.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Part 6 of 9: the soil stepped on its own}
  \roadmap{6}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The problem: the soil's stability sets almost every step}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Water moves between TF24's soil layers within days. An explicit Runge--Kutta step
      longer than that makes the soil unstable, so the step must stay short.
    \item On long drought the soil alone forces \textbf{16\,447 of the run's 17\,684 steps}. The
      cohorts on their own would need about 9200.
    \item For much of a run the stand draws only a small share of the water moving through the
      soil, so the soil hardly depends on the plants over a single step.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{docs/grid-dynamics.md \S1; docs/design-grid-controller.md, ``The time axis''.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{How it works: the soil takes short inner steps inside each step}
  \begin{center}
  \begin{tikzpicture}[x=1cm, y=1cm, >=Stealth]
    \draw[thick, accent] (0,0) -- (10,0);
    \draw[accent, thick] (0,-0.15) -- (0,0.15) node[above, font=\scriptsize, black] {$t$};
    \draw[accent, thick] (10,-0.15) -- (10,0.15) node[above, font=\scriptsize, black] {$t+h$};
    \node[font=\footnotesize, anchor=east] at (-0.2,0) {the cohorts' step};
    \foreach \x in {0,0.4,...,10.01} \draw[quiet] (\x,-0.75) -- (\x,-0.55);
    \node[font=\footnotesize, anchor=east] at (-0.2,-0.65) {the soil's inner steps};
    \draw[->, warm] (0,-1.15) -- (10,-1.15) node[midway, below, font=\scriptsize] {1. predictor: under uptake extrapolated from the last step};
    \draw[->, warm] (0,-1.9) -- (10,-1.9) node[midway, below, font=\scriptsize] {3. corrector: under uptake from the start's to the end's};
  \end{tikzpicture}
  \end{center}
  \small
  At a step's start plant computes the uptake's share of the water moving through the soil. If
  it is under \texttt{ode\_soil\_alone\_share} (0.1 in the preset), odelia:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item integrates the soil alone across the step in short inner steps, under the plants'
      uptake extrapolated from the last step;
    \item takes the cohorts' Cash--Karp step, each stage reading the soil from step 1;
    \item integrates the soil again, under the uptake the step found, and keeps that end;
    \item counts the gap between the two soil passes in the step's error.
  \end{enumerate}
  The run records the extrapolation and the inner steps, so a replay and the reverse pass take
  the same steps.
  \src{\texttt{PLANT-105}, odelia\#55; docs/design-soil-alone.md, ``A step taken alone, in order''.}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{The result: a gradient run takes 0.63 of the time}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Timed alone, a gradient run takes 376 s against 596 s.
    \item Steps fall 42--45\% on the pulsed records and 64\% under constant rain.
    \item Every elasticity moves by less than 0.063$\varepsilon$, and $\ln J$ stays within
      5.8e-5 of a reference at tolerance 1e-6 on all five records.
    \item It replaces an implicit solver for the soil (ARK) that was built first: under constant
      rain it costs 0.97 of that solver's steps, and the ARK branches are retired.
  \end{itemize}
  \src{docs/measurements/soil-alone/prereg.txt, gates G1--G7.}
\end{frame}

  \small
  \begin{itemize}

      ratios of successive changes are 3.5--4.0, against 4 in theory.
    \item That makes the error computable from two runs. With $Q$ a quantity from a run and

      \[ \text{converged answer} \approx Q + \frac{Q - Q_{1/2}}{3}. \]
    \item The half-introduction run now reports 0.87--0.99 of the true error, against
      0.33--0.41 before.
    \item The spread costs 6.7\% of a gradient run.

    \item \textbf{FF16 on the birth-date coordinate moves}, because part 8 changes how leaf area

\begin{frame}{Known gaps}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Episodic rain needs finer introductions} for part 9's estimate to hold. Its error
      falls at the square law only once the spacing is below its dry spells (median 31 days),
      past 429 introductions.
    \item \textbf{Under constant rain the gradients move by up to 3$\varepsilon$} with where the
      later introductions sit; only the first year is graded finer.
    \item \textbf{A gradient blow-up with the soil stepped with the plants at a loose tolerance.}

    \item \textbf{How far one resident's recorded steps can serve} a moved resident or a distant
      invader is unmeasured; \texttt{diagnose\_scm()} reports the distance without a bound.
    \item The branches sit on the fork's \texttt{develop} with odelia 0.5.0, and need a rebase
      onto upstream \texttt{develop} (odelia 0.6.2) before PRs open there.
  \end{itemize}
