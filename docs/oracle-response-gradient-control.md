# Oracle response — control algorithms with guarantees for a gradient on a shared grid

Three things carry most of the answers, so I'll state them first.

**A. The record splits the run into two regimes, and the integrator is doing a different job in each.** Under a smooth record the step is stability‑limited by the chain terminus: `u_L` sets `r_max` 77% of the time, `h|λ|` sits at the explicit boundary, and the 19% rejection rate is the controller hunting that boundary through the dead band. Time error there is `6.6e-8`; the creation grid owns the error (M8). Under an intermittent record the step is accuracy‑limited by `u_{1..L−1}` and the members, the `z_j` throws are a third limiter, and the record's `f''` jumps at active knots fall inside the steps (knots outnumber steps 3.5:1, and in event stretches the active ones are dense exactly where the steps are). The local error of any RK pair on a step containing a `C¹` breakpoint is `O(h³)` whatever its order; with several active knots per step the pair is a second‑order method there, at any tolerance. That is the leading candidate for the 142%. Test: insert the active knots into both grids of M8 and see whether they converge; if they don't, look for a classification flip between the two trajectories, which is item C below, not discretisation. M6's replay failures are the smooth regime showing through: the controller leaves every captured step at `h|λ| = β` with zero margin, so any θ that raises `|λ|` breaks it. That is why the box needs factor 2, and why a designed grid can carry a computable margin instead.

**B. `𝒢_b` is the spatial grid of a transport equation in `b`, not just `J`'s quadrature.** Each member is a characteristic; `a_ℓ` is a quadrature of `∫ρc db` at every step and feeds back into `u`. That is why M9 has two `O(Δb²)` terms of opposite sign, why raising the output rule's order alone makes the total worse, and why Q4's 11% is what M1 predicts rather than a paradox. It is also where `∇J`'s order loss comes from. `P` is `C⁰` across a switching surface, so at fixed `t` the `b`‑integrand of the coupling has a kink at the front and its θ‑derivative has a *jump* (envelope theorem on one side, bound‑derivative on the other). Trapezium error at a kink is `O(Δb²)`; at a jump it is `O(Δb)`, with a sign set by where the front sits in its cell. So `J` keeps order 2 while `∇J` acquires a first‑order term whose coefficient scales with switching activity: 0% constrained gives 1.8, 14% gives 1.34. That is a convergent lower‑order term, not a floor, and the two are separable (Q3).

**C. On a fixed grid, `J_h(θ)` is not smooth as implemented, and nothing at the operating point can be certified until it is.** Three sources. The `1e-3` terminal classification is a discrete decision on a continuous quantity; when a member flips, `J_h` jumps by that member's trapezium weight, which at `M ~ 10²` is up to ~1% — the size of M7's noise. The derivative rows divide by a Hermite second derivative that jumps at each of 401 knots, so `∇J_h` is discontinuous in θ at the `~1e-5` level — M4's fixed‑grid floor. A domain throw changes the grid, so a replay that throws is no longer the grid that was certified. The ±0.5% FD scatter is a property of the instrument, not of the derivative: the sweep matches a tangent to ten digits.

## 1. Construction

Guarantees (1) and (3) hold by construction: `𝒢_t ⊇ 𝒢_b ∪ K_act ∪ E ∪ {T}`, with `K_act` the active knots of the record, and everything built from the record plus at most one frozen reference solve at `θ₀`. H1 permits a frozen `θ₀`; it forbids only dependence on the θ at which the gradient is taken. Switching‑surface crossings are the one structural stop (1) cannot include, since they are trajectory‑dependent; they cost `O(h²)` per crossing and are either budgeted or removed by smoothing the switch (3c).

Guarantee (2) holds for structure and stability, not for accuracy. The record gives the active knots, the event times, and, from event size, the post‑event envelope `Λ(t)` of `|λ|` in closed form. Stability density is `h(t) ≤ β/(m·Λ(t))`. Because `|λ_ℓ| = qκ_ℓu_ℓ^{q−1} = q·outflux_ℓ/u_ℓ ∝ flux^{1−1/q}·κ^{1/q}`, the record's flux sets `Λ` and κ enters at the `1/q` power — `3000^{1/16} = 1.65` of your measured 2.0, the remainder through the flux falling with `u`. So κ over ±2× moves `Λ` by 4%; what moves it is throughflux to the terminus, i.e. the consumption parameters, and a ±2× box in those can move `flux_L` by about 2× — the right size for M6's factor. `m` is therefore a computable functional of the box via the reference run's flux partition. The same holds for the members' stiffness `1/τ_z = (c+d)/z_max`, whose stress dependence follows the record's absences. Accuracy density is not computable from the record: it needs the solution and the adjoint.

Guarantee (4) is a posteriori, at `θ₀`, and only as good as B and C allow. The minimal construction:

1. Structural stops `S = 𝒢_b ∪ K_act ∪ E ∪ {T}`. For `𝒢_b`, replace the dyadic generator's blindness to the record with nodes bracketing event times in `b`: members created just before and just after an event have different histories, so `φ(b)` is steep there.
2. Between stops, cap `h` by the stability envelope with margin `m` — or delete this line by making the chain and `z_j` implicit (Q6).
3. One reference run at `θ₀` with the adjoint. Per step form `η_n = |λ_nᵀe_n|`, the adjoint‑weighted embedded local error; `e_n` is already computed for the error test and `λ_n` is already formed, so this is free. Equidistribute `η_n`. For `𝒢_b`, form the drop‑one‑point Richardson estimate of the *coupling* trapezium at each step, weight it by `λ_{u,n}`, attribute to nodes, and bisect until the sum is under `TOL_b`. This is the indicator your refinement loop lacks: its current indicator sees only M9's `+0.359%` output term and never the `−0.617%` reconstruction term, which is why it declares convergence while `J` moves 3–4× and why it is non‑monotone — bisecting one node changes the trajectory and hence every other node's indicator, and the jump‑position term flips sign. The derivative inherits the `J`‑error up to `κ_i` (Q2b), so set `TOL` against `tol_∇/κ_max`; anything sharper needs a second‑order object (Q5a).
4. Split `TOL` between `t` and `b` by your order‑proportional rule *per stretch*: event stretches are time‑dominated, quiescent stretches creation‑dominated. The regime map is the one part of the accuracy budget the record gives in advance.
5. Freeze; certify at each new θ as in Q5.

Properties statable in advance: contains every structural stop; stable on `{θ : Λ(t;θ) ≤ m·Λ(t;θ₀) ∀t}`; θ‑independent; `J`‑error estimated by `Ση_n + Ση_j` at `θ₀`, `∇J`‑error by that times `κ_i`, tightened by Richardson at recapture.

Which gives way first: (2) in its strong form. "Before the run" becomes "before this run, from one adjoint solve on this record." Nothing about (4) was ever going to be a priori with a usable constant.

## 2. Certifying a derivative

(a) Run the sequence on the adjoint gradients themselves, each exact for its level, and read the order from ratios of consecutive differences; that removes the FD reference and its ±0.5% floor. Stop when

`‖∇J_h − ∇J_{h/2}‖_W / (2^p − 1) ≤ τ·‖∇J_{h/2}‖_W`

with `p` the observed order from the last two differences, required to be near or above 1 and stable (erratic or sub‑unit `p` means a floor or a jump: do not stop), and `W` the optimiser's metric. Control the *absolute* gradient error against the gradient norm, not per‑component relative error. M3's component is 1% of its parts, so it is small in the vector, and its sign matters to descent only when it is the remaining direction — which is exactly when the norm has shrunk and a norm‑relative `τ` has tightened. This is Carter's inexact‑gradient condition, and the grid schedule follows from it (Q5b). If a component's sign is needed for its own sake, the condition is `κ_i·err_gross,i < 1`.

(b) Necessary, not sufficient: `κ` is the amplifier, and the gross error still comes from Richardson or the estimate in step 3. Its general form is always exposed. The sweep forms `dJ/dθ_i = Σ_n λ_nᵀ∂_{θ_i}Φ_n`; accumulate `Σ_n|·|` alongside and report `κ_i = Σ|·|/|Σ·|`. That bounds coherent‑error amplification from above and reduces to M3's 100 when two paths are the only structure. One extra accumulator.

(c) Yes, with a stated scope. Going downward samples `E(Δb)` where the smooth term dominates every floor, which is the only place a constant difference ratio can be observed. It certifies `C` and `p` of the smooth term and hence an *extrapolated* error at the operating point. It cannot see a term that appears only at fine levels — Q3's floor or C's jumps. Pair it with one check at the operating point: the difference to one bisection finer must equal `C·Δb_op^p(1 − 2^{−p})` within the extrapolation's uncertainty. M7's 0.27% is that check; whether it is consistent is a number you already have.

## 3. The floored subpopulation

(a) Write `E_k = C·Δb_k^p + F_k`. Three cases, three signatures. A constant floor cancels in consecutive differences: order read from differences stays at `p` while order read against a reference slides to 0. A random floor — which is what a bracket midpoint gives, since the sign depends on where the root sits in the `1e-4` bracket and which members are constrained changes with the grid — does not cancel, and the order from differences turns erratic once `C·Δb^p(1−2^{−p}) ~ σ_F`. The jump mechanism in B gives `E = C₂Δb² + C₁Δb`: the order from differences slides *monotonically* from 2 toward 1 and holds. A stable 1.34 fits the third; a transition regime of the first would show it over two or three levels but not hold. Fit `C₂, C₁, F` to four or more levels and see which coefficients survive; split the derivative by branch (the sweep knows each member‑instant's classification); and re‑run the sequence with the bracket at `1e-6`. If the order recovers it was the floor; if not it is the jump.

(b) State `|E| ≤ C·Δb^p + F`, with `F ≲ ε_inner·S`, where `S` is the adjoint‑weighted sensitivity of `∇J` to bound placement summed over constrained member‑instants, which the sweep can form. The time‑varying fraction lives inside that sum, each instant with its own adjoint weight. `F`'s record dependence is why certificates don't transfer across records (M6) and is not a defect. Do not report a single order.

(c) `ε_inner ≤ (0.1–0.3)·E_disc(Δb_op)/S`, so the floor stays a fixed fraction of the discretisation error you are paying for; along a refinement sequence the bracket tolerance then scales with `Δb²`, rather than one hard‑coded constant used as both absolute and relative. Two other tolerances matter more. The `1e-3` terminal classification must become continuous (a smooth collapse of the feasible interval, not a threshold) or every grid change flips some member. The knot‑discontinuous second derivative should come from a `C²` interpolant so `∇J_h` is continuous in θ. Both are cheaper than any refinement they would otherwise contaminate. To recover order 2 in `∇J` as well, smooth the switch itself — a `C¹` blend of width `w` across the surface, budgeted as `O(w)` modelling error. That also restores the pair's order across switching instants in time.

## 4. M5

(a) Same `𝒢_t`, different `𝒢_b`, 11% in `∇J`: candidate 4, produced by candidate 2. The coupling trapezium differs at every step, `u` differs, every member's history differs; the five trapezia are the mechanism, not five candidates, and the reconstruction term is the larger of M9's two. H3's four roles are one mathematical change plus bookkeeping, and bookkeeping matched on exact equality fails catastrophically or not at all — it does not produce a smooth 11%. A sweep‑side error (H6) would show as a tangent–reverse mismatch; M3's ten‑digit match exonerates it at that level, and one tangent run on the M5 pair settles it there.

To separate: a two‑by‑two, {coarse, fine `𝒢_b`} × {coarse adaptive, fine `𝒢_t`}. The row difference at fixed `𝒢_t` is `b`‑error; the column difference at fixed `𝒢_b` is `t`‑error. Check the `b`‑difference against M1's rate from the operating point, and report `κ_i` for the component quoted — if it is M3's, 11% net is 0.1% gross and ordinary at a coarse level. To split reconstruction from output, evaluate the coarse trapezium on the fine run's members subsampled at the coarse abscissae, which is M9's decomposition. For the forward alone, compare `u(t)` on the shared grid.

(b) Structural. `𝒢_b` is a discretisation and its error does not vanish because `𝒢_t` was placed well. A record‑placed `𝒢_b` lowers the constant by bracketing events; the derivative's first‑order term cannot be targeted in advance because fronts are trajectory‑dependent. Only refinement or the smoothing in 3(c) removes the sensitivity.

(c) Legitimate, and the right instrument for a `b`‑study: it holds `𝒢_t` fixed across levels, so the sequence measures `b`‑convergence alone, which adaptive‑at‑each‑level confounds. It answers "the `b`‑error at a `t`‑grid whose own error is `1e-5`", which is the question a `b`‑certificate should answer; that it reproduces every adaptive answer to `1e-5` also certifies the smooth‑record adaptive time grids. On intermittent records verify `h|λ|` along the union, since a coarse level's steps may be unstable on a fine level's trajectory.

## 5. A shared grid with a certificate

(a) The cheap sufficient condition is the controller's own test, run passively. On replay at θ, form `r_{max,n}` (the embedded estimate comes from the same stages), `h_n·|λ_max(J_chain(y_n))|` (bidiagonal, analytic, free), and `h_n(c+d)_j/z_max`. Validity is `max r ≤ 1.1`, `max h|λ| ≤ β`, and no throw. This certifies the replayed grid is one the controller would have accepted at θ — the same accuracy class as adaptive — and it predicts every M6 failure. The instrumentation exists and is unread. The discrete adjoint of an RK pair is itself an RK‑type scheme for the adjoint whose order is checkable from the tableau (Hager 2000; Sanz‑Serna 2016), so the gradient's time error is in the same class up to `κ_i`. That is qualitative. Quantitative needs either Richardson in time — drop every other supplied point, keep structural stops, one cheaper gradient; invalid at the stability boundary, valid once the chain is implicit — or the θ‑derivative of the DWR estimate, which is a second‑order adjoint. A second‑order object is required for a *bound*, not for a certificate.

(b) The trigger is the inexact‑gradient condition failing — estimated `‖∇J_h − ∇J‖_W > η‖∇J_h‖_W` — or the passive certificate failing at a trial point. Recapture changes the objective, so the optimiser must tolerate inexact values: Carter's condition on the gradient, and the Kouri et al. condition that model error be a fraction of predicted reduction. Near convergence predicted reduction shrinks and the grid must refine; that is the schedule, not a nuisance. A provable trust region exists for the stability component, `{θ : Λ(t;θ) ≤ m·Λ(t;θ₀)}`, and through the flux scaling it becomes a box in the consumption parameters if `a_ℓ` is monotone in them. For accuracy there is no proof, only the certificate at each evaluation.

(c) Nearly. Add that recapture is handled by the optimiser as a change of objective, that the certificate covers `∇J` rather than the state, and that `𝒢_b` needs its own certificate (the adjoint‑weighted coupling indicator). A designed grid is provably wider in one respect only: the controller leaves a captured grid at `h|λ| = β` with zero margin by construction, while a designed grid carries `m`. For accuracy, "wider" is what you build in. One grid per record is the right unit — the record is the geometry, so zero transfer is inherent.

## 6. Attribution, and the guarantee not asked for

| | carries | incidental |

| Q1 | H1, H2, M6 (margin), M8 (regimes), the closed‑form envelope | H3, H6, M5 |

The guarantee not asked for is stability by construction, and the cheapest route is to make the chain and `z_j` implicit. It is smaller work than H5 suggests: the chain's implicit stage is lower‑triangular forward substitution with scalar Newton at a fixed iteration count, taped without branches; `z_j`'s implicit part is linear, so its stage solve is a division; the member loop and `a_ℓ` stay explicit and are evaluated once per stage, exactly as now — this is an additive RK split, not multirate, so your "collapses" argument does not apply. Check `∂a_ℓ/∂u` near `u_min` is not itself stiff. It removes the 17–27% wasted evaluations, every throw, the `h|λ|` fragility behind M6, and the whole stability clause of Q1 and Q5. Second, smoothness of `J_h(θ)` on the box (C), which is what makes a gradient meaningful to an optimiser. Third, order restoration by knot alignment, which turns the pair back into a fifth‑order method in event stretches.

Sacrifice (2)‑strong first; never (3); (4) is not sacrificed, it was always a posteriori.

Two free items: the `L` accumulators never bind and read nothing, so drop them from the norm and the tape; and the FSAL stage is formed before the error test, so a rejection costs six evaluations instead of five.
