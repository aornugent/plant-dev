## 1. The mechanism, derived

A golden-section search with a fixed count of `n` iterations returns an estimate of the argmax with a deterministic resolution floor: `|p̂ − p*| ~ (b_j − a_j) · φ⁻ⁿ` (interval reduction ~0.618 per iteration). The map `(x_j, u, s) → p̂` is continuous — but it is not smooth at the scale below the floor. It is a continuous piecewise structure whose "pieces" are set by which comparison outcomes the search takes: as the inputs move, the sequence of interior-point comparisons flips one by one, and `p̂` follows a path with small kinks and slope errors of relative size ~φ⁻ⁿ everywhere, densely, at no loggable surface. (Your branch monitor watched the four feasibility branches and saw ≥99.7% "full search" — the noise lives inside the full-search branch, in the 2ⁿ comparison outcomes the instrument doesn't log. R2 and refutation #4 are consistent with this, not evidence against it.)

Now push that through the solver. The RHS ingredients `g(·, p̂)` and `c(·, p̂)` inherit a deterministic, state-dependent, high-frequency ripple of relative amplitude ~ε_p·(local curvature terms), where ε_p is the floor. Note the value-vs-derivative asymmetry, the same envelope structure this system has had from the start: `P(p̂)` errs only at O(ε_p²) (stationarity), but `c(p̂)` and `g(p̂)` err at O(ε_p) — the non-stationary outputs feel the floor at first order. An adaptive embedded RK's error estimator is a difference of stage combinations: smooth truncation error scales as h⁵ (Cash–Karp's embedded order), while the ripple contributes an estimator floor that does not shrink with h. The controller therefore behaves normally while h is large (truncation dominates), and as it enters a quiet interval — where genuine truncation error is small — it descends until h⁵-error meets the noise floor. Below that point the estimator returns noise, `err/tol` fluctuates around 1 independently of h, and the controller does the only thing it can: reject, shrink, retry, accept at random, occasionally spiral to h_min.

Check this against every measured signature:

* Broadband, scattered, quiet-interval collapse (R7, prior rounds' geography): quiet intervals are where truncation error is smallest, so they hit the noise floor first. Not "fast structure in quiet intervals" — absence of structure exposing the floor.
* Rejections co-locate with nothing (R1, R5's `|ρ|<0.11`): noise-floor rejections are driven by pseudo-random comparison-flip patterns, not by any margin. An event mechanism cannot produce zero correlation; a noise mechanism must.
* The one weak positive correlate is bracket width (R5, ρ ≈ +0.24…0.42), narrower → smaller steps: at first glance backwards — a narrower bracket means a smaller absolute floor ε_p = (b−a)φ⁻ⁿ. But the bracket narrows exactly when the feasible set tightens (depletion), where the objective's curvature and the sensitivity of `c` to p̂ blow up; the induced RHS ripple is ε_p × sensitivity, and the sensitivity grows faster than the width shrinks. Weak positive correlation with heavy collinearity against depletion margins is precisely the expected signature. (E2 below separates this cleanly.)
* The h_min wall three decades past J-convergence: the classic two-plateau anatomy of integrating a noisy RHS — J converges when truncation error crosses J's sensitivity threshold; the controller keeps descending until it hits the floor's wall, which sits wherever h⁵·C = ε_RHS, unrelated to J. "A resolution limit, not a stability limit" — your own phrasing from two rounds ago — is exactly what a noise floor is.
* The 27–35% rejection fraction, uniform across sequences: a controller hovering at its noise floor accepts/rejects on a near-coin-flip in the affected intervals; blend with clean intervals and you get a stable ~30% aggregate, insensitive to forcing harshness. No accuracy-structure explanation ever accounted for the uniformity; this does.
* R6's tracked-p failure is corroborating, not separate: `∂P/∂p` evaluated near a tightening feasible boundary drives an unprojected gradient flow out of the domain — the same "the inner problem's conditioning degrades continuously with depletion" fact wearing different clothes.

One honest caveat: the exact `∂P/∂p` is stated to come from an IFT through an inner root inside `P`. If that root is itself iterative with a fixed count, it contributes a second, independent floor with the same phenomenology. The diagnosis below doesn't distinguish the two sources and doesn't need to; the fixes cover both.

## 2. The falsification tests — hours each, and decisive

E1 (the direct one): measure the RHS noise floor. Freeze a state `y` from a quiet-interval hard step. Evaluate `f(y + δ·v)` along a random direction for δ sweeping 10⁻¹²…10⁻³. A smooth RHS gives a clean secant limit; a floored RHS shows the derivative estimate diverging as δ⁻¹-scaled noise below some δ*. Do it at n = current, n+8, n+16 golden-section iterations: if δ and the noise amplitude walk down by ~φ⁻⁸ per increment, the mechanism is confirmed and quantified in one plot. Also run it in a busy interval for contrast.

E2 (the solver-level one): tolerance–iteration crossing. Rerun one bank sequence at n, n+8, n+16 inner iterations, same outer tolerance. Prediction: rejection fraction collapses toward the few-% of a healthy controller, min-h rises by ~10^(8·log₁₀φ) ≈ 1.7 decades per +8 iterations, quiet-interval small steps vanish, J unchanged (it converged long before the floor). If instead the collapse pattern is invariant in n — the mechanism is refuted and the residual is genuinely intrinsic; I'd then want E1's plot anyway, because something is setting a tolerance-independent floor.

E3 (the noise-aware controller check): compute, offline from saved embedded-error data, what an err-floor-aware acceptance rule (accept when err ≤ max(tol, floor_est)) would have done — your saved per-step logs make this a minutes-scale re-attribution, per your §5.

## 3. If confirmed — the fixes, ranked, with the tape constraint front and center

The deliberate design choice (fixed-iteration for tape-smoothness) was solving a real problem — a variable-iteration Brent stop makes `p̂(inputs)` genuinely discontinuous at stopping-decision boundaries, which is worse for AD. But the fixed-count search never delivered smoothness; it delivered continuity with dense sub-resolution kinks. The correct object, which this program has had in hand since round one, is: iterate to a residual tolerance off-tape, then present the converged point through an implicit-function node. That is the established IFT-node pattern, and it is available here because `∂P/∂p` is exact:

F1 — Newton/secant polish + IFT node (the real fix). Keep the golden-section bracketing for globalization (it's robust and its output no longer matters at first order), then polish with 2–4 safeguarded Newton steps on `∂P/∂p = 0` to residual ≲ machine-scale × curvature. Cost: ~2–4 extra objective/gradient evaluations per member per RHS — against a 30% rejection overhead of full O(M) sweeps, this is strongly cost-negative. The RHS becomes smooth to machine precision; the controller floor drops below every tolerance you use. For the tape: `p*` becomes a scalar-IFT node (adjoint = −P_pu/P_pp etc., denominators sign-definite away from degeneracy — and R2 says the degenerate branch never fires). This simultaneously discharges the still-open §6.6 item: the envelope-FD-at-fixed-p* adjoint, which drops the O(1) term `(∂c/∂p)(∂p*/∂u)` on the non-stationary channel, is replaced by the exact IFT composition — one change fixes the forward noise floor and the suspected first-order gradient bug. R6's failure does not transfer: tracked-p was an unconstrained ODE state free to exit the domain between evaluations; a within-evaluation polish starts from a bracketed feasible interval and is safeguarded inside it.

F2 — if the argmax must remain derivative-free: raise n adaptively until the induced RHS ripple (ε_p × a cheap curvature estimate) sits below outer-tol/10 — iteration count set per-solve from state, recorded pass-1, replayed passive. Cost ~+10–20 evaluations/member; still likely cost-negative against the rejection overhead.

F3 — regardless: a noise-floor-aware controller guard (never drive h below the measured floor; treat err≈floor as accept-at-floor) — protection against any residual floor from other iterative pieces, cheap, and honest about what tolerance means.

F4 — not recommended: smoothing/averaging the ripple — it biases the non-stationary channel into a 10×-amplifying J.
