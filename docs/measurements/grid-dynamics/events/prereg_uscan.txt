Pre-registration: the events reply's u-scan of second differences
Written before any run of this test (2026-10-04).

The question
  The events reply (docs/oracle-response-events.md, section 2) recommends taking the
  resident's curvatures by forward second differences of ln J at u near 1e-2 on a
  frozen grid with events. It predicts they are stable across u in [3e-3, 3e-2]
  within eps/10, and that a bias from unlocated class switches, if any, would show
  as an offset from the converged value that does not fall with u. The record
  already has the plain arm's second difference at u = 1e-2 within 0.3 of the
  adaptive value (-44.04 against -43.7; final_chord.txt, assessment step 5). So
  the curvature diagonal may not need events at that u at all.

The setting
  The events spike's driver and setting: long drought (seed 31), 108 uniform
  nodes, Cash-Karp under the tied tolerance (ATOL=1e-4), at tol 1e-4 and its
  +-5% nudges 9.5e-5 and 1.05e-4, each on its own recorded program (g_T.rds).
  lma alone, at lma (1 + r) for r in +-3e-3, +-1e-2, +-3e-2, with +-1e-3 added
  for the re-detected split at 1e-4.

The arms
  - plain: the program replayed with no split (rp).
  - split, re-detected: each replay finds its own crossings and splits them on
    the quintic through the half-step midpoint (sq). This is the reply's model,
    in which the structure changes as crossings pass from step to step.
  - split, frozen structure: the (row, member) pairs split at lma, as in
    final_chord.txt (fq), at 1e-4 only, for continuity with that table.

The quantity
  H(r), the second difference of ln J in ln lma about lma (analyze.R's diffs()),
  in units of eps for lma's curvature in the resident role, 1.17 (eps.csv).

The reference
  The adaptive chords at seed 31: -43.7 combined over 1e-2 and 3e-2, each run on
  its own grid (assessment step 5). Singly they are -43.1 and -43.6, so the
  reference itself is uncertain by about 0.3, a quarter of eps.

The predictions
  P1 (the reply). The re-detected split's H moves by less than eps/10 (0.12)
     across r in {3e-3, 1e-2, 3e-2} at 1e-4, and by less than eps/3 (0.39)
     under the nudges at r = 1e-2.
  P2 (the reply). If class switches leave a bias of order eps, the re-detected
     split's H sits at a distance of order eps from the reference at every r.
  P3 (ours, from the record). The plain arm's H at r = 1e-2 and 3e-2 lies within
     eps/3 of the reference and moves by less than eps/3 under the nudges.
  P4 (ours). The frozen-structure arm departs from the re-detected arm as r
     grows, because crossings that leave their recorded step are integrated
     unsplit in their new one.

What decides
  - P1 holds and P3 fails: the curvature diagonal needs events; build as the
    reply orders.
  - P1 and P3 both hold: second differences at r >= 1e-2 serve the diagonal
    without events, and events are justified by predictability (J's error
    falling with tol) and the gradients' margin instead.
  - P1 fails, with H moving across r by more than the passages allow
    (sqrt(N) x 5e-9 / r^2, N the crossings that change step): an unlocated kink
    shapes the frozen map, and P2 says whether it is a constant bias.
  The truncation at r = 3e-2 is about (0.03/0.245)^2/12 = 1.3e-3 of H, 0.06; it
  is reported, not removed.

Runs: uscan.sh. Analysis: uscan.R.

Extension, registered after the 1e-4 scan's values were seen and before the
nudges finished (2026-10-04, 03:40 UTC)
  The reference (-43.7, uncertain by about 0.3) cannot tell the re-detected
  split's -43.45 from the plain arm's -43.62 at r = 3e-2. A tighter tolerance
  can: the same second differences at tol 3e-5, on its own recorded program
  (g_3e-5.rds; bases rp_3e-5 and spq_3e-5), at r = +-1e-2 and +-3e-2, for both
  arms.
  P5. The re-detected split's H at r = 1e-2 and 3e-2 moves by less than eps/10
      from 1e-4 to 3e-5: its curvature has converged in tol.
  P6. The plain arm's H moves by more than the split's between the two
      tolerances, because how far its kinks have averaged out depends on its
      grid.
  Runs: uscan.sh ext. Analysis: uscan.R (its tol-convergence lines).
