# P4: detect and repair (move 1): the forward may straddle a kink; only passes that differentiate the resident take the step in pieces

Assigned move: weaken exactness, or change the quantifier. The guarantee "no step
straddles a non-smooth point" is replaced by "no step that a derivative is taken
through straddles one". The forward that chose the steps keeps them; the repair is
made where the derivative is read.

Code read: odelia `origin/ODELIA-55` (`adjoint.hpp`, `ode_step.hpp`,
`ode_solver_internal.hpp`, `ode_solver.hpp`, `ode_interface.hpp`, `ode_control.hpp`);
plant `PLANT-110` (`scm.h`: `run`, `walk`, `patch.h` recorded field). Docs: the brief,
`kinks/inventory.md`, JT notes parts 2–4, `design-grid-controller.md`,
`design-workflows.md` (the reference analysis), `perf-sweep.md`.

Labels: **(m)** measured, quoted from the notes; **(e)** my estimate, with its
derivation; **(d)** derived by arithmetic from measured numbers.

## Triage: 2

The seams are odelia's step, record, schedule and sweep, and one System member. All
are in-memory and reversible; nothing is a persisted format or an outside API. The
R interface is untouched except that a replay program gains the pieces' times.

## What the sweep can estimate (the derivation behind candidate A)

A step's contribution to a gradient's error is
c_k = λ_{k+1}ᵀ (h Σ b_i G(t_k + c_i h) − ∫G), with G = ∂f/∂θ + ∂f/∂y · s and
s = ∂y/∂θ. At a jump ΔG at fraction u, this is h · ΔG · φ(u), with
φ(u) = Σ_{c_i > u} b_i − (1 − u) (JT part 4). From RKCK's weights:
φ(0+) = −b1 = −0.098, φ(0.3∓) = ±0.20, and φ(1−) = 0 because the c = 1 stage has
weight 0. So a kink just before a step end costs nothing, and one just after a step
start costs 0.098 · h · ΔG.

What one reverse sweep provides at step k: λ_{k+1}, and every product
(∂f/∂y)ᵀλ and (∂f/∂θ)ᵀλ the recording can be seeded with. It does not provide s.

1. **Per step, without s.** Only the ∂f/∂θ part is computable. Part 4's H used
   exactly that: it predicted 27% of the measured move, cosine 0.44 (m). The part
   that carries the error is Δ(∂f/∂y)·s: the soil's slope change times its
   sensitivity accumulated over the drydown (H2, m). So the sweep alone cannot weigh
   steps.
2. **The kink identity does not escape it.** For a kink on a surface g(y, θ) = 0,
   ΔG = −Δḟ · dt*/dθ. Δḟ is computable at double, but dt*/dθ needs one reverse
   sweep per kink, and a cluster holds 51.
3. **The total, for every trait at once, is cheap.** Add one seed row μ that
   descends beside λ. At each step, inject the step's defect, the λ-weighted
   derivative of the embedded error, into μ, and its ∂/∂θ part into μ's parameter
   row. At the end, that row holds d/dθ Σ_k λ_{k+1}ᵀ e_k. It shares λ's recordings
   (`internal::sweep_each_seed` sweeps each seed on one tape), so it costs one more
   reverse per recording. That is 4.1 of 43 µs per member evaluation, +9.5% of the
   sweep (d, `perf-sweep.md` §2), and no rows.
   - Two catches. With the embedded pair as the defect, the factor is
     φ5(u) − φ4(u), not φ5(u): right in scale, wrong in value and sign.
   - With located jumps as the defect, the factor is right, but it needs Δ(Jᵀλ) on
     each side of every kink. That is 2 taped evaluations × 51 kinks × 234 steps,
     about +83% of the sweep (e), or per cluster +1.6% (e) at unknown accuracy.
   - Either way it estimates the error of the map without pieces, not of the map
     the run uses.
4. **Placement by a second pass** (A below): +1 forward and +1 sweep.

**Conclusion.** In reverse mode, where the error arises can be detected (a sign flip
in the step). How much it weighs in the gradient cannot be known per step without
forward sensitivities, which were ruled out. So this move cannot get its leverage
from choosing which steps to repair. It gets it from choosing which passes repair.

## Candidates

All candidates are inside move 1, as assigned. Each weakens a different quantifier.

- **A [first thought]: adjoint-weighted repair placed by a second pass.** Weakens
  "every kinked step" to "the kinked steps whose estimated c_k matters".
  - *Commitment:* the repair budget follows the adjoint.
  - *Pays for:* R1, if the ranking holds. The top 30 of 221 switch steps hold 90%
    of Σ|c| by the a-priori bound and 70% by |λ_soil| alone (m).
  - *Costs:* a weight, a threshold, a stored per-step decision, and a replay with
    its sweep. That is +100% rows on each gradient run (e). The weight needs ΔG,
    which needs s (item 1), so it runs on a proxy. And λ belongs to one functional,
    while a grid serves several.
  - *Wins when:* every pass over the grid sweeps the resident anyway, so the second
    pass is free, and kinks sit in most steps, so selecting pays.
- **B: pieces, only where the resident is differentiated.** Weakens "every pass" to
  "every pass that differentiates through the resident's state".
  - The forward reads a declared kink value at the stage readings it already takes,
    and stores a bracket on the row.
  - The sweep of the run that chose the steps transposes a bracketed row as up to
    three pieces, taken at double inside the sweep.
  - The schedule (the replay program) carries the pieces as ordinary steps.
    Invaders walk the recording, which never holds pieces.
  - *Commitment:* see below.
  - *Pays for:* R1 by the measured bracket (0.0020, m). R3 by putting nothing in
    forwards or walks. R4 with one member.
  - *Costs:* 5 names. A sweep that steps (it takes the pieces at double). The
    building run's gradient is off its own forward by about 0.001ε (e).
  - *Wins when:* most passes of an analysis do not differentiate the resident. That
    is the reference analysis.
- **C: correction term.** Weakens "differentiate the discrete map" to "differentiate
  the continuous one to first order". At each located kink, the sweep adds
  −h·φ(u*)·Δ(Jᵀλ) to λ and to the parameter rows.
  - *Pays for:* R1 with no pieces and no stepping in the sweep.
  - *Costs:* 2 taped evaluations per kink, about +83% of the sweep for 51-kink
    clusters (e). The gradient matches no map, so central differences miss by
    Σ|c|, 0.05–0.17ε (m, part 4).
  - *Wins when:* kinks are isolated (one per step) and the consumer wants the
    continuous model's gradient, not the discrete one's.
- **D: tighter tolerance where flagged.** The error is O(h·ΔG), and h ∝ tol^(1/5).
  Cutting it 24× needs tol × 24⁻⁵ ≈ 1.3e-7. Measured as X's ETT: 0.025 at +8.6% rows
  (m).
  - *Wins when:* the kink is a W (smooth, narrow), not a K. Out of scope here.
- **P (a variant of B):** the pieces in the schedule only, and the gradient taken
  from a replay of the schedule. Exact for its own map, and the sweep is unchanged.
  - *Costs:* +1 forward per gradient run, +~30% of the run (e), and two recordings
    in memory: the walks' and the sweep's.
  - *Wins when:* R6 must hold strictly for the building run, or the sweep must not
    step.

**Winner: B.** Eliminations:
- **A:** fails R3. The second pass is +100% rows per gradient (e). Its per-step weight
  without s captures 27% (m, H), so the steps it chooses are not the ones that
  matter.
- **C:** fails R6. Its gradient differs from central differences by 0.05–0.17ε
  against a bar of 0.01ε (m), and it costs +83% of the sweep (e).
- **D:** fails R1 by arithmetic: 0.025 measured against 0.006.
- **P:** fails R3 per run (+~30%, e). It is the fallback if R6 is tightened (see the
  kill condition).
- **The floor:** meets the brief per run (m). It loses to B under the R3 challenge,
  at about +8–10 forwards per analysis against B's +1–1.5 (e). It also breaks the
  controller's commitment that the norm alone sets the global step.

## The commitment

**A step the controller chose is never retaken. It is taken in pieces only by passes
that differentiate the resident: the sweep of the run that chose it, and replays of
that run's schedule.**

Kept true by structure:
- The bracket is a field the forward writes in `push_step` and the controller never
  reads. No path in `SolverInternal::step` can shorten a step for a kink.
- `pieces(rec, k)` is the only function that divides a row. It is called from
  exactly two places, `schedule()` and `sweep_range`. It returns the row whole
  unless the row was chosen by the controller (`error_index !=
  OdeControl::no_component`, an existing field) and holds a bracket.
- Replays and walks set no error component (`step_by` and `step_to` call
  `forget_error_component`). So their rows are never divided again, and a pass
  over the grid cannot change its own map.
- Invaders walk the recording (`SCM::walk(invaded_run)`, rows with recorded
  fields). Pieces exist only in the schedule, so no invader can be handed one.

## Kill question

*The assumption whose falsity makes this design unnecessary:* the repair matters only
to passes that differentiate through the resident's soil.

Argued from ledger facts only:
- **Invaders.** They are at zero density and do not feed back on the soil (R7). The
  soil is recorded data in their walk, not state, so their sweeps carry no
  λ_soil. Part 4 found λ_soil to be what sets the error (sd of log10 1.22, the
  largest factor, m).
- **What invaders read.** The soil they read is C1 in time across a switch, and
  their own P is C1 (envelope). So their G stays continuous, and their error at the
  resident's switches is O(h²), not O(h).
- **Secant forwards.** They read only J. J's error at the switch is O(h²) and moves
  1e-3ε (m, JT root cause).
- **Volume.** The passes that differentiate the resident are one gradient per
  analysis plus its report (`design-grid-controller.md`, "once per analysis, at the
  answer").

**Verdict: survives.** It would die if invaders fed back on the soil, or if most
passes swept the resident (two or more traits evolving with the sweep inside the
loops). Then the floor costs the same and is exact.

## What survives deletion

- `kink_values` (a System member, under concept `MarksKinks`) → R4 (one member) and
  R1 (detection).
- `kink_bracket` (on `step_record`) → R1 (where to cut) and R6. One stored decision,
  read by the schedule and by the sweep, so the two cannot disagree.
- `pieces(rec, k)` → R1, R2, R6. One derivation of the pieces and their substeps,
  for both readers.
- The sweep taking pieces at double (private `transpose_pieces`) → R3. Without it
  the building run needs a replay first (P, +~30%), and R1 for the adaptive run
  goes with it.
- `pieces_refused()`, a count of rows the sweep transposed whole because a piece
  threw `DomainError` → O-Diagnosed ("its failures") and "never fails".

Deleted in the deletion pass:
- **The companion seed row μ** (+9.5% of the sweep, d). It estimates the error of
  the map without pieces, not of the run's map, and no ledger line asks for that.
  The acceptance suite gets the same number with no flag: the difference between
  this sweep and the sweep of a replay of the recording's own steps.
- **The λ-weighted selection and its threshold.** Repairing every bracket costs
  +12.2% rows in the one gradient run (e). The most a selection could save is about
  8% of one sweep per grid (e). The weight needs s, and a λ belongs to one
  functional while the grid serves several.
- **A step-length floor on pieces.** Its scale is not free of θ, and it is not
  needed under R3 (e).
- **A per-run counter of brackets.** Derivable from the record (rows with a
  bracket). On a replay, rows with flips show the drift from θ₀.

## What this settles

- No restep in the controller. No state carried between steps for cutting (F1 needed
  "a step cut at its first root makes the next cut at its last"). No interplay
  between cuts and rejections or step-size suggestions.
- The forward's steps are set by the norm alone, as the grid controller commits.
- No role flag for invaders. They read the recording, and pieces exist only in the
  schedule.
- No grid that depends on λ. One schedule serves every functional of an analysis.
- No model code for locating. The bracket comes from the six readings odelia already
  takes per step for sign values. No `value_at` evaluation is added.
- A replay can never change its own map, so a pass over the grid can never jump in
  θ′ because a repair switched on.

## What this makes hard

- **The building run's gradient is not exactly its own forward's.** The pieces start
  from the recorded y_{k−1} and end at y′_k ≠ y_k. Their difference is the
  straddling step's local error, at most the tolerance.
  - (e): the tolerance's whole move from 1e-4 to 3e-4 shifts the elasticities
    0.018 rms (m). Only 234 of 4786 rows carry the mismatch, so about
    0.05 × 0.018 ≈ 0.001ε.
  - Cope: when a check needs exactness, take the gradient from a replay of the
    schedule (P), at +1 forward.
- **J kinds (#13, #14, #19).** A held step end does not supply the time term
  Δf · dt*/dθ. A declared J kind would be bracketed at a cost and not fixed.
  - Cope: declare it as a split block, the moving cut with the implicit function.
    That is model code. R9 decides whether it is needed.
- **Invaders' own node-reach K kinks** (#5 rooting depth, #12). Walks never take
  pieces.
  - Cope: the walk's own rows show the flips. If one is measured to matter, the
    invader's sweep can piece its own controller-free rows by relaxing the
    `error_index` gate for walks. That is one predicate.
- **Kinks in most steps of a record.** Each bracketed step adds about two steps to
  the sweep and to replays. At more than about 270 bracketed steps on a run of this
  size, the gradient run passes +14% rows (e).
  - Cope: A's selection, by |λ| over the declared value's reach (70% capture at the
    top 30, m), as the retrofit.
- **Two zeros between two readings** (an in-and-out excursion inside one stage gap,
  33 node-steps on dry, m) are not seen. They are left to the step, as today.
- **Reader load: a sweep that steps.** `transpose_pieces` builds a scratch
  `SolverInternal` and calls `step_by`. It is the walk path, reused, but a reader of
  `sweep_range` meets a forward inside it.
- **A run's `ode_times` (`get_times`, read off `schedule()`) now lists the pieces'
  times.** `schedule()` is documented as "the schedule a replay of this run would
  take", so this is on-spec, but R users reading step times see times the adaptive
  run did not stop at. `SCM::refine_schedule` reads `schedule()` too; check it.

## Kill condition

- **Invaders that feed back on the soil**, or analyses where most passes sweep the
  resident (the sweep inside regnans's loops at k ≥ 2, `design-workflows.md`).
  Hands off to **the floor F1**: equal cost, and exact.
- **R6 tightened to exactness for the building run.** Hands off to **P**.
- **Kinks bracketed in more than about 6% of steps.** Hands off to **A's** selection,
  as a retrofit inside B: in-sweep, on |λ|, with no second pass.

## The design

### Parts

1. **One member, in plant (and phylloptim).**
   `void kink_values(std::vector<double>& v) const` gives one value per kink at the
   state just evaluated. Non-finite means no reading.
   - For TF24: each node's f_hi, the marginal profit at the dry end, which the collar
     solve computes on every solve (inventory #10: "yes, not kept").
   - phylloptim keeps it on the leaf, as `arm_margin_dry_` did in libJTstop.
   - About 10 lines in phylloptim and 10 in plant (e).
   - Adding #5, #12, #16 or #20–23 later is one more entry each.
   - The concept, in `ode_step.hpp`:
     ```cpp
     // A System whose rates' derivative jumps where a value it computes changes
     // sign; it gives one value per kink at the state it last evaluated.
     template <typename System>
     concept MarksKinks = requires(const System& s, std::vector<double>& v) {
       { s.kink_values(v) } -> std::same_as<void>;
     };
     ```
2. **Readings, in `Step`.**
   - `take_step` reads `kink_values` after each stage's `derivs`, as it reads
     `sign_values`. `step()` and `read_end_sign_values` read them at the end.
     `SolverInternal` keeps the start's readings (`kink_values_in`) and hands them
     over in `save_dydt_out_as_in`, as with `sign_values_in`.
   - The readings are at fractions {0, 0.2, 0.3, 0.6, 0.875, 1}, the same
     `at[]` that `taken_step::sign_changes` uses.
   - Zero extra evaluations.
3. **The bracket, in `push_step`.** For each value, every flip between neighbouring
   readings gives a zero by linear interpolation. `kink_bracket` = {first, last}
   over all values. It is empty where nothing flips. A zero within 1e-6 of an end
   (the JT rule) leaves that side uncut.
   - Because φ(1−) = 0, a cut misplaced by the interpolation costs at most one
     node's share times 0.098 · h_piece, at the right cut (e).
4. **`pieces(rec, k)`.** The instructions a bracketed, controller-chosen row stands
   for: up to three steps [0, a], [a, b] and [b, 1] of the row's size. The last
   piece is placed at the row's own time.
   - If the row substepped the soil, each piece takes the row's substep ends that
     fall inside it, rescaled, plus its own stage and sample fractions, with the
     row's input slope. This is deterministic from the record, so it is held under
     θ′, and the knots stay substep ends (R8).
   - Otherwise it returns the row.
   - About 35 lines.
5. **`schedule()`** emits `pieces(rec, k)` in place of each row. About 5 lines
   changed.
6. **`sweep_range`.** At row k, if `pieces(rec, k)` has more than one entry, it
   calls `transpose_pieces`:
   - `be_at_step(system, rec, k − 1)` on the double System. The active copy is
     separate (`active_system` rebinds).
   - A scratch `SolverInternal` with states kept, seeded with
     `rec[k − 1].solved.at_state`. It runs `step_by` on each piece, which stores
     solved values, splits nodes and substeps the soil as any replay does.
   - Then `solver.step_adjoint` on its rows, top piece first. `std::swap(lambda,
     lambda_in)` follows each, as the loop does now.
   - On `DomainError` it transposes the row whole and counts it in
     `pieces_refused`.
   - `restore_on_exit` already returns the double System to the run's end.
   - About 40 lines.

### Data flow

- forward → readings → bracket on the row.
- The run's sweep → pieces at double → step_adjoint per piece.
- `schedule()` → replay program with pieces.
- Recording (with no pieces) → invader walks.

### How each requirement is met

- **R1.** The pieces bracket each cluster as F1's stop does (0.0020, m). The middle
  piece is the interpolated cluster span, about 0.22 of the step against F1's exact
  zeros. The mismatch adds about 0.001ε (e). Expect 0.002–0.004 rms (e), against
  0.006. **Registered:** the nudged pair on dry, sharp gate.
- **R2.** Replays hold the pieces and never divide again, so nothing switches with
  θ′.
  - A node's kink crossing a stage jumps the gradient by b_i · (θ/J/ε) · λ · h ·
    ΔG_n. Part 4's largest switch steps give (θ/J/ε)·λ·h·ΔG ≈ 0.15ε, and ΔG_n is
    about 1/51 of that, with b_i ≤ 0.40. That is ≤ 0.0012ε in an unpieced step and
    ≤ 3e-4ε inside a middle piece (e), all under 0.01ε.
  - The building run's gradient and a replay at θ₀ differ by the mismatch, about
    0.001ε (e).
  - The kink part's radius is how far θ′ can move before clusters leave their
    middle pieces. It is unmeasured; the replay's rows with flips report it.
- **R3.** +12.2% rows (+11.1% time) on the gradient run (e). +0 on forwards and
  walks.
- **R4.** One member. odelia detects, locates, records, replays and differentiates.
- **R5.** One account. Every division that every pass holds is a step end in the
  schedule: scheduled times (Events, insertions), controller ends, and pieces from
  kink brackets. Every division located again in each pass is a block's own cut
  inside a step: the node split at P's zeros (moving, implicit function), and the
  soil's substep ends at knots and stage fractions.
  - The tool follows the reach. A node-local kink is a split block. A kink whose
    reach is shared (soil, field) is a bracket, because the node split at f_hi
    measured 0.044 and the soil split 0.010, against the step end's 0.002 (m).
  - Events are untouched.
- **R6.** Replays are exact for their own maps. The building run's sweep is within
  about 0.001ε of central differences over the grid (e). **Registered:** central
  differences along TF24_cost_scale on the grid, against the building sweep.
- **R7.** Walks are unchanged. Their sweeps carry no soil adjoint.
- **R8.** The node split runs inside each piece. Its count is unchanged in the
  forward and about the same in pieces. The knots stay substep ends. Insertions sit
  at row boundaries, which pieces never cross.
- **O-Diagnosed.** Each run reports `pieces_refused` (failures) and its bracketed

  for the kink part. The error estimate stays the companion's, which becomes valid
  again once jitter (0.002–0.004, e) is below a third of the tolerance's own move
