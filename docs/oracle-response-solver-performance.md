# Oracle response: where the error in `J` comes from

Reply to `oracle-consultation-solver-performance.md` as it stood at `0fe2e07`, from a fresh
session with no prior context, captured verbatim. Earlier replies are in this file's git
history. What was tested against it is in `docs/handover.md`.

---

Short version. The tolerance is the wrong variable because it bounds the state's error in the state's units, while `J`'s error is the state's error weighted by `J`'s sensitivity to it, and for one component that weight runs over ten decades. The component is each member's pool near empty; the sensitive objects are `ln S_j` (a survival clock) and the moments at which the pool switches from emptying to filling. Cost is set by a different object entirely, the chain's stiffness inside pulses plus the stops, so `tol` moves cost and error almost independently, which is what T1–T2 show.

1. Mechanism

The amplifier. `m_j` is a cumulative hazard, and `μ(r) = 0.01 + 5.5·e^{−r/0.05}` is, as a function of `ln r`, a unit-width step between 0.01 and 5.5. While `P < 0` the pool empties linearly, `Ṡ = −c(t)·S`, so a relative error in `S` is carried unchanged through the stretch and becomes a shift of the moment the hazard switches on: `∂m/∂ln S_start = −μ₁·τ_pool·(1 − e^{−r_s/r₀})`, with `τ_pool = S_max/u⁻ + τ_s` (≈ 20δ ≈ 0.054 in T6's stretch), i.e. 0.1–0.3 per stretch. `n_j = e^{−m_j}` multiplies every later increment of `F_j`. So a relative pool error `ε` at a stretch's start becomes `~0.3ε` in the member's survival and in its share of `J`; T6's incoming `1.7e-3` gives a few `1e-4` in that member's `m`, which is the loss part. The same `ε` enters `g = P⁺G(r)` through `G'(r)·r/G ≈ 0.1–0.3` at small `r`: the output part is the same error through a second door. That is why T8 removes both parts at once.

The floor. The pool's error scale is `tol·|S| + tol`, and the pools that carry `J` sit near `1e-3·S_max` when it matters. At `tol = 1e-4`, `S = 0.006`, the absolute part allows 1.7% relative error per step — T6's 1–2.5% jumps. T7 is the direct evidence: the low-pool rows are flat from `tol = 1e-3` to `1e-4` (4.0e-4 → 2.2e-4) while the high-pool rows follow `tol` (9.0e-5 → 2.7e-5). The chain sets the step on 90% of steps; the pool's error rides underneath, bounded by a floor that `tol` lowers linearly while the weight on it is `1/S`.

Where the error is made. At the switch of the pool's rate at `P = 0` (`P⁺` with `ε = 1e-4` is a kink at any resolvable scale). Downward: the pool is at ~1% of `S_max` under quiescent steps of 5–10δ; the straddling error `h²·0.73·|Ṗ|·K(ϑ)` is ~`4e-4` absolute at `h = 5δ`, 0.5% of a 0.07 pool. T5's `0.5δ` row, which touches only these, takes `+8.1e-4` to `−9.5e-5`, so at `tol ≥ 1e-4` they carry most of the aggregate. Upward: the pool is at ~0.1% of `S_max` and starts filling at slope `0.73·|Ṗ| ≈ 7e3`; at `h = 0.3δ` the straddling error is ~`2e-5` absolute, 0.4–1.5% of a 0.006 pool; T6's trace caught these in one member. In both cases the pair sees a quarter and the floor would pass the whole. `K` has mean zero in `ϑ`, `ϑ` is redrawn at every tolerance, and `h ∝ tol^0.1` so `h² ∝ tol^0.2`: random sign, flat magnitude. That is T2. T3's quadrature estimate was applied to the wrong state: the kink error in the output is a one-off `O(h²)`, the kink error in the pool is amplified by the hazard and carried through every later stretch. T15 fails for the same reason a smoothing must: to be resolved, the smoothing width has to exceed the step's span of `P` (8–17 units), and at that width it moves `J` by far more than the target.

Standard names. (i) Goal-oriented error: `J_h − J ≈ Σ_n λ_{n+1}ᵀ·le_n`, `λ` the adjoint (Cao & Petzold 2004; Estep 1995; Becker–Rannacher's dual-weighted residual). (ii) atol misspecification: "atol is the level below which the component stops mattering" is violated by 4–8 decades for the pool. (iii) Order reduction at C⁰ points of the vector field (Hairer–Nørsett–Wanner II.6; Gear & Østerby 1984; Shampine & Thompson 2000): `O(h²)` local error with an unreliable embedded estimate, remedied by event location or by steps that shrink there. (iv) Hazard/survival integrals: carry the log of the decaying input, or control it relatively. In one phrase: a switched, scale-separated system with a hazard-weighted functional, integrated under a state-norm controller.

Load-bearing / incidental. Load-bearing: `μ`'s shape (`μ₁/μ₀ = 550`, `r₀ = 0.05`); the pool's range with linear emptying; the `P⁺` switch; the timing (the earliest members' pools are at `1e-3·S_max` when the pulse arrives, and the downward crossings sit under long steps); the shared absolute part; `G(r)` for the output part. Incidental for the error: the chain's stiffness and exponent (T9; T10 at tight tol), the knots and stops (necessary per T16, but not the mechanism), Φ's interpolant, the creation weights (C¹, `< 1e-4`), members' persistence, the sweep's structure. Secondary but unquantified: the other C⁰ events in the ~0.5δ window around each upward cluster — φ leaving its cap as `v` rises (a kink common to all members, and likely what starts `P` rising; `P ≈ −15 throughout` a stretch is what a capped φ looks like), the class switches 0.26δ before, and the untallied clamp. Once the floor is gone they are the residual random walk (T8's `1e-5`) and the prime suspects for what T12 leaves.

2. One setting, cost, guarantee

The setting. Control the pool relatively, with an absolute part tied to the member rather than the run: `σ_S = tol·(|S| + c·r₀·S_max(x_j))`, `c ≈ 1e-3…1e-4`. The floor is where `|∂μ/∂ln r|·τ_pool = μ₁(r/r₀)e^{−r/r₀}·τ_pool` falls below the target, about `r = 1e-4·r₀`; `S_max(x_j)` is a free reading. The equivalent change of variable is `ln S` while `P < 0` (no positivity guard, quiescent steps no longer limited by `hλ_S`) and `S` while `P > 0`, which needs the switch at a step end. T8 is a crude global version (floor `0.01·tol`) and already gives `+1.2e-5` at `1e-4` for +19%, or `+7.8e-5` for +4% with the absolute part alone; its error falls 30× from `1e-3` to `1e-4`, i.e. it follows `tol` until the kink random walk floors it.

The switch. With the scale tightened the controller finds the straddling steps by rejection (1202 of T8's 1561 added steps). Cheaper: let the estimate see the kink. On any step across which a member's `P` changes sign (a free reading), replace that member's pool estimate by the analytic straddling error `h²·(0.73 + r)·|Ṗ|·K(ϑ)`, with `ϑ` from linear interpolation of `P` and `Ṗ` from the stage values; you already have `K` and `K/K̂`. Apply a ×3–4 factor on steps with a class-switch or φ-cap reading. Restart after a crossing at `≤ 0.1δ` rather than the carried proposal (T12's `0.05δ` row is a 4× effect). Expect about T8's cost with a smaller, tol-proportional error; the test bed can settle it.

Prediction. Nothing about the state can make the constant in "error ∝ tol" known across `θ`; the constant is the adjoint, which you already compute. Store the embedded difference per step (it is computed anyway), and in the sweep accumulate `E_J = Σ_n λ_{n+1}ᵀ·le_n` with the kink-corrected `le_n` — one dot product per row. Guarantees: `E_J` is a first-order-accurate estimate of `J`'s time error, `J − E_J` is second-order accurate (adjoint error correction, Pierce & Giles 2000), the estimate is per-`θ` by construction, and the forward cost is zero. It also maps `|λ·le|` over the run, which says where refinement pays; the previous gradient's `λ` can weight the next run's controller (H1 allows the grid to change between gradients), which is the principled form of T8 and stops spending steps on members whose survival is nil or whose output cannot arrive before `T`. What it cannot give: an a priori bound, and it inherits the estimator's blindness on any kink you neither correct nor locate.

3. Why T5, T8, T12, T13 differ

They differ in how much of the window around each crossing they resolve, whether they touch the floor, and what they waste. The numbers themselves are single draws of a random walk over ~200 clusters, so differences of `1e-4` between variants are within noise; compare treatments by the pool-error trace (T6/T7) or by `E_J`.

* T5 (`P_c = 0`, `0.05δ`) resolves everything by halving every crossing step to `0.05δ` and letting the 5× regrowth cover the next 0.3δ; downward clusters get 126 steps each and every halving discards a full attempt — 3.4× the cost, tol-following error. `0.5δ` touches only the quiescent (downward) crossings and gets `−1e-4`. `P_c = 3` shifts the small steps 0.1δ after / 1.8δ before the kinks; `P_c = 10` shifts them 0.4δ after the upward kink, past the filling onset and the rest of the window, and misses.
* T8 resolves whatever moves the pool's estimate, wherever it is, and adds steps only there: cheapest.
* T12 puts one event type at step ends and restarts at the carried proposal (19δ in quiescence, ~0.3δ in pulses), so the filling onset and the other events in the window run at the pair's ordinary sizes under the unchanged floor; each located crossing costs ~3 attempts (a discarded accepted step, a throw at the proposal, the retake), 45 per cluster. It fixes the smallest piece at the largest price. Its `3e-5` row is no better than T2's, and its combination with T8 being worse than T8 alone is the one measurement this account does not explain; the suspect is the retake/restart rule, and T6's trace on that run would show whether the error is still in the pools.
* T13 fixes the kink for the crossing member only, on a background read from the straddling step's Hermite cubic — in a pulse the chain is at `hλ ≈ 1–3` and the pair has no continuous extension, so the member is re-integrated against a wrong φ — with no error control on its two steps.

4. Least cost

Measured and within `1e-4`: T8 absolute-only at `1e-4`, `4.81e6` (`+7.8e-5`, marginal); T8 both parts at `1e-4`, `5.53e6` (`+1.2e-5`). The pair at `1e-5` and T10 at `1e-5` pay `6.4e6` and `5.8e6` to buy the same thing by accident. With the per-member scale and the kink-aware estimate, `tol = 3e-4` should suffice (T8 gives `+1.2e-4` there without them): about `4.5–4.8e6`. That is near the limit for this pair, because the pool fix does not touch what sets the cost.

The cost object is the chain in pulses: 64% of member evaluations are in the 1δ legs at 2.66 steps each, and 18% are on rejected attempts. A clipped step enters the pulse at the carried proposal, the chain at `v ≈ 0.85` has `hλ > β`, the attempt is rejected, the retry is 0.2–0.4δ, and the 5× cap and the next stop take the rest; none of it is member accuracy. Sub-cycle the chain inside each member-stage interval (its rates given `a` cost `1.4e3` instructions, three orders below a member loop) with `a(t)` interpolated from the member stages: T11 says `a` is 1% of the chain's flux in pulses and a quadratic in time fits it to `2e-5`, so the coupling error in the chain's rate is ~`2e-7`. Stay single-rate in quiescence, where `hλ_chain < β` and `a` dominates. T17's failures came from re-running the member loop inside the fast step and from holding or linearising `a` across long quiescent steps, neither of which this needs. This is the MRI-GARK / MIS structure (Sandu 2019; Wensch–Knoth–Galant 2009); the sweep records the chain's substeps as chain-only rows (H3). Member steps then drop to ~1 per leg away from pulse starts and ~3 in the first leg of a pulse (φ's boundary layer, 0.2–0.5δ): ~6000 steps, roughly `2–2.5e6` evaluations at the same accuracy. Below that, the floor with these stops is one step per leg, ~`1e6`, which the pulse-start transients keep out of reach. These are projections for the test bed, not measurements.

Two free levers: skip members whose survival has reached nil (they contribute exactly zero to `a`, `Φ`, `J` and `λ`), and warm-start the inner problem from the previous evaluation under the same bracket and stopping rule — that cuts µs, not counts, and needs a branch-consistency check.

If you return to T10: its loose-tol failure is the embedded estimate's blindness to the transient in the step where `s` stops (both solutions L-damped, true error 15× the estimate). Pulse ends are knots you already stop at; restart with `h ≤ δ` there. It saved nothing at tight tol because the steps were no longer stability-bound.

On the gradient. The straddled-kink term is `O(h²)` in `J` but `O(h)` in `dJ/dθ` (its `θ`-derivative goes through `K'(ϑ)·dt_c/dθ`), and with `h ∝ tol^0.1` the gradient's kink noise is essentially flat in `tol`. H1–H3 do not change that. Measure `dJ/dθ`'s convergence separately; the same treatment, kinks at step ends or shrunk steps at the events, weighted by `λ`, is what fixes it.
