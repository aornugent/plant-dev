## 1. Where `J`'s error is made, revised; the gradients

**The kink factor.** With `|(1−G)(1−r) − r|` the pool's own kink is 0.30·|Ṗ| at the downward median `r = 0.35`, 0.14·|Ṗ| at the upward median 0.16, and 0.7·|Ṗ| only near empty. So the pool's slope jump is smaller than I said at typical crossings, and it vanishes near `r = 0.2`. Yet the over-large first factor, which refines every crossing step regardless of `r`, gives the steadiest `J` (std 6.8e-6 against 2.9e-5). The reading is that the crossing step's damage is not only in the pool: `P⁺` also sits in `ẋ`, `Ẏ` and `Ḟ` through `g = P⁺G(r)`, whose slope jump `|Ṗ|·G(r)` never vanishes, and those components' straddling errors were left at their own scales. The four exact jumps are `S: |(1−G)(1−r) − r|·|Ṗ|`, `x: G(1−f)κ·|Ṗ|`, `Y: Gf/ν·|Ṗ|`, `F: e^{−m}(σ/σ_b)Gf/ν·|Ṗ|`. Prediction: the kink-aware estimate applied to all four, each against its own scale, is at least as steady as the first factor at lower cost. Cheapest test: the seven-run nudge set at `3e-4`, compare the standard deviation with 6.8e-6.

**The pools' levels.** The amplifier needs `r_s ≳ r₀` at the stretch's start, so it acts on relative errors present when emptying begins: the downward crossings (`r ≈ 0.35`, `u = 7`, full strength 0.3) under long quiescent steps, and the preceding fill. A near-empty upward onset (11% of upward crossings, after stretches over `100δ`) feeds little amplification on its own stretch (`u = 0.02–0.3` gives 0.006–0.08), and its relative error is diluted by the next fill; it survives only when the refill is small, which is T6's case (a 1–2.5% error at `r = 0.014`, a ninefold fill, `1.7e-3` at `r = 0.128`). So the dominant path at `tol ≥ 1e-4` is the downward crossings and the emptying's own regular error, with the near-empty onsets mattering after long stretches followed by short pulses. I had over-weighted the onsets.

**The class over the stretches.** The switch into the lower end sits `6.6δ` after the downward crossing, under 5–19δ steps: a kink in `P` (the emptying rate, through `r·ΔṖ`) and in `c`, hence in `a`, which dominates the chain in quiescence, hence in φ (×6.57) for every member. That is a common-mode kink in every stretch with crossings, treated by none of the variants except T5's cascade tail and whatever moves the pool's estimate. With φ never capping, this and the untallied clamp are the remaining candidates for the residual draw. Cheapest test: on the `1e-6` run, tally the clamp's binding changes per step, and difference `a`'s stage values across the class-switch steps to see the jump in `ȧ`; if it is there, the same `h²·jump·K` estimate applied to the chain on those steps should shrink the nudge spread further.

**T12 + T8.** The rules as stated give me no mechanism for a loss part rising by `1.7e-4` and an output part by `1.5e-4`; both rising together points at `P⁺`-bearing terms being too large after retakes. Two cheap tests: T12 with the restart at `min(0.1δ, the retaken step's own proposal)`, and T6's per-stretch pool-error trace on the T12 + T8 run. If the pools are clean there and `J` is still off, the error is in the retake path itself, not in the dynamics.

**The gradients (§2).** They are 10–30× `J`'s error, non-monotone, and about as large at `1e-5` as at `1e-3`. That is the `O(h)` term: on any grid, straddled or not, `d/dθ [h_e²·Δ_e·K(ϑ_e)] = h_e·Δ_e·K'(ϑ_e)·dt_e/dθ`, with `|K'| ≈ 0.1–0.15` over most of `ϑ` for this pair. One downward event on a coarse step, `h_e = 5δ`, `Δ_e = 185`, `dt_e/dln θ_A ≈ |∂P/∂ln θ_A|/|Ṗ| ≈ 15/616 ≈ 9δ`, gives ~`6e-3` in `S` per unit `ln θ`, ~`3%` relative, ~`1e-2` in `m`, and after the member's share and `√88` events of random sign, a few `1e-4` to `1e-3` in the relative gradient, which is §2's range. It falls in proportion to `h_e` at the events, not to `tol`, which is why the refinements (crossing steps at ~1–3δ) brought it to `1e-4–4e-4` and why no ladder shows an order. `θ_B` has no explicit path to `P`, so its `dt_e/dθ` runs through `n → a → chain → φ`; its larger errors say that path is not small. Cheapest measurement: two pinned runs per constant at `θ_k(1 ± 1e-2)`, reading only the crossing times; their shifts give `dt_e/dln θ_k` directly, and the sum `Σ_e h_e·Δ_e·|K'|·|dt_e/dln θ_k|`, weighted as above, should reproduce §2's magnitudes constant by constant. Branch flips at stages (class, clamp) add corners of the same order; their density in `θ` is measured by the sweep's gradient at `θ(1 ± 1e-3)` against the pinned difference across the same interval.

## 2. Transfer radius

**What governs it.** Four things, in order. (i) Event motion against the refined zone: an event moves by `dt_e/dln θ·Δ` and the pinned grid's error at that event is `h(t_e)²·Δ_e·K`; the radius is `(half-width of the small-step zone around the event) / |dt_e/dln θ|`, minimised over the events that matter. Downward crossings set it: `9δ` per unit `ln θ_A` against `0.6δ` for upward ones. A zone of `±0.5δ` gives 5–6%, which is what probes need today; `±3δ` gives about 30%. (ii) Positivity: a step whose stages straddle a near-empty pool's crossing goes negative through the pair's negative coefficients; on the adaptive run the throw retries, on the pinned run it stops. The guard at `−1e-8·S_max` is a debug assertion, not a model constraint: a pool at `−1e-9` has restoring rates on both branches, and `μ`, `G` and `λ_S` are smooth there. (iii) The chain's stability margin: steps at `hλ ≈ β` at `θ₀` go unstable when `v` shifts with `a`; §4's 39 and 83 steps are mostly these, and T9's `h ≤ 0.8β/|λ|` is free. (iv) The creation moments' absolute part, below.

**Predictions for (c).** The pair's grid at `1e-3`: a draw of ~`1e-3` at every `θ`, which is why it "transferred" to `1.1e-3` across ±30%. The per-member grid at `1e-4`, guard relaxed: `J` within ~`1e-4` while the events stay inside the steps that were shortened by rejection (roughly ±20–30% in `θ_A`, the width of those steps not being controlled), then a plateau at the coarse-kink level `5e-4–1e-3`, not growing further with `Δ`. The gradient transfers with its `O(h_e)` error at the events' new positions. Cheapest measurement: pin the per-member grid at `θ_A × {0.8, 0.9, 0.95, 1.05, 1.1, 1.2}` with the guard at `−1e-4·S_max`, against adaptive runs at each point; then the same with explicit zones.

**Which end.** The frozen grid, rebuilt only when the check trips or the iterate leaves the region. Rebuilt per `θ`, `J_h` jumps by ~`1e-5` (fine) but the gradient jumps by its `1e-4–1e-3` draw, which corrupts any quasi-Newton update built from gradient differences. Frozen, `L_h(θ)` is one smooth function (up to branch corners), the sweep's gradient is exact for it, no attempt is rejected (the 18–23% waste goes), and the optimiser converges to the optimum of `L_h`, whose distance from `L`'s is `|H⁻¹·(∇L_h − ∇L)|`, a bias to estimate once, not a draw to average. Probes force this end anyway. This is the frozen-mesh strategy of PDE-constrained optimisation, with a trust region.

**Least change, with cost.** Build the base grid with explicit event zones: from the first sign change in any member's `P` (a free reading), `h ≤ h_e` until `margin` past the cluster's last, with `margin = Δ·max_k |dt_e/dln θ_k|` from the two-pinned-run measurement, and a smaller zone at `h ≤ 0.1δ` over the upward clusters. Keep the per-member scale and the four-component kink estimate outside the zones, the chain margin everywhere, the schedule fixed per series, and relax the guard on pinned runs. For `Δ = 0.3` in `θ_A`, `h_e = 0.5δ`: 98 downward clusters × ~23 steps and 98 upward × ~4, about `+7.5e5` member evaluations, +15%; the pinned runs then cost accepted steps only, ~`5e6`. Guarantee: within `Δ` every event stays in its zone, so the pinned run's local errors are bounded by the rule's own scales, `J`'s error is the rule's at `θ₀` up to the adjoint weights' smooth variation, and the gradient's event term is bounded by `h_e·Σ_e Δ_e|K'||dt_e/dθ|`, ~`5e-5` relative at `h_e = 0.5δ` on my estimate. Beyond the least change, an exponential (integrating-factor) step for the pool's linear emptying would remove throws and the `hλ_S` limit at once, at the price of one component's rows in the sweep.

## 3. The check on a pinned run

The quantity exists and is being discarded: the embedded difference. Keep it, form `ρ_n` with the grid's rule's scales, replacing the pool's, `x`'s, `Y`'s and `F`'s estimates by the analytic jump on any step where a member's `P` changes sign, and report per run: `max ρ`, the count of steps with `ρ > 1.1`, the count of sign changes on steps longer than `h_e` (an event has left its zone), the pool's deepest excursion below zero, and `max hλ_chain/β`. All are free readings. Guarantee: if all are within limits, the pinned run satisfies the same local-error bounds as the adaptive rule at `θ₀`, so its `J` error is bounded by the rule's `κ(θ)·tol` with `κ(θ) = Σ_j s_j Σ_k μ₁τ_jk(1 − e^{−u_jk})` computable from readings (`r` at each downward crossing, `|P|` over the stretch, `S_max`); if any trips, rebuild. The same holds for a probe, whose own members' `P`, `r` and estimates are on its pass. The sharper check, when the sweep runs, is `E_J = Σ_n λ_{n+1}ᵀ·le_n`, which reports the time error itself, per `θ`, and for a probe `E_J′` from the probe's sweep.

It does not bound an optimum's or a root's location; that needs the gradient's bias. On a frozen grid the bias is smooth in `θ` and `O(h_e)`, so two frozen grids at `h_e` and `h_e/2` (two forward runs and two sweeps, once near convergence) give it by Richardson and extrapolate it away; `|H⁻¹·δg|`, with `H` from the quasi-Newton or from differences of the sweep's gradient on the frozen grid, is the location error, and `δg′/(dg′/dθ′)` the root's. The analytic sum is the cheap proxy for `θ_A`-like constants, where the explicit `∂P/∂θ` dominates `dt_e/dθ`; for `θ_B`-like constants it cannot be formed without state sensitivities, so measure.

## 4. Are (a)–(c) the right objects, and the rules

(a) is right for `J` and wrong as stated for the gradient. The time axis has two knobs: `tol`, which the smooth part of the error follows, and the step at the C⁰ events, `h_e`, which the kink part follows, `h_e²` in `J` and `h_e` in `dJ/dθ`. The gradient never follows `tol` on a controller-built grid, at any tolerance. (b) is the kink random walk again: it is not a property to test but to make small by construction through `h_e`. (c) is the right object, and with the frozen-grid strategy it is the only convergence question that matters between rebuilds. The reframing: the object for calibration and probes is a frozen discretisation per series, asked to be smooth in `θ` on a trust region and to carry a certificate of its distance from the continuum; convergence of the rebuilding rule is demonstrated once by a ladder per series, not required of every run.

Rules, each with its guarantee and check:
- *Steps.* Per-member pool scale `tol·(|S| + c·r₀·S_max)`, four-component kink estimate with the `h²` exponents, chain margin `0.8β/|λ|`, restart `≤ 0.1δ` after crossings. Guarantee: `|J − J*| ≤ κ(θ)·tol` with the measured 0.2–0.5 constant. Check: `ρ`, `E_J`.
- *Stops.* Knots, creations, and event zones sized from `Δ` and the measured `dt_e/dln θ_k`. Guarantee: the transfer radius `Δ`. Check: sign changes on steps longer than `h_e`.
- *Creation times.* One schedule per series, fixed through the calibration. §4's own evidence favours this: rebuilding by the density recipe at each point made the error worse, because `q″` from a noisy run is a draw. Guarantee: `O(Δb²)` only if the ladder shows it; `F(T; b)` may be rough in `b` at the pulse scale, in which case the schedule is part of the model's definition and its bias is estimated once by Richardson on the ladder and its `θ`-derivative by the ladder at two `θ`. Cheapest measurement: the 108/215/429 ladder with the per-member scale at `1e-4`, now that the time draw is `1e-5` rather than `8e-4`.
- *Error scales for the moments.* The coupling you saw is the pool's problem again: the absolute part `tol` is the panel mass's increment per step, and at 857 members it is 2% of `I_j`. Use `tol·(|I_j| + ρ̄_c·Δ_j)`, with `Δ_M = t − b_M` for the open panel. Guarantee: relative resolution of every panel. Check: the weights' part of `E_J`.

What breaks them, readable from a series before running: crossings with small `|Ṗ|` (a `P` that meanders near zero) widen the zones and shrink the radius in proportion; many stretches with `P < 0` multiply the zones' cost; long stretches followed by short pulses leave near-empty onsets undiluted and need the `0.1δ` restart and the floor `c`; survival concentrated in a few members raises `κ`; and a `q_j` rough at the schedule's spacing breaks the creation ladder. A dense uniform run's `q_j` against `b_j`, the distribution of `|Ṗ|` at crossings, the cluster count and the stretch-length distribution are the four readings to take on each series of the bank.
